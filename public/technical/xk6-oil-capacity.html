Notes & Caveats

The XK6 is famously durable, but its oil requirements are more nuanced than a modern engine. For pre-1972 engines with flat-tappet camshafts, zinc (ZDDP) is absolutely critical — modern low-zinc oils will cause rapid camshaft and lifter wear, often within a few thousand miles [EM-INTERVIEW]. Independent specialists we've spoken to universally recommend a high-zinc, 20W-50 classic oil for all XK6 engines, regardless of year. This isn't factory guidance for later units — the factory updated its recommendation when oil chemistry changed — but it's a practical adjustment based on what the specialists see across their workshops. For owners whose cars aren't used regularly, they also recommend shortening the interval to 3,000 miles or once a year, whichever comes first, to prevent moisture-related acidity build-up. This is covered in more detail on our XK Nikasil bore wear failure page.

Common Mistakes

  • Using modern low-zinc oil in a pre-1972 XK6. This is the single most destructive mistake owners make — flat-tappet camshafts require a minimum of 1,200ppm zinc content, which modern oils do not contain.
  • Trusting the later service interval for an early engine. 6,000 miles is acceptable for 1972+ units, but pre-1972 engines with flat-tappet camshafts should be changed at 3,000 miles or annually, regardless of the manual's suggestion for later cars.
  • Confusing XK6 with the later AJ6/AJ16. While related, the AJ6/AJ16 engine family has a different capacity and specification — check our AJ6/AJ16 engine page if you're not certain which family you have.
  • Assuming all classic 20W-50 oils are equal. Many modern 20W-50 oils are formulated for motorcycles or classic cars with roller camshafts and may not contain sufficient ZDDP — always check the specification label.

How to Verify on the Actual Vehicle

The dipstick on an XK6 is typically located on the left-hand side of the engine (as viewed from the cockpit), near the distributor on early units. The oil filler cap is on the cam cover. Confirm your exact engine displacement and year of manufacture via the engine number stamp, described in our Engine Number Location guide, before assuming these figures apply — 2.8L, 3.4L, 3.6L, 4.0L, and 4.2L variants all have slightly different oil requirements, particularly regarding viscosity and zinc content.

Why This Specification Matters

Getting the oil wrong on an XK6 isn't a minor issue. The wrong viscosity can lead to low oil pressure, increased wear, and eventual bearing failure — particularly on 4.2L and 4.0L high-compression variants. The wrong zinc content (or lack thereof) is catastrophic: without sufficient ZDDP, the flat-tappet camshaft and lifters experience metal-to-metal contact that rapidly destroys the cam lobes and sends metal debris through the entire oil system, eventually requiring a full engine rebuild. This is also why we treat "any 20W-50 will do" as a genuine misconception worth correcting: the specification includes a minimum zinc content that many off-the-shelf 20W-50 oils do not meet. Our XK6 engine page and XK Nikasil failure page both explain this in more detail.
