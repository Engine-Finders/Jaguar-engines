A Jaguar engine code is stamped on the block and encoded in the VIN. The most common codes are AJ126 (3.0L V6 supercharged), AJ133 (5.0L V8 supercharged), 204DTD (2.0L Ingenium diesel), and 204PT (2.0L Ingenium petrol).

  • Code format: usually 3–6 alphanumeric characters, often with a suffix indicating power or market variant [THIRD-PARTY — Jaguar workshop manual]
  • VIN position 8: the engine code is embedded in the VIN – position 8 is the engine code letter for most modern Jaguars [THIRD-PARTY — OEM VIN decoding standard]
  • Block stamp: located on the lower front or rear of the block, visible from under the bonnet on most models

How to Read a Jaguar Engine Code

Jaguar engine codes follow a logical pattern, but the exact format varies by era and source (engine block stamp vs VIN).

VIN Position 8 – Modern Jaguars (2000+)

In the 17‑digit VIN, position 8 is the engine code letter. For example, in SAJAA2024C... the engine code is A, which maps to the 204DTD. The VIN decoder is the most reliable way to confirm the factory engine for your chassis.

For most modern Jaguars, the VIN code letters are:

  • A – 204DTD (2.0L Ingenium diesel)
  • D – 204PT (2.0L Ingenium petrol)
  • F – AJ126 (3.0L V6 supercharged)
  • G – AJ133 (5.0L V8 supercharged)
  • H – 306DT (3.0L V6 diesel)
  • J – AJ20D6 (3.0L I6 diesel)

Engine Block Stamp – Physical Location

The engine code is stamped directly into the cylinder block, usually on a machined pad near the front of the block (behind the water pump on V6/V8) or on the rear face adjacent to the bellhousing. On Ingenium 4‑cylinder engines, the code is on a machined pad on the left-hand side of the block below the exhaust manifold.

The stamp is typically 6–8 characters and may include a suffix. For example, AJ133‑S indicates the supercharged high‑output version. A suffix of ‑A or ‑B often denotes a running change (e.g., revised chain tensioners).

Decoding the Family Code

Jaguar engine families are easily recognised by the prefix:

  • AJ – AJ‑V8 / AJ‑V6 / Ingenium (AJ200, AJ300 series)
  • DT – Diesel (usually paired with a number, e.g., 204DTD)
  • PT – Petrol (Ingenium petrol)
  • XK – Legendary straight‑six
  • V12 – Classic V12

How to Confirm Your Engine Code on the Vehicle

The most reliable method is the VIN, as documented above. If you need to visually confirm the engine code from the block, here is the location per major family:

  • Ingenium 2.0 (204DTD/204PT): Machined pad on the left‑hand side of the block, below the exhaust manifold, visible from above on most models.
  • AJ126 / AJ133 (V6/V8): On the front face of the block, behind the water pump pulley. A mirror and torch are often required to read it clearly.
  • 306DT / 276DT (V6 diesel): Stamped on the rear face of the block, adjacent to the bellhousing, visible from underneath or with a mirror.
  • AJ‑V8 4.0/4.2: On a machined pad on the front of the block near the timing cover.
  • XK6 / V12 (classic): Stamped on a pad on the inlet manifold side of the block, often near the distributor.

For detailed location photos, refer to our Engine Number Location Guide. If the stamp is corroded or unreadable, the VIN is your fallback.

Common Mistakes When Decoding Jaguar Engines

  • Confusing 204DTD (diesel) with 204PT (petrol). They are completely different engines – the code suffix matters. A quick check of the fuel filler cap will confirm.
  • Assuming all AJ126 engines are the same. The AJ126 has multiple power outputs (340PS, 380PS, 400PS) and the block stamp often includes a suffix to indicate the tune.
  • Misreading the VIN. Position 8 is the engine code, but some online decoders use position 7. Always verify against Jaguar's own VIN lookup.
  • Using aftermarket parts catalogues as a definitive reference. They often omit running changes and late‑production variants. Cross‑check with the engine code decoder and a Jaguar specialist.
