Notes & Real‑World Caveats

The F‑Type’s service schedule is straightforward, but a few critical points often get overlooked:

  • Supercharger oil is not lifetime. Many owners ignore the 36,000‑mile change, but the supercharger on both the AJ126 and AJ133 relies on clean oil for cooling and lubrication. Neglect can lead to premature wear and a costly rebuild [EM-INTERVIEW].
  • Oil grade matters. Jaguar specifies 0W‑20 for most F‑Type models (post‑2016) and 5W‑30 for earlier cars. Using the wrong viscosity can affect fuel economy and, in extreme cases, timing chain tensioner operation.
  • Spark plugs are due at 36,000 miles. Iridium plugs last longer than standard, but the supercharged engines run hot; deferred changes can cause misfires and increased fuel consumption.
  • Brake fluid is often overlooked. The F‑Type’s high‑performance braking system needs fresh DOT 4 LV fluid every 2 years to maintain pedal feel and prevent moisture‑related corrosion.

Common Mistakes F‑Type Owners Make

  • Following the service indicator blindly. The F‑Type’s variable service display can extend intervals based on driving style, but many specialists recommend sticking to fixed annual intervals, especially for supercharged engines [EM-INTERVIEW].
  • Using non‑JLR specification oil. Always use oil meeting JLR.03.5006 or equivalent - cheaper oils may lack the necessary anti‑wear additives for the supercharged valvetrain.
  • Forgetting to replace the supercharger belt. The supercharger drive belt is not part of the standard service schedule; inspect it at 60,000 miles and replace if cracked or glazed.
  • Confusing V6 and V8 service items. The 5.0L V8 takes more oil and a different supercharger oil volume - check your engine code before ordering parts Engine Code Guide.
  • Ignoring the cabin filter. It’s cheap and easy to replace, yet often skipped; a clogged filter reduces air‑con performance and can cause odours.
