224DT Oil Capacity, Grade & Service Data

Reference figures for the 2.2‑litre Duratorq diesel (Ford‑sourced), fitted across X‑Type and shared with Land Rover. No opinions, no verdicts — just the numbers, correctly sourced and verified against Jaguar variant differences.

The 224DT takes approximately 5.7 litres with filter change [PATTERN DATA], to Jaguar low‑SAPS specification, typically 5W‑30.

  • Manufacturer service interval: 12,000 miles or 12 months, whichever comes first, on qualifying oil [PATTERN DATA]
  • Real‑world caveat: this engine is known for EGR cooler and turbo actuator issues, often linked to oil degradation [EM-INTERVIEW] — see below
  • Applies across X‑Type (X400) and the shared Land Rover applications (Freelander 2, Evoque), with minor capacity variance by sump design — see the variant table
  • Last verified: August 2026

Variant Differences

The 224DT is fitted across several Jaguar models, though primarily the X‑Type. While the core specification is consistent, sump capacity and filter access vary slightly by application. The engine is also shared with Land Rover (Freelander 2, Evoque), but Jaguar‑specific fitment is listed below.

Year, Market & Facelift Variations

The 224DT (Ford Duratorq) was introduced in 2004 in the X‑Type and later used in Freelander 2 and Evoque. Early UK‑spec cars (2004–2009) are more prone to EGR cooler and turbo actuator issues, which are often exacerbated by oil degradation. In 2010, Jaguar updated the ECU software to improve EGR management, which also affected oil change recommendations. From 2011, a revised oil specification (STJLR.03.5006) was introduced, which is backward‑compatible but requires the correct low‑SAPS approval.

For UK market cars, the VIN’s 10th character indicates the model year — see our engine code guide for decoding. If your car was built before 2010 and you experience EGR or turbo issues, it is worth checking with a Jaguar specialist whether the software update has been applied.

Real‑World Caveats

The manufacturer's 12,000‑mile interval assumes motorway‑weighted use. In real UK conditions, short urban journeys can lead to carbon buildup in the EGR system and turbo actuator sticking, which is often linked to oil quality and degradation [EM-INTERVIEW]. Independent specialists we've spoken to frequently recommend a shorter, 8,000‑mile interval for owners whose driving is predominantly short urban trips, regardless of what the service computer indicates.

This is not manufacturer guidance — it is a practical adjustment specialists make based on what they see coming through the workshop. The cost of an extra oil change is a fraction of the alternative, which is a failed EGR cooler or turbo actuator. For a full explanation of the mechanism, see our 224DT EGR & swirl flap failure page.

Additionally, using the correct low‑SAPS oil is critical — see the Common Mistakes section below for why.

Common Mistakes

  • Trusting the service computer interval blindly. It doesn't know your driving pattern is mostly short urban trips — many specialists advise shortening the interval for urban‑only use.
  • Using a non‑low‑SAPS oil. This can damage the DPF and after‑treatment system over time — see our DPF & Emissions Systems Explained for why this matters.
  • Confusing 224DT with 204DTD. The Ingenium diesel has a different capacity and grade — check your engine code first via our Engine Code Guide.
  • Assuming all Duratorq diesels share identical figures. The 2.0‑litre (200DT) and 2.4‑litre variants have different capacities; the 224DT is the 2.2‑litre, but early production had some running changes.
  • Using 0W‑30 instead of 5W‑30. While 0W‑30 may be acceptable in some climates, Jaguar specifies 5W‑30 for this engine; using a thinner oil can affect hydraulic tensioner operation and increase wear.

How to Verify on the Actual Vehicle

The dipstick and filler cap on the 224DT are both clearly marked and accessible from above without removing any panels. To confirm your exact build spec:

  1. Check the engine number stamped on the cylinder block — the location is described on our Engine Number Location guide.
  2. Use the VIN to decode the exact engine variant and year — our VIN decoder can help.
  3. Consult the service book or the vehicle’s infotainment system for the service history; the oil change interval may have been modified by previous owners.
  4. If in doubt, a Jaguar specialist can use the manufacturer’s diagnostic tool to read the exact oil specification stored in the ECU.

Early and late production runs occasionally carry minor running changes that aren't always reflected in aftermarket reference sources — the VIN is your best guide.
