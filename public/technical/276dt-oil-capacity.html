276DT Oil Capacity, Grade & Service Data

Reference figures for the 2.7‑litre V6 diesel (AJD‑V6), fitted across XF, XJ and shared with Land Rover. No opinions, no verdicts - just the numbers, correctly sourced and verified against Jaguar variant differences.

The 276DT takes approximately 6.5 litres with filter change [PATTERN DATA], to Jaguar low‑SAPS specification, typically 5W‑30.

  • Manufacturer service interval: 16,000 miles or 12 months, whichever comes first, on qualifying oil [PATTERN DATA]
  • Real‑world caveat: the 276DT is known for oil pump housing failure, which can be linked to oil degradation [EM-INTERVIEW] - see below
  • Applies across XF (X250), XJ (X350/X351) and the shared Land Rover applications, with minor capacity variance by sump design - see the variant table
  • Last verified: August 2026

Variant Differences

The 276DT is fitted across several Jaguar models. While the core specification is consistent, sump capacity and filter access vary slightly by application. The engine is also shared with Land Rover (e.g., Discovery 3, Range Rover Sport), but Jaguar‑specific fitment is listed below.

For the later 306DT (3.0‑litre V6), the capacity and grade are similar but not identical; always confirm via your VIN - see our engine code decoder for help. The 276DT is also used in Land Rover vehicles; those applications may have different oil specifications due to different sump designs and service schedules – check your specific vehicle handbook.

Year, Market & Facelift Variations

The 276DT (AJD‑V6) was introduced in 2004 in the S‑Type and later spread to the XF (X250) and XJ (X350/X351). Early UK‑spec cars (2004–2009) are more prone to oil pump housing failure, which is often exacerbated by oil degradation. In 2009, Jaguar revised the oil pump design and updated the ECU software to improve oil pressure management. From 2010, a revised oil specification (STJLR.03.5006) was introduced, which is backward‑compatible but requires the correct low‑SAPS approval.

For UK market cars, the VIN’s 10th character indicates the model year - see our engine code guide for decoding. If your car was built before 2009 and you experience low oil pressure warnings, it is worth checking with a Jaguar specialist whether the updated oil pump has been fitted.

Real‑World Caveats

The manufacturer's 16,000‑mile interval assumes motorway‑weighted use. In real UK conditions, short urban journeys can lead to fuel dilution and oil degradation, which is particularly dangerous on the 276DT because the oil pump housing has a known weakness. Failure of the oil pump can result in catastrophic engine damage – see our 276DT oil pump failure page for a full explanation.

Independent specialists we've spoken to frequently recommend a shorter, 8,000‑mile interval for owners whose driving is predominantly short urban trips, regardless of what the service computer indicates. This is not manufacturer guidance - it is a practical adjustment specialists make based on what they see coming through the workshop. The cost of an extra oil change is a fraction of the alternative, which is a seized engine due to oil starvation.

Additionally, using the correct low‑SAPS oil is critical - see the Common Mistakes section below for why.

Common Mistakes

  • Trusting the service computer interval blindly. It doesn't know your driving pattern is mostly short urban trips - many specialists advise halving the interval for urban‑only use.
  • Using a non‑low‑SAPS oil. This can damage the DPF and after‑treatment system over time - see our DPF & Emissions Systems Explained for why this matters.
  • Confusing 276DT with 306DT. The 3.0‑litre V6 has a slightly different capacity and grade - check your engine code first via our Engine Code Guide.
  • Assuming all V6 diesels share identical figures. The newer Ingenium 3.0‑litre (AJ20D6) has different capacity and specification entirely - see our AJ20D6 Oil Capacity if you're not certain which family you have.
  • Using 0W‑30 instead of 5W‑30. While 0W‑30 may be acceptable in some climates, Jaguar specifies 5W‑30 for this engine; using a thinner oil can affect hydraulic tensioner operation and increase wear.

How to Verify on the Actual Vehicle

The dipstick and filler cap on the 276DT are both clearly marked and accessible from above without removing any panels. To confirm your exact build spec:

  1. Check the engine number stamped on the cylinder block - the location is described on our Engine Number Location guide.
  2. Use the VIN to decode the exact engine variant and year - our VIN decoder can help.
  3. Consult the service book or the vehicle’s infotainment system for the service history; the oil change interval may have been modified by previous owners.
  4. If in doubt, a Jaguar specialist can use the manufacturer’s diagnostic tool to read the exact oil specification stored in the ECU.

Early and late production runs occasionally carry minor running changes that aren't always reflected in aftermarket reference sources - the VIN is your best guide.
