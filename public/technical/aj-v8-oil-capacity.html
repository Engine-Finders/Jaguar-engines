Variant Differences — AJ-V8 Across Jaguar Models

The AJ-V8 family includes 4.0‑litre and 4.2‑litre naturally aspirated versions, plus a supercharged variant (4.2 SC). While the core oil specification is similar, capacity can vary slightly by application and sump design.

Notes & Caveats — The Viscosity Caveat

The AJ-V8 is a robust engine, but it is sensitive to oil viscosity. The hydraulic tappets (lifters) rely on correct oil pressure and flow to operate quietly and efficiently. Using a viscosity that is too thick (e.g., 10W‑40) can cause cold‑start rattles and reduced oil circulation at low temperatures, while too thin (e.g., 0W‑20) may not provide adequate protection at high temperatures. Jaguar’s original recommendation evolved over time: early 4.0‑litre engines specified 5W‑30, while later 4.2‑litre versions were often recommended with 5W‑40, particularly in warmer climates or for high‑performance use. Independent specialists we've spoken to generally recommend sticking with a high‑quality 5W‑30 for normal UK driving, but for hard‑used cars or track days, 5W‑40 may offer better high‑temperature film strength [EM-INTERVIEW]. Always check your owner's manual for the specific grade for your model year and market — this is covered in more detail on our AJ-V8 Timing Chain or Belt? page, which also addresses the importance of maintaining oil pressure for the timing chain tensioners.

Another real‑world caveat: the AJ-V8’s oil filter is cartridge‑style and top‑mounted on most models, making DIY oil changes relatively straightforward. However, the sump plug is located at the rear of the engine, which can be awkward to access without a ramp. Always use a new sump plug washer and torque the plug to specification — over‑tightening can strip the alloy sump threads, a common and costly mistake.

Common Mistakes — AJ-V8 Oil & Service

  • Using the wrong viscosity grade. 5W‑30 is the most widely recommended, but some later 4.2 engines specified 5W‑40. Check your owner’s manual and use a quality oil that meets JLR spec.
  • Over‑filling or under‑filling. The dipstick is located at the front of the engine; always check when the engine is warm and on level ground. Over‑filling can cause oil aeration and reduce engine performance.
  • Reusing the sump plug washer. Always replace the copper or aluminium washer to prevent leaks. A leaking sump plug can lead to low oil levels and engine damage.
  • Not priming the oil filter. Pre‑fill the new cartridge filter with fresh oil before installation to minimise dry‑start time and reduce wear on the tappets and bearings.
  • Ignoring the service interval. Even though 10,000 miles is the official interval, many specialists recommend changing the oil more frequently (e.g., 6,000–8,000 miles) if the car is used for short trips or track work, to prevent sludge buildup in the hydraulic tappets.

How to Verify on the Actual Vehicle

The dipstick on the AJ-V8 is located at the front of the engine, near the oil filler cap, and is easily accessible from above. To check the oil level, ensure the engine is warm, park on level ground, switch off and wait a few minutes for the oil to drain back into the sump, then pull the dipstick, wipe it clean, reinsert and read the level. The filler cap is clearly marked with the recommended grade. For specific model variations, confirm your engine code — the 8th character of the VIN indicates the engine variant — using our Engine Code Decoder guide. If you're unsure which displacement you have, the engine number stamped on the front of the block (near the thermostat housing) can also be cross‑referenced with our Engine Number Location page.
