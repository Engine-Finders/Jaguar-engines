This page provides reference torque specifications for common Jaguar engine families — AJ‑V6, AJ‑V8, and Ingenium (diesel and petrol). Values include cylinder head bolts, main bearing caps, connecting rods, and camshaft caps. All figures are for guidance only; always use the official Jaguar workshop manual for your specific engine code.

  • AJ‑V6 (2.5/3.0L) head bolt torque: 90 Nm + 90° angle [THIRD-PARTY]
  • AJ‑V8 (4.0/4.2/5.0L) head bolt torque: 100 Nm + 180° angle [THIRD-PARTY]
  • Ingenium 2.0 (204DTD/204PT) head bolt: 50 Nm + 120° + 120° [THIRD-PARTY]
  • Always replace stretch bolts and use thread lubricant as specified

Notes & Real‑World Caveats

  • Torque + angle is the standard method. Most modern Jaguar engines use torque‑to‑yield (stretch) bolts. The final tightening is always a specified angle, not just a torque value.
  • Thread lubrication matters. Unless specified otherwise, use clean engine oil on threads and under the bolt head. Dry threads give incorrect readings and can cause bolt failure.
  • Always replace stretch bolts. Head bolts, main bearing bolts, and con rod bolts are designed to stretch permanently. Reusing them risks incomplete clamping and eventual failure.
  • Follow the correct sequence. Cylinder heads, main bearings, and camshaft caps all have a specific tightening sequence. Start from the centre and work outward in steps.
  • Calibrated torque wrenches are essential. A poor‑quality or uncalibrated tool will produce incorrect torque, compromising the joint.

Common Mistakes & Misconceptions

  • Using the wrong torque value for the wrong engine. For example, applying 4.2L V8 head bolt torque (100 Nm + 180°) to an AJ‑V6 (90 Nm + 90°) will overstretch the bolts and damage the block.
  • Ignoring the angle step. Some mechanics only torque to the initial value and skip the angle, leading to inadequate clamp load.
  • Reusing stretch bolts. Many owners and even some workshops reuse head bolts to save cost — this is a false economy that risks gasket failure.
  • Not using a torque wrench for critical fasteners. “Feel” is not accurate; a torque wrench is mandatory for all internal engine fasteners.
  • Forgetting to clean threads. Dirt, old sealant, or corrosion affects torque readings; always clean threads with a tap and use thread chaser before assembly.

Why Correct Torque Matters

Correct torque ensures that the clamping force between components is sufficient to maintain a gas‑tight seal, prevent gasket failure, and avoid fretting or movement that leads to premature wear. Under‑torquing can cause head gasket leaks, oil leaks, or even catastrophic bolt loosening. Over‑torquing can stretch bolts beyond their yield point, leading to breakage or thread stripping in the block. For modern aluminium engines (AJ‑V6, AJ‑V8, Ingenium), the margins are even tighter; aluminium threads are more easily damaged by over‑torquing than cast iron.

Following the manufacturer’s specified torque and angle sequence is not optional — it is a critical part of any engine rebuild or major repair. The figures above are a reference only; always verify against your vehicle’s specific workshop manual.
