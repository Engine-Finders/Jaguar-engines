204PT Oil Capacity, Grade & Service Data

Reference figures for the Ingenium petrol, fitted across XE, XF, F‑Pace and E‑Pace. No opinions, no verdicts - just the numbers, correctly sourced and verified against Jaguar variant differences.

The 204PT takes approximately 6.5 litres with filter change [EM-QUOTE], to Jaguar low‑SAPS specification, typically 0W‑20.

  • Manufacturer service interval: 16,000 miles or 12 months, whichever comes first, on qualifying oil [PATTERN DATA]
  • Real‑world caveat: the 204PT is more reliable than the diesel Ingenium, but cooling system issues (water pump failure) are well‑documented [EM-INTERVIEW] - see below
  • Applies across XE, XF, F‑Pace, E‑Pace and the shared Land Rover AJ200P, with minor capacity variance by application - see the variant table
  • Last verified: August 2026

Year, Market & Facelift Variations

The 204PT was introduced in 2015 with the XE and later spread across the range. Early UK‑spec cars (2015–2018) are more prone to cooling system issues, particularly water pump failure, due to a design that was revised in 2019. Jaguar updated the water pump and thermostat housing materials to improve durability. This revision was applied to all new engines from 2019 onward and is retrofittable to earlier cars.

From 2020, a revised oil specification (STJLR.03.5006) was introduced, which is backward‑compatible but requires the correct low‑SAPS approval. For UK market cars, the VIN’s 10th character indicates the model year - see our engine code guide for decoding. If your car was built before 2019 and you experience coolant loss or overheating, it is worth checking with a Jaguar specialist whether the updated water pump has been fitted.

Real‑World Caveats

The 204PT is generally more reliable than its diesel counterpart, but cooling system issues are well‑documented. The water pump and thermostat housing can develop leaks, especially on early examples. This is not a catastrophic failure, but if ignored it can lead to overheating and potential head gasket damage.

Independent specialists we've spoken to recommend replacing the water pump as a preventative measure at around 60,000 miles or 6 years, especially on cars used in stop‑start urban traffic. The cost of a water pump replacement is around £400–£800 [EM-QUOTE], which is a fraction of the cost of a head gasket repair or engine rebuild.

Additionally, while the oil change interval is 16,000 miles, many specialists recommend a shorter 10,000‑mile interval for owners who do a lot of short journeys, to prevent fuel dilution (less of an issue on petrol than diesel) and maintain engine health. For a full explanation of the cooling system weaknesses, see our 204PT cooling system failure page.

Common Mistakes

  • Trusting the service computer interval blindly. It doesn't know your driving pattern is mostly short urban trips - many specialists advise halving the interval for urban‑only use.
  • Using a non‑low‑SAPS oil. While petrol engines don't have DPF concerns, the low‑SAPS specification helps protect the catalytic converter and GPF (gasoline particulate filter) on later models - see our DPF & Emissions Systems Explained for why this matters.
  • Confusing 204PT with 204DTD. The diesel Ingenium has a different capacity and grade - check your engine code first via our Engine Code Guide.
  • Assuming all four‑cylinder Ingenium petrols share identical figures. The AJ20P3 (1.5‑litre) and AJ20P6 (2.0‑litre) variants have different capacities; the 204PT is the 2.0‑litre, but early production had some running changes.
  • Ignoring coolant leaks. A small coolant loss may indicate a water pump or thermostat housing failure; addressing it early avoids more expensive repairs.

How to Verify on the Actual Vehicle

The dipstick and filler cap on the 204PT are both clearly marked and accessible from above without removing any panels. To confirm your exact build spec:

  1. Check the engine number stamped on the cylinder block - the location is described on our Engine Number Location guide.
  2. Use the VIN to decode the exact engine variant and year - our VIN decoder can help.
  3. Consult the service book or the vehicle’s infotainment system for the service history; the oil change interval may have been modified by previous owners.
  4. If in doubt, a Jaguar specialist can use the manufacturer’s diagnostic tool to read the exact oil specification stored in the ECU.

Early and late production runs occasionally carry minor running changes that aren't always reflected in aftermarket reference sources - the VIN is your best guide.
