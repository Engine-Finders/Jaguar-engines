Notes & Caveats

The manufacturer’s 16,000‑mile interval assumes a balanced mix of motorway and urban driving. In real‑world UK conditions - especially for supercharged V8s used mainly in town or on track - the oil is subjected to higher thermal stress and more frequent short‑trip dilution. Independent specialists we’ve consulted consistently recommend reducing the interval to 8,000 miles or 6 months for vehicles that see frequent stop‑start traffic, short journeys, or track use, regardless of what the service computer suggests. This is not a manufacturer directive - it’s a practical adjustment based on what workshops observe when they strip these engines. The cost of an extra oil change is negligible compared to the potential repair bill from a neglected supercharger or timing chain tensioner. Also note that the supercharger itself has its own separate oil system; it is not part of the engine oil capacity and should be checked at around 80,000 miles - see our supercharger oil service guide for details.

Common Mistakes

  • Using 0W‑20 instead of 5W‑30. The AJ133 is not specced for low‑viscosity oils; using a thinner grade can lead to inadequate protection under supercharger loads.
  • Ignoring the severe‑service interval. Many owners follow the computer blindly and end up with sludge‑related issues - see our AJ133 chain tensioner failure page for why this matters.
  • Confusing AJ133 with AJ126. The 3.0 V6 has a different capacity and spec - always confirm your engine code first via our engine code guide.
  • Assuming the supercharger oil is topped up with engine oil. It is a sealed system with its own lubricant; never mix them.
  • Forgetting to replace the oil filter O‑ring. A cheap part that, if overlooked, can cause a slow leak and oil pressure loss.

How to Verify on the Actual Vehicle

The dipstick and filler cap on the AJ133 are clearly marked and accessible from the top. The filler cap will indicate the correct grade (5W‑30). For absolute certainty, cross‑reference your engine number with our engine number location guide - early‑build XF (pre‑2012) and later F‑Type units have the same oil spec, but some non‑UK market cars may have different factory fills. Always use oil that meets JLR.03.5003 (or the current equivalent) to protect the catalytic converters and the supercharger coupler.

Why This Specification Matters

The AJ133 is a high‑performance supercharged V8 that generates significant heat and places heavy demands on the oil. Using the correct 5W‑30 grade ensures the oil flows quickly at cold start (protecting the timing chain tensioners) and maintains sufficient film strength under full‑throttle supercharger boost. The JLR.03.5003 specification is not a marketing label - it defines a specific additive package that resists aeration (critical for the supercharger drive) and protects the supercharger coupler from premature wear. Using a cheaper oil that meets the viscosity but not the approval can lead to coupler rattle, timing chain stretch, or even catastrophic bearing failure - all of which are well documented on our AJ133 chain tensioner and supercharger coupler failure pages. A single extra oil change every 8,000 miles costs around £150 and is the cheapest insurance you can buy against a £5,000+ repair.
