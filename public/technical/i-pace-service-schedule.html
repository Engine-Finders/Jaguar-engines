The I-Pace has no engine oil changes. Instead, the service schedule centres on battery coolant replacement every 5 years or 90,000 miles [EM-INTERVIEW], brake fluid every 3 years [EM-INTERVIEW], and a cabin filter change every 2 years [EM-INTERVIEW].

  • Major service interval: every 2 years or 24,000 miles - alternating inspection and full service checklists [EM-INTERVIEW]
  • Typical annual service cost: £200–£450 at independent specialists [EM-QUOTE] vs. £400–£850 at main dealers
  • Key EV-specific maintenance: coolant system health check, battery module balancing, and HV safety inspection [EM-INTERVIEW]
  • First service due at 12 months or 24,000 miles (whichever comes first) - unlike most JLR models, the I-Pace does not have a strict oil-change-based first service

Notes & Real-World Caveats

The I-Pace service schedule is deceptively simple compared to combustion-engine JLR models, but EV servicing requires specific skills and equipment - not every garage can handle high-voltage coolant work or battery health diagnostics. Many owners report that independent specialists charge 30–50% less than main dealers for the same service items [EM-SURVEY], but it's critical to verify that the specialist has Jaguar EV training and access to JLR’s diagnostic software (GDS).

A commonly overlooked item: the 12V battery. The I-Pace’s auxiliary battery powers the vehicle’s computers, infotainment, and cooling system pumps when the high-voltage system is off. Failure is a common cause of “won’t start” and immobiliser issues, especially if the car is left parked for extended periods. Battery replacement is relatively inexpensive (£120–£200) but diagnostic time can add another £100 if the fault isn't immediately obvious.

Common Mistakes I-Pace Owners Make

  • Treating it like a combustion service. The I-Pace has no oil, no filters, no spark plugs - its maintenance needs are different. Some owners incorrectly assume the first service is due at 12,000 miles like the XF, when in fact the I-Pace can go to 24,000 miles.
  • Ignoring the battery coolant flush. The high-voltage battery requires a specialised coolant that degrades over time. Delaying the 5-year flush can reduce battery efficiency and, in extreme cases, lead to thermal management faults.
  • Using non-JLR specified brake fluid. The I-Pace’s regenerative braking system is sensitive to fluid properties; use only DOT 4 Low Viscosity as specified by JLR.
  • Forgetting to update the service schedule after recall work. Several recalls (e.g., H441 for battery software) affect the thermal management system - check your recall status on our I-Pace recall page and ensure your service provider knows about any software updates that alter coolant requirements.

How to Verify Your I-Pace Service History

The most reliable way to confirm your I-Pace’s service status is to use the Jaguar InControl system (available via the main infotainment screen or the InControl app), which stores service schedules, recalls, and software update history. Alternatively, any Jaguar main dealer or specialist with access to the GDS system can pull a complete service record using your VIN.

For independent specialists, we recommend using our VIN Decoder to confirm the exact build specification before any service work - this avoids assumptions about coolant type, brake booster software level, or battery firmware.
