The Jaguar XK requires annual or 10,000‑mile oil changes (older X100) or 12,000‑mile intervals (X150) with a major service every 36,000 miles or 3 years that includes spark plugs, air filters, and brake fluid. Transmission fluid and filter are due at 60,000 miles for the ZF 6HP/8HP units [EM-INTERVIEW]. Independent specialist costs are typically 30–50% lower than main dealers [EM-QUOTE].

  • Transmission service (ZF 6HP/8HP): £400–£700 at specialists [EM-QUOTE] - often overlooked but critical for longevity
  • Brake fluid: every 2 years - use DOT 4 LV as specified

Notes & Real‑World Caveats

The XK service schedule is well understood, but several critical points often get overlooked:

  • Transmission fluid is not lifetime. ZF recommends fluid and filter changes every 60,000 miles, but many owners ignore this. Neglect leads to shift quality degradation and, eventually, costly transmission rebuilds [EM-INTERVIEW].
  • Oil grade matters across generations. Earlier X100 models use 5W‑30; later X150 with the 5.0 V8 require 0W‑20 for fuel economy and cold‑start protection. Using the wrong viscosity can affect timing chain tensioner operation.
  • Spark plugs are due at 36,000 miles. The V8 engines run hot; deferred changes can cause misfires and increased fuel consumption.
  • Brake fluid is often overlooked. The XK's braking system needs fresh DOT 4 LV fluid every 2 years to maintain pedal feel and prevent moisture‑related corrosion.
  • Coolant replacement is critical. The 5.0 V8 (AJ133) is known for water pump and crossover pipe leaks; regular coolant changes help prevent corrosion and seal degradation.

Common Mistakes XK Owners Make

  • Following the service indicator blindly. The XK’s variable service display can extend intervals based on driving style, but many specialists recommend sticking to fixed annual intervals, especially for high‑mileage cars.
  • Using non‑JLR specification oil. Always use oil meeting JLR.03.5006 or equivalent - cheaper oils may lack the necessary anti‑wear additives for the V8 valvetrain.
  • Ignoring the transmission service. Many owners believe the ZF box is "sealed for life" - it is not. A service at 60,000 miles is a fraction of the cost of a replacement.
  • Confusing 4.2 and 5.0 service items. The 5.0 V8 takes more oil and a different spark plug type - check your engine code before ordering parts Engine Code Guide.
  • Forgetting the cabin filter. It’s cheap and easy to replace, yet often skipped; a clogged filter reduces air‑con performance and can cause odours.

How to Verify Your XK Service History

The most reliable way to confirm your XK’s service status is to use the Jaguar InControl system (available via the infotainment screen or the InControl app) for X150 models, or check the physical service book for X100 cars. Alternatively, any Jaguar main dealer or specialist with access to the GDS system can pull a complete service record using your VIN.

Why This Specification Matters

Sticking to the correct service schedule on an XK isn't just about maintaining resale value - it directly affects the longevity of the engine and transmission. The 5.0 V8 (AJ133) is particularly sensitive to oil grade and change intervals; using 0W‑20 as specified ensures proper flow to the timing chain tensioners, which have been a known weak point on early examples. Similarly, the ZF transmission relies on clean, fresh fluid to maintain clutch pack health and prevent shift solenoids from sticking. A £400–£700 transmission service every 60,000 miles is far cheaper than a £4,000–£6,000 replacement. For X100 owners, regular coolant changes (every 3–5 years) help prevent the notorious heater core and water pump issues that plague older AJ‑V8s.
