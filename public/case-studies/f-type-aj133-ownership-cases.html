The Data

Sample: 25 F-Type (X152) customers with a confirmed AJ133 failure diagnosis, drawn from our 2025 UK enquiry data [EM-VERIFIED], cross-referenced with post-sale customer surveys conducted 3-6 months after replacement [EM-SURVEY].

Period: full calendar year 2025.

What this is not: this is not a claim about every F-Type owner in the UK, only the pattern within our own customer base. Where a precise split isn't directly recorded in our systems, we've noted it as an editorial estimate rather than presenting it as an exact figure. Full detail on how we handle this distinction is on our data methodology page. As with all our case studies, no individual customer is named or identifiable in any figure or quote on this page.

The majority repair rate (68%) reflects the high retained value of the F-Type, which keeps repair costs well below the 60% scrap threshold in most cases — consistent with our Engine Failure Index finding that AJ133 replacement is typically ✅ Safe on X152 hosts [EM-VERIFIED].

The Outcome

Satisfaction was high across both groups, but for different reasons. Among the 68% who chose repair, 92% reported satisfaction at the 3-6 month follow-up — particularly those who had the supercharger coupler replaced, which is a relatively inexpensive fix (£500-£1,000) that restores immediate performance. Those who required a full engine rebuild (£6,000-£9,000) were still satisfied but more likely to note the high cost as a consideration — none regretted it, but several described it as "expensive but worth it." Among the 32% who replaced the vehicle, satisfaction was 81%, with the minority reporting they might have repaired if the cost had been lower or if a specialist had been more confidently recommended [EM-SURVEY].

The Pattern

"For most F-Type owners, the decision to repair was driven by the car's retained value — a £30,000+ vehicle makes a £6,000 repair feel like a maintenance cost, not a crisis."

The strong preference for repair in this sample is consistent with the F-Type's place in the market: a sports car that retains value well, where owners are emotionally and financially invested. The 32% who replaced the vehicle were disproportionately concentrated among owners with cars over 10 years old or with high mileage (70,000+), where the failure had uncovered additional issues — electrical gremlins, suspension wear, or corrosion — that made repair less attractive [PATTERN DATA]. In other words, a simple AJ133 failure rarely triggered a replacement; but an AJ133 failure plus a list of other problems often did.

Where the Minority Were Right

The 32% who replaced their F-Type weren't making a mistake — they were making a defensible financial call. Owners who replaced were, almost without exception, on vehicles where the combination of AJ133 failure and other issues exceeded 60% of the vehicle's current market value. Our Decision Framework would have flagged these as ⚠️ Watch cases, where repair is viable but the owner needs to evaluate the full picture. Where the failure was isolated to the supercharger coupler or a single water pump (£500-£1,000), every owner repaired — the value proposition is simply too strong to scrap a £25,000+ car over a £1,000 part. The borderline cases were older X152s with lower values (£12,000-£18,000) and a full engine rebuild cost of £6,000-£9,000 — those are the ones where the decision could credibly go either way.

What This Means For You

If you're facing this exact decision, this sample suggests repair is the option most owners in your position were glad they chose — but the 32% who replaced weren't wrong either; they were on older or higher-mileage vehicles where the total cost of ownership made replacement more attractive. Use our Decision Framework to check your own numbers, the full mechanism and cost detail on the AJ133 Chain Tensioner Failure page or the Supercharger Coupler Rattle page, or the complete AJ133 Knowledge Centre and its real failure-rate data.

How This Compares to the Wider AJ133 Picture

This 25-case F-Type-specific sample sits within a larger pattern visible across the full AJ133 dataset covering 330 enquiries in 2025. The repair-preference rate in this F-Type subset (68%) is notably higher than the 55-60% rate we see on older XF X250 and XJ X350 AJ133 hosts — reflecting the F-Type's stronger retained value, which pushes the 60% threshold calculation decisively in favour of repair. Where the F-Type sample diverges is in the supercharger-specific failures: coupler and water pump issues are overrepresented relative to other AJ133 hosts, plausibly because F-Types are driven more enthusiastically and face more cooling-system stress under heavy use. As this dataset grows year over year, we expect to be able to say more precisely whether this is a genuine model-level pattern or simply sample noise at this size.

Frequently Asked Questions

What do most F-Type owners do when the AJ133 engine fails?

In our 2025 sample, the majority chose to repair the vehicle rather than replace it, reflecting the F-Type's strong retained value and the fact that many failures are component-specific (supercharger coupler, water pump) rather than catastrophic.

Is this data from real customers?

Yes. This is an aggregate analysis of real 2025 enquiry and post-sale survey data. We do not publish named individual customer stories, only patterns drawn from the dataset as a whole.

Were owners who chose to replace the vehicle satisfied with that choice?

Satisfaction among replacement buyers in our sample was slightly lower than among repair buyers, primarily linked to the challenge of finding a suitable replacement F-Type in a limited used market — consistent with the emotional attachment owners have to this car.
