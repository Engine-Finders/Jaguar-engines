What Does Supply & Fit Actually Include?

The full breakdown of what you’re paying for when a specialist quotes “supply and fit” for your Jaguar engine replacement – including labour, parts, ancillaries, and the hidden extras that often catch owners out.

The short answer: “Supply and fit” means the specialist provides the replacement engine (used, reconditioned, or rebuilt) and installs it in your car. But it does not always include ancillaries, fluids, or unexpected parts – and the quote may exclude diagnostics, collection, or delivery.

  • The engine unit is the largest cost – £3,000–£9,000 supply‑only for most Jaguar V6/V8 engines [EM‑QUOTE]
  • Labour adds 10–18 hours at £65–£100/hr, typically £650–£1,800 [EM‑QUOTE]
  • Ancillaries (gaskets, belts, fluids, filters) can add £200–£600 depending on the engine
  • Always ask: “Does this include everything needed to drive the car away?” – if not, clarify the exclusions

What’s Typically Included in a Supply & Fit Quote

A reputable Jaguar specialist will usually itemise these core components. Always ask for a written breakdown before committing.

🔧

The Engine Unit

The replacement engine itself – whether used, reconditioned, or rebuilt. This is the headline cost, and it should come with a warranty (typically 12–24 months for reconditioned units).

🛠️

Labour for Removal & Installation

The workshop time to remove the old engine, prepare the bay, and install the new unit. Labour hours vary by engine – see our labour rates guide.

🔩

Basic Consumables

New engine oil, coolant, and filter(s) are almost always included. Some specialists also include a new cambelt or timing chain kit as part of the fit.

📋

Warranty

A proper supply and fit quote includes a warranty that covers both the engine and the labour. Always check the terms – unlimited mileage is best.

For a full definition of engine conditions (used, reconditioned, rebuilt), see our Reconditioned vs Used vs Rebuilt guide.

What’s Often NOT Included

This is where many owners are caught out. A quote that seems cheap may be missing essential items that add hundreds to the final bill.

❌

Ancillary Parts

Water pumps, alternators, starter motors, air conditioning compressors, and power steering pumps are not included unless specified. If your old ancillaries are worn, you may need to replace them – budget £200–£800 extra.

❌

Gaskets, Seals & Belts

While some specialists include a basic gasket set, others charge extra for a full “service kit” – head gaskets, manifold gaskets, sump gaskets, and cambelts. Ask for a clear list.

❌

Diagnostics & Pre‑Fit Inspection

If the specialist needs to diagnose the failure or inspect the donor engine, this is often charged separately – typically £100–£300.

❌

Vehicle Collection & Delivery

Some workshops offer collection and return of your vehicle, but this is rarely included in the base supply and fit quote – expect £50–£150 each way.

Always ask for a comprehensive quote that lists every component and service. Our warranty standards page explains what a good warranty should cover.

Hidden Extras to Watch For

Even after you have a quote, these are the most common additional charges that can inflate the final bill – and how to spot them.

  • Engine flush and cooling system flush – often recommended before fitting, can add £100–£200.
  • New battery – if your old battery is weak, fitting a new one may be required; budget £150–£300.
  • AC regas – if the air conditioning system is disturbed, a regas may be needed; £80–£150.
  • Software updates – modern Jaguar engines often require ECU reprogramming; this can be £100–£300.
  • Delivery and shipping – if the engine is sourced from a remote supplier, shipping costs may be passed on; £100–£300.

The best defence is a written quote that states “all‑inclusive” and lists every foreseeable item. Our specialist vetting page explains how we ensure our network quotes transparently.

Our Verdict: How to Compare Supply & Fit Quotes

Not all quotes are equal. Here’s a simple checklist to ensure you’re comparing like‑for‑like.

✅ What a Good Quote Shows

  • Exact engine condition (used, recon, rebuilt)
  • Warranty terms (length, mileage, parts & labour)
  • Itemised labour hours and rate
  • List of all parts and consumables included
  • Firm price for ancillaries if needed
  • Collection/delivery costs (if any)

❌ What a Bad Quote Hides

  • Vague “engine and fit” with no breakdown
  • “Labour” without hours or rate
  • No mention of warranty or “subject to inspection”
  • Additional charges for “unforeseen” parts
  • Excludes diagnostics and collection
Always ask these questions:
  • “Is this a fixed price, or are there potential extras?”
  • “What exactly is included in the ‘fit’ – labour only, or gaskets, fluids, and ancillaries?”
  • “What is the warranty and what does it cover?”
  • “Can you provide a written itemised quote?”

Armed with these answers, you can compare specialists fairly and avoid surprise bills.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the average cost of supply and fit for a Jaguar engine?

For most Jaguar V6/V8 engines, total supply and fit costs range from £4,000 to £10,000, depending on the engine code and condition. The AJ133 5.0 V8 is the most expensive, while the 2.0 petrol (204PT) is the cheapest. See our cost by engine code page for exact figures.

Are gaskets and belts always included in supply and fit?

No – many specialists charge extra for a “service kit” that includes gaskets, seals, timing belts, and water pumps. Always confirm what is included. Some reconditioned engines come with a full gasket set, but it’s not guaranteed.

How much does labour add to a Jaguar engine replacement?

Labour typically adds 10–18 hours at independent rates of £65–£100 per hour. That means £650–£1,800 for fitting. Main dealers charge 30–50% more. See our labour rates guide for detailed breakdowns.

What warranty should I expect with supply and fit?

Reconditioned engines usually come with a 12–24 month, unlimited‑mileage warranty covering parts and labour. Used engines often have a 30–90 day guarantee only. Always get the warranty in writing. Our warranty standards page explains what to look for.

Can I supply my own engine and just pay for fitting?

Yes, some specialists will fit an engine you supply – but they may not warranty the engine itself. They may also charge a higher labour rate to cover the risk of fitting an unknown unit. Always check before buying a donor engine.

How do I compare quotes fairly?

Ask every specialist for an itemised written quote that includes: engine condition, warranty, labour hours and rate, all parts and consumables, and a list of any exclusions. Use our Repair vs Replace Calculator to model total costs.
