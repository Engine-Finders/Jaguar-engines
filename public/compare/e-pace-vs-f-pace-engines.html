Engine Comparison

E‑Pace vs F‑Pace
Engine Focus

Two compact SUVs, very different engine line‑ups. The E‑Pace offers efficient four‑cylinders; the F‑Pace adds V6 and V8 power. Here’s which one fits your driving and budget.

The F‑Pace is the better buy for most owners - it offers a broader engine line‑up (including the punchy AJ126 V6 and potent AJ133 V8), more space, and stronger retained values. The E‑Pace is the smarter city choice: lighter, more efficient, and cheaper to run, but limited to four‑cylinder Ingenium engines.

  • E‑Pace: 140 enquiries in 2025, engines: 204DTD (diesel) and 204PT (petrol) [EM‑VERIFIED]
  • F‑Pace: 220 enquiries, engines: 204DTD, 204PT, AJ126 3.0 V6 SC, AJ133 5.0 V8 SC [EM‑VERIFIED]
  • F‑Pace’s AJ126 V6 costs £4,500–£7,500 to replace recon; AJ133 V8 costs £5,500–£9,000
  • E‑Pace’s 204DTD replacement £3,500–£5,800; 204PT £3,000–£5,000 - both significantly cheaper
  • F‑Pace has higher average running costs but offers superior performance and towing capacity

The Verdict

The F‑Pace is the stronger all‑rounder. Its wider engine range - from efficient four‑cylinders to the charismatic AJ126 supercharged V6 and the thunderous AJ133 V8 - gives it a versatility the E‑Pace simply cannot match. The F‑Pace also commands higher retained values and offers superior towing and load‑carrying capability. However, the E‑Pace is the more affordable choice for city‑based drivers who prioritise fuel economy, parking ease, and lower running costs. If you don't need the extra space or power, the E‑Pace is a very sensible buy; for everyone else, the F‑Pace wins.

E‑Pace Engine Line‑up

The E‑Pace is offered with two Ingenium four‑cylinder engines: the 204DTD 2.0‑litre diesel and the 204PT 2.0‑litre petrol. Both are shared with the XE, XF, and F‑Pace, but in the E‑Pace they are tuned for a lighter, more agile chassis. The 204DTD produces 150–240 PS depending on variant; the 204PT offers 200–300 PS. The defining failure for the 204DTD is pre‑2019 timing chain tensioner wear, substantially revised after the 2019 model year [EM‑INTERVIEW]. The 204PT’s main weakness is the water pump and cooling system, which typically fails between 60,000–120,000 miles [PATTERN DATA]. Replacement costs are moderate: 204DTD £3,500–£5,800 fitted, 204PT £3,000–£5,000. Full specifications and reliability ratings are on the 204DTD Knowledge Centre and 204PT Knowledge Centre.

F‑Pace Engine Line‑up

The F‑Pace offers everything the E‑Pace does, plus the AJ126 3.0‑litre supercharged V6 (340–380 PS) and the AJ133 5.0‑litre supercharged V8 (550 PS in SVR). The AJ126 is known for cooling system leaks - water pump and crossover pipe failures are common, with repair costs of £600–£1,200, but full replacement runs £4,500–£7,500 [EM‑QUOTE]. The AJ133, especially pre‑2013, suffers timing chain tensioner wear; post‑2013 units are much improved, but a recon engine still costs £5,500–£9,000. The F‑Pace’s V6 and V8 options give it genuine performance credentials and a towing capacity of up to 2,400 kg, against the E‑Pace’s 1,800 kg. For detailed failure analysis, see our AJ126 Cooling System Failure and AJ133 Chain Tensioner pages.

Where F‑Pace Genuinely Wins

The F‑Pace’s superiority isn’t just about power; it’s about capability. The AJ126 V6 delivers a soundtrack and urgency that the four‑cylinder Ingeniums can’t match, while the AJ133 SVR is a genuine performance SUV. The F‑Pace also offers superior towing capacity, more rear passenger space, and a larger boot. In our enquiry data, the F‑Pace accounts for 220 requests in 2025 versus 140 for the E‑Pace, reflecting stronger demand from owners who value performance and practicality [EM‑VERIFIED]. While the E‑Pace is cheaper to run, the F‑Pace retains its value better, making the total cost of ownership surprisingly close over five years for many buyers.

Five‑Year Ownership Cost Comparison

Purchase price aside, the running cost difference between the E‑Pace and F‑Pace hinges on engine choice. The E‑Pace’s four‑cylinder engines are cheaper to service, insure, and fuel. The F‑Pace’s V6 and V8 engines consume more fuel and carry higher insurance groups, and their replacement costs are £1,500–£3,500 higher. However, the F‑Pace’s residual values are stronger, often offsetting the extra running costs. For a typical 10,000‑mile‑per‑year driver, the E‑Pace 204DTD will cost about £500–£800 less per year in fuel and maintenance than an F‑Pace AJ126. Over five years, that’s a £2,500–£4,000 difference - significant, but the F‑Pace’s extra space and driving pleasure may justify it for many owners. Which Should You Buy? - Three Scenarios

🚙

City Commuter / Urban Driver

If you spend most of your time in town, the E‑Pace is the obvious choice. Its smaller footprint, lighter steering, and efficient four‑cylinder engines make parking and navigating tight streets a breeze. Choose the 204DTD for high‑mileage motorway commutes or the 204PT for quieter petrol running.

🏔️

Family / Towing / Long‑Distance

If you need space for passengers, a large boot, or the ability to tow a caravan or trailer, the F‑Pace is the winner. The AJ126 V6 provides effortless torque for towing and overtaking, and the cabin is noticeably more spacious. The F‑Pace also has a more refined ride on motorways.

⚡

Performance Enthusiast

If you crave power and sound, there’s no contest: the F‑Pace SVR with the AJ133 V8 is a genuine sports car in SUV clothing. The E‑Pace’s range‑topping P300 is brisk, but it can’t match the V8’s theatre. Be prepared for higher running costs, but the grin factor is unmatched.

Whichever you choose, the same repair vs replace framework applies when the engine needs attention. Always factor in the vehicle’s current value and the cost of a replacement engine before making a final decision.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the F‑Pace engine better than the E‑Pace?

It depends on your needs. The F‑Pace offers more powerful V6 and V8 options that are better for towing and performance, but the E‑Pace’s four‑cylinders are more efficient and cheaper to run. For most buyers, the F‑Pace’s broader range makes it the more versatile choice.

Which is cheaper to replace an engine on - E‑Pace or F‑Pace?

The E‑Pace is significantly cheaper. Its 204DTD costs £3,500–£5,800 to replace, while the F‑Pace’s AJ126 is £4,500–£7,500 and the AJ133 is £5,500–£9,000. Both are within the 60% scrap threshold for most examples, but the E‑Pace is easier on the wallet.

Which Jaguar SUV has the better reliability record?

Our data shows that the E‑Pace’s four‑cylinder Ingenium engines have fewer catastrophic failures than the F‑Pace’s V6 and V8, but the F‑Pace’s issues are well‑understood and manageable with preventative maintenance. The 2019‑on E‑Pace with revised timing chain tensioner is particularly solid.

Is the F‑Pace worth the extra money over the E‑Pace?

If you need the space, performance, or towing capability, yes. If you primarily drive in the city and value economy, the E‑Pace is the smarter financial decision. The F‑Pace’s stronger residuals help offset its higher purchase price over time.

What is the 60% scrap threshold for Jaguar?

Jaguar uses a 60% scrap threshold: if the cost of a replacement engine exceeds 60% of the vehicle’s market value, it’s usually cheaper to scrap the car. We apply this rule in our repair‑or‑replace framework.
