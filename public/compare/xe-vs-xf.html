XE vs XF – Which Should You Buy?

Two of Jaguar's most‑enquired saloons, head to head. One is lighter, more agile, and aimed at the driver. The other offers more space, a more relaxed ride, and a longer heritage. We break down the real‑world differences using 2025 UK enquiry data.

The XE is the better choice for most buyers – it offers a sharper drive, lower running costs, and the same core engine line‑up for less money. The XF makes sense if you regularly carry rear passengers, need a larger boot, or prefer the more traditional luxury saloon feel.

  • XE X760: 260 enquiries in 2025, 7,000‑20,000 typical value, available with AJ200D and AJ200P engines [EM‑VERIFIED]
  • XF X260: 276 enquiries in 2025, 8,000‑25,000 typical value, also available with AJ200D and AJ200P [EM‑VERIFIED]
  • Both share the same Ingenium 2.0 diesel and petrol engines – differences are in chassis, weight, and interior space
  • XE is lighter, more fuel‑efficient, and costs less to service; XF offers a more refined motorway ride and superior rear‑seat space
  • Our verdict: XE for driving enthusiasts and single‑car owners; XF for families and those who prioritise comfort over handling

The Verdict

The XE is the better buy for most drivers. It delivers the same core engine and technology for less money, and its lighter weight gives it a noticeable edge in agility and fuel economy. The XF is the choice if rear‑seat space or a more traditional Jaguar limousine feel matters to you – its extra length and weight smooth out rough roads and make it a superb motorway cruiser.

XE Deep Dive

The XE is Jaguar's compact executive saloon, launched in 2015 as a direct rival to the BMW 3‑Series and Mercedes C‑Class. It's the lightest Jaguar saloon and benefits from the brand's aluminium‑intensive architecture. The XE X760 has generated 260 enquiries in our 2025 data [EM‑VERIFIED], making it the second most‑enquired Jaguar saloon after the XF X260.

The most common engine in the XE is the Ingenium 2.0 diesel (AJ200D / 204DTD), followed by the petrol (AJ200P / 204PT). The XE is available with rear‑wheel drive or all‑wheel drive, and offers a genuinely engaging driving experience – precise steering, well‑controlled body roll, and a ride that's firm but not harsh. Full specs and reliability ratings live on the 204DTD Knowledge Centre and 204PT Knowledge Centre.

XF Deep Dive

The XF is Jaguar's executive saloon, the direct rival to the BMW 5‑Series and Mercedes E‑Class. The X260 generation (2015–2023) is the most‑enquired Jaguar saloon in our 2025 data, with 276 enquiries [EM‑VERIFIED] – only marginally ahead of the XE. Its appeal lies in a larger cabin, a bigger boot, and a more compliant ride over long distances.

The XF shares the Ingenium 2.0 diesel and petrol engines with the XE, but also offers the AJ126 3.0 V6 supercharged petrol in higher‑spec models, delivering 340bhp and a noticeably more refined, effortless driving character. The XF's aluminium architecture makes it lighter than many rivals, but it's still heavier than the XE – a difference you can feel in both acceleration and fuel consumption. Full specs on the AJ126 Knowledge Centre and the 204DTD Knowledge Centre.

Cost of Ownership Comparison

Over a typical three‑year ownership window, the XE is noticeably cheaper to run. Its lower kerb weight translates into better fuel economy – expect 5–10% better mpg from the same engine. Service costs are also slightly lower due to fewer consumables (tyres, brakes) over the same mileage. The XF's main cost advantage is depreciation – it tends to hold its value slightly better than the XE, especially on the V6 models, but that benefit is offset by higher running costs. Insurance groups are broadly similar for equivalent engines.

Which Should You Buy? Three Scenarios

There's no single 'right' answer – it depends on your priorities.

🏎️

Driving Enthusiast

Buy the XE. It's lighter, more agile, and feels more alive on a twisty B‑road. The 2.0 petrol or diesel both deliver strong performance in a more eager chassis.

👨‍👩‍👧‍👦

Family User

Buy the XF. The extra rear legroom and boot space make a real difference for child seats, school runs, and weekend trips. The softer ride also keeps passengers happier on longer journeys.

💼

Business User / Motorway Commuter

XF with diesel – if you're covering high motorway mileage, the XF's longer wheelbase and quieter cabin make it the more comfortable companion. The 2.0 diesel returns decent economy while offering a more relaxed cruising experience.

If you're still undecided, try a back‑to‑back test drive. The difference in character is immediately apparent within the first mile.

Which Is Easier and Cheaper to Replace When It Fails?

Both the XE and XF share the same core engines, so the cost of replacing an engine is identical for the same engine code. The fitted cost of a reconditioned Ingenium 2.0 diesel (AJ200D / 204DTD) is £4,150–£6,710, while the 2.0 petrol (AJ200P / 204PT) is £3,650–£5,910 [EM‑QUOTE]. The XF's optional AJ126 V6 costs £5,280–£8,540 fitted, making it the most expensive option.

Labour times are slightly longer on the XF due to the larger engine bay and the V6's additional complexity – allow an extra 1–2 hours. Both models are equally well‑supported by independent specialists, but the XE's higher sales volume means used engines and parts are slightly more readily available. Full cost tables are available on our Replacement Cost by Engine Code page.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which is more reliable, XE or XF?

Both share the same core engines and many electrical systems, so reliability is broadly similar. The XE's lighter construction and fewer optional V6 engines give it a marginal advantage in long‑term reliability, but well‑maintained examples of both are capable of 150,000+ miles. Always check service history for both.

Is the XF more expensive to maintain than the XE?

Yes – the XF is slightly heavier and often equipped with larger wheels and tyres, which increases consumable costs. Servicing costs are also marginally higher due to the larger engine bay and, on V6 models, additional oil and filter capacity. Plan for an extra £100–£200 per year.

Which has better rear passenger space?

The XF is the clear winner here. It offers genuine adult‑sized rear seats with good legroom, while the XE is more of a 2+2. If you regularly carry rear passengers, the XF is the right choice.

Can I fit a child seat in the XE?

Yes – the XE has Isofix mounts in the rear, but the rear cabin is tight. A rear‑facing child seat will force the front seat forward significantly. For families, the XF is strongly recommended.

Which model is better for long motorway journeys?

The XF, decisively. Its longer wheelbase, quieter cabin, and softer suspension tuning make it the more relaxing motorway companion. The XE is more engaging but is firmer and noisier on the motorway.

Should I buy a V6 XF or stick with a 2.0?

The V6 is wonderful for enthusiasts – it's smooth, characterful, and effortless. However, it costs significantly more to run and buy. For most buyers, the 2.0 diesel offers the best balance of performance, economy, and value.
