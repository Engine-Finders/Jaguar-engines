XF vs XJ

Two of Jaguar's most‑enquired saloons, head to head. One is the sporty, more affordable executive choice; the other is the flagship luxury limousine. Here's the honest verdict on which one makes sense for your budget and lifestyle.

The Jaguar XF is the better choice for most buyers - it’s more affordable to buy and run, offers a sportier driving experience, and its engine range (Ingenium diesel, petrol and V6) covers the vast majority of needs. The XJ delivers undeniable prestige, space and V8 thunder, but at a significantly higher purchase price and running cost. For everyday driving and long‑term value, the XF wins.

The Verdict

For the vast majority of buyers, the Jaguar XF is the stronger choice. It generates more enquiries in our data, reflecting its broader appeal and larger sales volume. It offers a better blend of driving dynamics, practicality and running costs. The XJ is a magnificent luxury saloon that commands attention, but its higher purchase price, thirstier engines and more complex repair needs make it a less sensible choice for everyday ownership. Unless you specifically need the extra rear legroom, the V8 experience, or the prestige of a flagship, the XF delivers 90% of the XJ’s luxury at a significantly lower cost of entry and ownership.

XF Deep Dive

The Jaguar XF is the brand’s executive saloon, first launched in 2008 (X250) and replaced by the X260 in 2015. It’s available with a wide range of engines: the 2.0‑litre Ingenium diesel and petrol, the 3.0‑litre supercharged V6 (AJ126), and the 5.0‑litre V8 in the XFR. The XF is renowned for its sharp handling, aluminium construction and sleek styling. In our data, the X260 generation accounts for 276 enquiries in 2025 [EM-VERIFIED], making it one of the most‑searched Jaguars.

Common issues vary by engine: the Ingenium diesel (204DTD) can suffer timing chain tensioner wear on pre‑2019 cars, while the AJ126 V6 is prone to cooling system leaks, particularly from the water pump and crossover pipes [PATTERN DATA]. The V8 AJ133 in the XFR has its own chain tensioner risk on early models. However, these are well‑understood problems with established repair routes, and independent specialists are plentiful. The XF’s running costs are modest for its class, with the Ingenium diesel achieving 50+ mpg on a motorway run. For a detailed breakdown, see the Ingenium failure‑rate report and the AJ126 data page.

XJ Deep Dive

The Jaguar XJ is the company’s flagship luxury saloon, with a lineage stretching back to 1968. The modern X350 (2003‑2009) and X351 (2010‑2019) are aluminium‑bodied, offering a supremely comfortable ride, a palatial interior and a range of powerful engines: the 3.0‑litre V6 diesel, the 3.0‑litre supercharged V6 (AJ126), and the 5.0‑litre supercharged V8 (AJ133) in Supersport and XJR forms. The X351 generation generated 151 enquiries in 2025 [EM-VERIFIED], reflecting its niche status.

The XJ’s major weak points are the timing chain tensioners on pre‑2013 V8s, a costly repair that can exceed £2,500 [EM-QUOTE]. The V6 diesel (276DT) also carries an oil pump housing failure risk, as detailed in our 276DT Oil Pump Failure page. Labour costs are higher due to the XJ’s more complex packaging, and parts are often more expensive. However, for those who value rear passenger space, a wafting ride and the prestige of a flagship, the XJ remains a compelling proposition. Full specs and reliability ratings are on the 276DT Knowledge Centre and the AJ133 Knowledge Centre.

Five‑Year Ownership Cost

The cost gap between the XF and XJ is significant. The XF’s lighter weight and more efficient engines (especially the Ingenium diesel) yield substantially better fuel economy - expect 45‑55 mpg in the diesel XF versus 30‑40 mpg in the V6 or V8 XJ. Insurance groups are lower for the XF, and servicing costs are reduced by simpler engine bay access and more common parts. Routine maintenance on the XJ, particularly the V8, can be 30‑50% more expensive due to labour time and component costs.

When a major failure occurs, the XF is also cheaper to fix. A reconditioned Ingenium diesel costs £3,500‑£5,800 [EM-QUOTE] versus £5,000‑£7,500 for the 276DT V6 diesel and £5,500‑£9,000 for the AJ133 V8 [EM-QUOTE]. Labour hours are lower on the four‑cylinder and even the V6 in the XF is less intrusive to work on than the XJ’s tightly‑packed engine bay. Over a typical 60,000‑mile ownership period, we estimate the XF saves the owner between £2,500 and £5,000 compared to the XJ, depending on engine choice and maintenance history. Which Should You Buy?

🚘

Everyday Commuter / Family Car

Choose the XF, ideally a post‑2019 Ingenium diesel or a V6 petrol. It offers plenty of space, excellent fuel economy in diesel form, and lower running costs than the XJ.

👑

Prestige / Chauffeur / Long‑Distance Cruiser

The XJ is hard to beat. Its rear legroom and ride comfort are superb, and the V8 Supersport delivers a majestic experience. Budget for higher running costs, but enjoy the flagship feel.

💰

Budget‑Conscious Buyer

XF, decisively. Lower purchase price, cheaper parts, wider specialist network, and a lower probability‑weighted repair bill over five years.

🏎️

Enthusiast / Performance Seeker

If you crave V8 power, the XFR or XJR are both fantastic. The XFR is lighter and more agile, while the XJR is a muscle limousine. Both are special, but the XFR is the more affordable entry.

Ultimately, the XF is the sensible, all‑round choice that doesn’t compromise on style or driving pleasure. The XJ is for those who prioritise luxury and presence over economy. For a deeper dive into the numbers, explore our Engine Failure Index 2026 and the Owner Decision Split data.

Which Is Easier and Cheaper to Replace When It Fails?

The XF wins this category hands‑down. Its smaller engine bay and more common engine configurations (the Ingenium diesel is shared with many other JLR models) mean that replacement engines are more readily available and cheaper. The XF’s suspension and drivetrain components are also shared with the XE and F‑Pace, keeping parts costs down.

The XJ, particularly with the V8, requires more specialist knowledge. The engine removal is more complex, and the V8 itself is less common in the used‑engine market. Labour hours for an engine swap on the XJ can be 20‑30% higher than on the XF. Even a routine job like a water pump replacement on the AJ133 V8 is more involved than on the AJ126 V6. For these reasons, we strongly recommend factoring in a higher contingency budget if you choose an XJ. The scrap‑value guide offers further insight on decision‑making.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the Jaguar XF more reliable than the XJ?

Generally, yes. The XF’s engines and components are more modern and widely used across the JLR range, making them better understood and easier to maintain. The XJ’s complexity, especially with the V8 and air suspension, introduces more potential failure points.

Which is cheaper to run, XF or XJ?

The XF is significantly cheaper to run, thanks to better fuel economy, lower insurance groups, and cheaper servicing and parts. The XJ’s larger engines and luxury‑oriented components push running costs higher.

What are the common problems with the Jaguar XF?

Common issues include cooling system leaks on the V6, timing chain tensioner wear on pre‑2019 Ingenium diesel, and occasional DPF problems on diesel models. Most are well‑documented and repairable by specialists.

What are the common problems with the Jaguar XJ?

Timing chain tensioner wear on pre‑2013 V8s, oil pump failure on the 2.7 diesel (276DT), and air suspension compressor failures are the most frequent. Repairs can be costly due to the complexity of the vehicle.

Which is better value for money, XF or XJ?

The XF offers better value for the vast majority of buyers. It provides 90% of the XJ’s luxury and driving experience for a significantly lower purchase price and running cost. The XJ is only better value if you specifically need the extra space, prestige, or V8 performance.
