F‑Pace vs E‑Pace – Which Should You Buy?

Two of Jaguar's most‑enquired SUVs, head to head. One is larger, more powerful, and designed for families. The other is smaller, more agile, and easier on the wallet. We break down the real‑world differences using 2025 UK enquiry data.

The F‑Pace is the better choice for most buyers – it offers more space, a wider engine range, and a more refined driving experience, with only a modest increase in running costs. The E‑Pace is the right choice if you prioritise urban agility, lower purchase price, and the smallest possible footprint.

  • F‑Pace X761: 220 enquiries in 2025, typical value £12,000–£38,000 [EM‑VERIFIED]
  • E‑Pace X540: 140 enquiries in 2025, typical value £12,000–£30,000 [EM‑VERIFIED]
  • Both share the Ingenium 2.0 diesel (AJ200D) and petrol (AJ200P) engines – the F‑Pace also offers the AJ126 3.0 V6 and AJ133 5.0 V8 in SVR trim
  • F‑Pace is heavier and larger – expect 5–10% higher fuel consumption and slightly higher servicing costs
  • Our verdict: F‑Pace for families, space, and refinement; E‑Pace for city driving and lower budgets

The Verdict

The F‑Pace is the stronger all‑rounder. It offers more cabin space, a larger boot, and a wider engine choice – all for a relatively modest increase in purchase price and running costs. The E‑Pace is a great car, but its smaller dimensions and less refined chassis make it feel more like a premium hatchback than a true SUV. If you value space, comfort, and the ability to carry passengers and luggage without compromise, the F‑Pace is the clear winner.

F‑Pace Deep Dive

The F‑Pace is Jaguar's mid‑size SUV, launched in 2016 as a direct rival to the BMW X3, Audi Q5 and Mercedes GLC. It's the brand's second‑best‑selling model in our 2025 enquiry data, with 220 requests [EM‑VERIFIED]. Its appeal lies in a spacious, well‑appointed cabin, a range of powerful engines (from the efficient 2.0 diesel to the supercharged 5.0 V8 in the SVR), and a surprisingly engaging drive for a large SUV.

The F‑Pace shares its platform with the XE and XF, but its taller ride height and longer wheelbase give it a commanding driving position and excellent all‑round visibility. It's also one of the best‑looking SUVs on the market – a fact that hasn't been lost on buyers. Full specs, known issues and reliability ratings are on the 204DTD Knowledge Centre, AJ126 Knowledge Centre and AJ133 Knowledge Centre.

E‑Pace Deep Dive

The E‑Pace is Jaguar's compact SUV, launched in 2017 as the entry‑point to the brand's SUV line‑up. It competes with the BMW X1, Audi Q3 and Volvo XC40. In our 2025 data, it generated 140 enquiries [EM‑VERIFIED] – a healthy number, but significantly behind the F‑Pace. The E‑Pace shares its architecture with the Land Rover Discovery Sport and uses the same Ingenium 2.0 diesel and petrol engines.

The E‑Pace is more agile in the city, easier to park, and costs less to run than the F‑Pace. However, its smaller interior means that rear passenger space is tight, and the boot is noticeably smaller. For many buyers, the E‑Pace feels like a stylish hatchback with a raised ride height – which is exactly what Jaguar intended. Full specs and known issues are on the 204DTD Knowledge Centre and 204PT Knowledge Centre.

Cost of Ownership Comparison

Over a typical three‑year ownership window, the E‑Pace is cheaper to run. Its lighter weight and smaller engine result in better real‑world fuel economy – expect 5–8% better mpg from the same 2.0 diesel. Servicing costs are also slightly lower due to smaller brakes and tyres. The F‑Pace's main cost advantage is its stronger residual values – particularly on the V6 and SVR models – which can offset the higher running costs. However, the E‑Pace's lower purchase price means it's the more affordable entry point.

Which Should You Buy? Three Scenarios

There's no single right answer – it depends on your priorities.

👨‍👩‍👧‍👦

Family User

Buy the F‑Pace. The extra rear legroom, wider cabin and larger boot (550 litres vs 430 litres) make a genuine difference for child seats, pushchairs, and weekend luggage. The more refined ride also keeps passengers happier on longer journeys.

🏙️

City Driver

Buy the E‑Pace. Its shorter length and tighter turning circle make it far easier to park and manoeuvre in urban environments. The higher driving position still gives good visibility, but the smaller footprint is a real advantage.

💼

Motorway Commuter / Towing

F‑Pace with diesel or V6. The F‑Pace's longer wheelbase and heavier weight give it a more planted feel on the motorway, and it can tow up to 2,500 kg – significantly more than the E‑Pace's 1,800 kg limit.

If you're still undecided, a test drive is essential – the difference in character between the two is immediately apparent.

Which Is Easier and Cheaper to Replace When It Fails?

Both models share the Ingenium 2.0 diesel and petrol engines, so the cost of replacing an engine is identical for the same engine code. The fitted cost of a reconditioned AJ200D / 204DTD is £4,150–£6,710, while the AJ200P / 204PT is £3,650–£5,910 [EM‑QUOTE]. The F‑Pace's optional V6 and V8 engines are more expensive – the AJ126 costs £5,280–£8,540 fitted, and the AJ133 is £6,410–£10,170.

Labour times are slightly longer on the F‑Pace due to the larger engine bay and the complexity of the V6/V8 layouts – allow an extra 1–2 hours. Both models are well‑supported by independent specialists, but the F‑Pace's higher sales volume means used engines and parts are slightly more available. Frequently Asked Questions

Which is more reliable, F‑Pace or E‑Pace?

Both share the same core Ingenium 2.0 engines and many electrical systems, so reliability is broadly similar. The E‑Pace's lighter construction and fewer high‑powered engine options give it a marginal reliability advantage, but well‑maintained examples of both are capable of 150,000+ miles. Always check service history for both.

Is the F‑Pace more expensive to maintain than the E‑Pace?

Yes – the F‑Pace is heavier and often equipped with larger wheels, tyres, and brakes, which increases consumable costs. Servicing costs are also marginally higher due to the larger engine bay and, on V6/V8 models, additional oil and filter capacity. Plan for an extra £100–£200 per year.

Which has better rear passenger space?

The F‑Pace is the clear winner. It offers genuine adult‑sized rear seats with good legroom and headroom, while the E‑Pace is more of a 2+2. If you regularly carry rear passengers, the F‑Pace is the right choice.

Can I fit a child seat in the E‑Pace?

Yes – the E‑Pace has Isofix mounts, but the rear cabin is noticeably tighter. A rear‑facing child seat will force the front seat forward significantly. For families, the F‑Pace is strongly recommended.

Which model is better for towing?

The F‑Pace tows up to 2,500 kg (braked) compared to the E‑Pace's 1,800 kg. The F‑Pace's heavier platform and optional V6/V8 engines also provide more stable towing performance.

Should I buy a V6 F‑Pace or stick with a 2.0?

The V6 is wonderful for enthusiasts – it's smooth, powerful, and characterful. However, it costs significantly more to run and buy. For most buyers, the 2.0 diesel offers the best balance of performance, economy, and value.
