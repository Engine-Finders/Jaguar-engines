AJD‑V6 Family (276DT / 306DT) - Full Jaguar Fitment Guide

Two V6 diesel engines sharing the same family heritage but very different displacement, character, and cost to replace. Exactly which Jaguar models got the 276DT 2.7 TDV6, which got the 306DT 3.0 SDV6, how they differ, and how to confirm which one your car actually has.

If your Jaguar is a diesel V6 badged 2.7D, 3.0D, TDV6 or SDV6, it will be one of two closely related but mechanically different engines: the 276DT 2.7 TDV6 or the 306DT 3.0 SDV6 - and the badge alone won't always tell you which one you're looking at.

  • The 276DT is a 2.7‑litre V6 diesel, used mainly in the XF X250 (2008–2011) and early XJ X351 (2010–2015) [PATTERN DATA]
  • The 306DT is a 3.0‑litre V6 diesel, introduced to replace the 2.7 and used in later XF X250 (2011–2015), XF X260 (2015–2024), XJ X351 (2015–2019), and F‑Pace X761 (2016–present)
  • Both are badged 3.0D on some model years, creating genuine confusion - some 3.0D badges are actually the 276DT, and some are the 306DT
  • The most reliable identification method is the engine number stamp, not the badge or even the listed displacement [PATTERN DATA]
  • Ordering a 306DT replacement for a 276DT is a costly mistake - fitment, ECU calibration and ancillaries differ enough to make them non‑interchangeable

276DT vs 306DT - What's the Difference?

Same V6 family, but the 306DT is more than just a capacity bump.

276DT (2.7 TDV6)

  • 2.7‑litre displacement
  • Twin‑turbo (parallel)
  • ~207–275 PS depending on tune
  • Used 2008–2015 across XF/XJ
  • Timing belt (requires scheduled replacement)
  • Oil pump housing a known weak point [PATTERN DATA]

306DT (3.0 SDV6)

  • 3.0‑litre displacement
  • Twin‑turbo (sequential)
  • ~300–350 PS depending on tune
  • Used 2011–2024 across XF/XJ/F‑Pace
  • Timing chain (no scheduled replacement)
  • Crankshaft bearing failure a known concern [PATTERN DATA]

The 306DT is a genuine evolution - not just a bored‑out 276DT but a different block casting, different turbo configuration, different timing system, and different ECU strategy. Parts compatibility between the two is minimal [EM-INTERVIEW].

Why This Matters When Buying a Replacement

The two engines are not interchangeable in any sense that matters to a buyer. Mounting points, ECU calibration, ancillary compatibility and even the physical dimensions differ enough that a supplier quoting for "an XF diesel engine" without confirming which architecture you actually have is quoting blind. Ordering a 306DT for a 276DT car means a return, a delivery delay, and in most cases a second shipping cost on top of the original - entirely avoidable by confirming the exact engine code before requesting a quote.

The replacement cost difference is also significant. A reconditioned 276DT supply‑only sits around £5,000–£7,500 [EM-QUOTE], while a 306DT is typically £6,500–£9,500 [EM-QUOTE] - a difference of up to £2,000 before labour. That alone makes identification worth the effort.

Common Misidentifications

The specific wrong answers people arrive at, and why.

  • Assuming "3.0D" means 306DT. Early XF X250 and some XJ X351 cars wore a 3.0D badge but actually had the 2.7‑litre 276DT - the badge was a marketing decision, not a technical one.
  • Assuming the year alone tells you. Changeover was staggered: XF moved to 306DT from 2011, but XJ didn't move until 2015. There is no single year where every model switched.
  • Ordering a 306DT replacement for a 276DT car. This is the costliest version of this mistake - the two engines are not interchangeable, and a wrong‑engine order means a return, a delay, and a second shipping cost.
  • Confusing 276DT with 224DT. 224DT is a 2.2‑litre four‑cylinder Duratorq, not a V6. The 276DT is a V6 - completely different engine family.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the 306DT just a larger 276DT?

No. They share a V6 layout and the AJD‑V6 family name, but they are different block castings. The 306DT has a different bore/stroke, different turbocharger configuration (sequential vs parallel), a timing chain instead of a belt, and different ECU calibration. Parts are not interchangeable.

How do I know if my XF has a 276DT or a 306DT?

Check the engine number stamp. As a rule of thumb: pre‑2011 XF X250 with the 2.7D badge is a 276DT; post‑2011 facelift XF with the 3.0D or SDV6 badge is a 306DT. But always verify - some early cars are mis‑badged. Use our Registration Lookup tool for certainty.

Which engine is more reliable, the 276DT or the 306DT?

Both have their own documented issues. The 276DT is known for oil pump housing failure (which can kill the engine), while the 306DT has a reputation for crankshaft bearing fatigue, particularly on pre‑2015 units [PATTERN DATA]. Neither is immune, but the 306DT is generally considered the more durable of the two when properly maintained - though it costs significantly more to replace when it does fail.

Did the F‑Pace ever get the 276DT?

No. The F‑Pace was launched in 2016 with the 306DT 3.0 SDV6 as its diesel V6 option. All diesel V6 F‑Paces are 306DT. Some later models switched to the AJ20D6 straight‑six from around 2020.

Can I swap a 306DT into a 276DT car?

Technically possible but economically unviable. The engines have different mounting points, different ECU and wiring harnesses, different cooling system routing, and different gearbox maps. A full conversion would cost more than buying a car with the correct engine already fitted. If your 276DT has failed, replace it with the same engine code.
