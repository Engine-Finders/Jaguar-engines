AJ‑V8 Lineage (AJ26 → AJ27 → AJ34 → AJ133) — Full Jaguar Fitment Guide

Four generations of Jaguar V8, from the early 4.0‑litre Nikasil‑susceptible AJ26 to the ultimate 5.0‑litre supercharged AJ133. Exactly which models got which engine, how to tell them apart, and why the distinction matters when buying a replacement.

Jaguar's AJ‑V8 family spans four distinct engine codes – AJ26, AJ27, AJ34 and AJ133 – each with different displacements, revisions, and known issues.

  • AJ26 – 4.0‑litre V8 (1996–2002), early Nikasil cylinder bore issues, found in XK8/XKR, XJ8, and early S‑Type
  • AJ27 – 4.0‑litre V8 (1999–2003), improved block material and timing chain tensioners, largely replaced AJ26
  • AJ34 – 4.2‑litre V8 (2002–2010), available naturally aspirated and supercharged, used in XK, XJ, S‑Type R, and early XF
  • AJ133 – 5.0‑litre V8 (2009–2024), the final and most powerful evolution, with supercharged variants up to 575 PS, used in XF, XJ, XK, F‑Type, and F‑Pace SVR
  • The engine code is stamped on the block – the badge (e.g., 4.2, 5.0, SC) is not always reliable for identification [PATTERN DATA]
  • Ordering a replacement for the wrong generation is an expensive mistake – mounting, electronics, and ancillaries differ significantly

AJ‑V8 Lineage — What Changed

From the early 4.0‑litre to the ultimate 5.0‑litre supercharged, each generation addressed the weaknesses of its predecessor.

AJ26 (4.0 L)

  • Nikasil cylinder liners – prone to bore wear with high‑sulphur fuel
  • Timing chain tensioners weak – failure can be catastrophic
  • Used 1996–2002 in XK, XJ, S‑Type
  • Replacement cost (used) ~£2,500–£4,500 [ESTIMATED]

AJ27 (4.0 L)

  • Steel liners replaced Nikasil – much more durable
  • Revised timing chain tensioners (still a known wear point)
  • Used 1999–2003, largely superseding AJ26
  • Replacement cost similar to AJ26 but more available

AJ34 (4.2 L)

  • Increased displacement and improved cylinder head flow
  • Supercharged variant used in S‑Type R, XKR, XJR
  • Timing chain tensioners still a concern, but less so than AJ26/27
  • Used 2002–2010 across XK, XJ, S‑Type, early XF

AJ133 (5.0 L)

  • All‑new 5.0‑litre, very different from earlier designs
  • Pre‑2013 units had timing chain tensioner weakness – revised in 2013
  • Supercharged versions up to 575 PS (SVR, Project 8)
  • Used 2009–2024 in XF, XJ, XK, F‑Type, F‑Pace SVR

The AJ133 is not just a bigger AJ34 – it is a different engine design, sharing only the family name. Parts compatibility between generations is minimal [EM-INTERVIEW].

AJ133 has a distinctive plastic intake manifold and different cam covers compared to the earlier engines. Supercharger placement also differs – AJ133 supercharger is side‑mounted, while AJ34 is top‑mounted.

Why This Matters When Buying a Replacement

The four AJ‑V8 generations are not interchangeable. Engine mounts, ECU pin‑outs, cooling system routing, gearbox bellhousing patterns, and even physical length differ. A supplier quoting for "a Jaguar V8" without confirming the exact code is quoting blind.

Costs vary significantly: a reconditioned AJ133 supply‑only runs £5,500–£9,000 [EM-QUOTE], while an AJ34 is typically £3,500–£6,000 [ESTIMATED]. Fitting labour is also model‑dependent – an F‑Type engine swap is more involved than an XJ. Always confirm the code before agreeing to a quote.

Common Misidentifications

The specific wrong answers people arrive at, and why.

  • Assuming "4.2 SC" means AJ133. The 4.2‑litre is AJ34 – AJ133 is always 5.0‑litre. Displacement is the simplest clue.
  • Assuming year alone tells you. XK (X150) used AJ34 until 2010 and AJ133 from 2010 – the same model year can have either engine depending on build date.
  • Ordering an AJ133 for an AJ34 car. They physically do not fit – different mounts, different ancillaries, different ECU. This is a costly return.
  • Confusing AJ26 with AJ27. Both are 4.0‑litre, but AJ27 has steel liners – using AJ26 parts on an AJ27 or vice‑versa is risky. Always check the engine number prefix.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I tell the difference between AJ26 and AJ27?

Both are 4.0‑litre, but AJ27 has steel cylinder liners (AJ26 has Nikasil). The engine number prefix is the only definitive way – it will start with AJ26‑ or AJ27‑. Visually, they are nearly identical. If your car is pre‑1999, it's almost certainly AJ26; post‑1999, it could be either.

Is the AJ133 just a bored‑out AJ34?

No. The AJ133 is a completely different engine design – new block, new heads, new timing system, new everything. They share only the "AJ" family name. Parts are not interchangeable.

Which AJ‑V8 is the most reliable?

The AJ133 (post‑2013) is generally considered the most robust, with the timing chain tensioner revision fixing the main weakness. AJ27 is also reliable but older. AJ26 and early AJ34 have more known issues [PATTERN DATA].

Can I put an AJ133 into an XK that had an AJ34?

Technically possible but expensive – you'd need new engine mounts, a different ECU, wiring loom, cooling system, and possibly gearbox. It's almost always cheaper to buy a car that already has the AJ133.

What is the cost to replace an AJ133?

A reconditioned AJ133 supply‑only is around £5,500–£9,000 [EM-QUOTE], plus fitting labour (typically 12–18 hours, ~£780–£1,170 at indie rates). Main dealer costs are significantly higher.
