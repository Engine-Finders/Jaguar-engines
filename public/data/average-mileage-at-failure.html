Methodology

How we derived these average mileage figures and what they represent.

Data sources: We combine three proprietary channels: (1) 24,650 quote requests from EnginesMarket.co.uk and EngineFinders.co.uk in 2025 — of which 4,625 are Jaguar-specific; (2) direct interviews with 100+ UK engine specialists who rebuild these engines daily; (3) post‑sale customer surveys 3‑6 months after replacement. The mileage-at-failure figures are drawn from the Known Failure Patterns table, which is triangulated from these sources and cross‑referenced with manufacturer TSBs and public forum data.

What counts as "failure": We define failure as a mechanical or electrical issue that requires significant repair or full engine replacement. Minor wear items (e.g., sensors, belts) are not included unless they led to a major failure.

Limitations: Mileage ranges are indicative — individual outcomes vary widely based on service history, driving style, and model-specific factors. The ranges reflect the most common failure windows observed in our data, not a guarantee of failure at those miles.

Refresh cycle: Annually, each February, covering the prior full calendar year.

What This Means For Owners

The data shows that most Jaguar engines reach failure between 60,000 and 120,000 miles. This isn't a design flaw — it's the point at which many components (timing chains, water pumps, turbos) naturally wear out. The key is preventative maintenance: for example, replacing the AJ126 water pump before 60k can avoid a catastrophic coolant loss. For the AJ133, early chain tensioner replacements on pre-2013 cars are well-documented. The 276DT tends to fail later, but when it does, the oil pump failure is often terminal — so proactive inspection at 80k is advised.

Enquiry volumes aren't failure rates — they reflect vehicle parc size and age. The AJ200D leads enquiries (590), but that's partly because it's fitted to the high-volume XE, XF, and F-Pace. Always check your specific engine’s Knowledge Centre for tailored advice.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the average mileage at which Jaguar engines fail?

Most Jaguar engines, including the AJ126, AJ133, and Ingenium diesels, typically fail between 60,000 and 120,000 miles. The 276DT 2.7 TDV6 tends to fail slightly later, around 80,000–140,000 miles, while the 224DT 2.2 Duratorq can reach 150,000 miles before major issues.

Which Jaguar engine fails at the lowest mileage?

The AJ126 and AJ133 both have known cooling system and timing chain issues that can appear as early as 60,000 miles, especially on pre‑2013 AJ133 units. The Ingenium diesel (AJ200D) also suffers tensioner failures in that window. However, proper maintenance can push failure much further.

Is higher mileage always a sign of imminent failure?

No — mileage is just one factor. Service history, driving style, and model-specific quirks matter more. A well‑maintained AJ126 with 120,000 miles may be healthier than a neglected one at 70,000. Always get a professional inspection.
