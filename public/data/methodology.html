Methodology

What this data is, what it isn't, and how we built it.

Source: Internal CRM quote-request data from EnginesMarket.co.uk and EngineFinders.co.uk, combined. Full calendar year 2025. 24,650 unique quote requests across all brands we cover; 4,625 of these relate specifically to Jaguar engines [EM-VERIFIED].

Triangulation: We don't rely on enquiry data alone. Our Known Failure Patterns table is triangulated from three proprietary channels:

  • Enquiry descriptions — free-text problem statements submitted with quote requests
  • Supplier quote notes — mechanics listing specific failures when quoting
  • Post-sale customer surveys — 100+ customers 3‑6 months after replacement [EM-SURVEY]

These are then cross-referenced with manufacturer TSBs (Tier 1) and public forums / Honest John / Parkers (Tier 2) for validation [THIRD-PARTY].

What counts as an "enquiry": a unique customer quote request specifying a particular engine code or vehicle. Repeat requests from the same customer for the same engine are not double-counted.

What counts as a "failure": a mechanical or electrical issue requiring significant repair or full engine replacement. Minor wear items (e.g., sensors, belts) are not included unless they led directly to a major failure.

Data integrity correction: The original "Top 300" model ranking table in our proprietary data contained a duplicated, decrementing block. We used Section 4's chassis-coded table as ground truth for model-level figures to ensure accuracy [EM-VERIFIED].

Refresh cycle: Annually, each February, covering the prior full calendar year. Figures are locked at time of publication and are not silently revised — any correction is logged on our public Corrections Log rather than edited away.

Limitations — What This Data Cannot Tell You

Our dataset reflects people who contacted us about a replacement engine — it is not a random sample of every Jaguar on UK roads. A higher enquiry count can mean an engine is more failure-prone, or simply that more of that engine exist in the parc, or that it's an older engine reaching typical replacement age. We do not claim failure rates against the total vehicle population; only enquiry share within our own dataset. Request volume ≠ failure rate.

What Changed Since Last Year

This is our first published edition — 2025 is the baseline year. Every future report, published each February, will compare the previous year's enquiry volumes, failure patterns and cost data against this baseline. We will track:

  • Absolute volume changes for each engine code
  • Model‑level shifts (e.g., XF vs. XE enquiry share)
  • Regional trends (as captured in our regional data)
  • Cost evolution (reconditioned supply and labour rates)
  • Owner decision splits (repair vs replace vs scrap)

Initial observations from the 2025 data suggest that the AJ200D Ingenium diesel is the most frequently enquired‑about engine, consistent with its widespread fitment across XE, XF and F‑Pace. The petrol Ingenium (AJ200P) is also strong, reflecting growing interest in petrol alternatives. We expect the AJ126 V6 to remain a steady performer given its presence in older but popular models like the XF X250 and XJ.

What This Means For Jaguar Owners

The enquiry trend data is a practical tool for owners and prospective buyers. A high enquiry count for an engine doesn't necessarily mean it's "bad" — it often means it's common. However, when combined with our failure rate data and mileage‑at‑failure figures, you can build a realistic picture of what to expect.

If you're considering a used Jaguar, pay attention to the engines with moderate enquiry volumes but known catastrophic failures — e.g., the 276DT (oil pump) or the early 204DTD (timing chain). Use our Repair vs Replace Calculator to assess the financial risk for your specific vehicle.

We'll update this page annually. Bookmark it to stay informed about shifting demand patterns — they often precede changes in parts availability and specialist pricing.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the Jaguar Engine Failure Index?

It's an annual report built from real 2025 UK enquiry data (24,650 quote requests) and specialist interviews. It tracks which Jaguar engines generate the most replacement enquiries, at what mileage, and at what cost — with full transparency on limitations.

How is the data collected?

We use internal CRM data from EnginesMarket.co.uk and EngineFinders.co.uk, combined with direct interviews with 100+ UK engine specialists and post-sale customer surveys. All figures are tagged with their source (e.g., [EM-VERIFIED], [EM-INTERVIEW]).

What does [EM-VERIFIED] mean?

It means the figure comes directly from our internal CRM quote-request data (24,650 enquiries in 2025) and is exact — not an estimate. We use this badge for enquiry volumes, model requests and condition splits.

Does a high enquiry count mean an engine is unreliable?

Not on its own. Enquiry volume reflects how many vehicles are on the road with that engine as much as it reflects failure frequency. We pair enquiry data with our separate Known Failure Patterns research to distinguish popularity from reliability.

How often is this page updated?

Annually, each February, covering the prior full calendar year. This is our first published edition and establishes the baseline every future report will be measured against.
