Failure Rate in Context

How the AJ6 and AJ16 compare to other Jaguar engines - and why they're different.

The AJ6 (Advanced Jaguar 6‑cylinder) and its later evolution, the AJ16, are among Jaguar's most celebrated engines. Introduced in 1984, the AJ6 replaced the ageing XK6 straight‑six and brought modern engineering to Jaguar's core powerplant. The AJ16, introduced in 1994, was the final evolution of the design, featuring improved breathing, electronic ignition, and OBD‑II compliance.

Unlike the more modern engines in our 2025 dataset - such as the 204DTD (590 enquiries), AJ126 (560), and 204PT (500) - the AJ6 and AJ16 are classic engines. They are no longer in production, and their primary presence today is in the classic car market. As a result, they do not appear in our modern enquiry data, which focuses on cars still on the road as daily drivers.

Mileage‑at‑Failure Distribution

At what mileage do AJ6 and AJ16 owners typically report problems?

Based on our [PATTERN DATA] from specialist interviews and classic car owner reports, the AJ6 and AJ16 show a mileage‑related pattern that reflects their age and usage profile. Unlike modern engines that fail due to specific design weaknesses, the AJ6 and AJ16 typically suffer from age‑related wear and maintenance neglect.

  • 80k–120k miles (Early): Distributor cap and rotor wear, spark plug leads degrading, and cooling system corrosion (radiator, water pump, hoses) start to appear. Regular servicing catches these.
  • 120k–160k miles (Mid): Camshaft wear becomes more common, particularly on engines that have not had regular oil changes. Timing chain tensioner wear can also appear.
  • 160k–200k+ miles (Late): Cylinder head gasket failure is more frequent if cooling system has been neglected. Full engine rebuilds are often sought at this mileage to restore performance.

Importantly, many AJ6 and AJ16 engines have exceeded 200,000 miles with nothing more than routine maintenance. The engine's cast‑iron block and robust internals make it one of the most durable Jaguar engines ever produced.

Camshaft wear is the most significant long‑term concern on AJ6 and AJ16 engines. It is typically caused by high mileage and infrequent oil changes. A regular oil change schedule (every 3,000–5,000 miles) with a high‑quality 20W‑50 classic oil is essential to prevent this.

Cooling system corrosion is the other major issue. The AJ6 and AJ16 use a conventional cast‑iron block and aluminium head, which can suffer from electrolytic corrosion if the coolant is not changed regularly. Using a high‑quality antifreeze and changing it every 2–3 years is critical.

Timing chain tensioner wear is less common but can occur on higher‑mileage engines. The chain itself is robust, but the tensioner can wear, leading to a rattling noise on startup.

How Long They Actually Last

Real‑world lifespan of the AJ6 and AJ16 - and what makes the difference.

With proper maintenance, the AJ6 and AJ16 are capable of 200,000–300,000 miles of reliable service [EM-INTERVIEW]. Many examples on UK roads have exceeded this, particularly those in the XJ and XJS that have been well‑cared for by enthusiasts.

The key to longevity is regular oil changes at 3,000–5,000 mile intervals using a high‑quality classic oil (20W‑50) that meets the correct specifications. Cooling system maintenance is equally important - changing the coolant every 2–3 years and using the correct antifreeze mixture prevents electrolytic corrosion.

For buyers looking at a used AJ6 or AJ16, the most important factor is service history. A well‑maintained example with documented oil changes and cooling system work will outlast a neglected engine by a significant margin. Camshaft wear can be checked by listening for a ticking noise from the top of the engine - any unusual top‑end noise should be investigated by a specialist.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the AJ6 engine, and which Jaguar models use it?

The AJ6 (Advanced Jaguar 6‑cylinder) is a 3.6‑litre or 4.0‑litre straight‑six engine introduced in 1984. It powers the XJ (XJ40), XJS, and early XK. The AJ16 is the final evolution, introduced in 1994 with improved electronics and emissions control.

Is the AJ6 or AJ16 a reliable engine?

Yes, they are considered among the most reliable Jaguar engines ever built. With a cast‑iron block and robust design, they are capable of 200,000+ miles when properly maintained. Regular oil changes and cooling system maintenance are essential.

What are the signs of camshaft wear on an AJ6 or AJ16?

The most common sign is a ticking or tapping noise from the top of the engine, which may become louder with engine speed. This is caused by worn cam lobes or followers. If you hear this, have a specialist inspect the engine promptly.

How much does it cost to rebuild an AJ6 or AJ16 engine?

A full rebuild typically costs £2,500–£5,000 at an independent specialist, depending on the extent of the work. A top‑end rebuild (camshafts, followers, head gasket) is cheaper, while a full bottom‑end rebuild is more expensive.

What is the typical lifespan of an AJ6 or AJ16 engine?

With proper care, many AJ6 and AJ16 engines exceed 200,000 miles. Some well‑maintained examples have reached 300,000 miles. Regular oil changes and cooling system maintenance are the key to achieving high mileage.
