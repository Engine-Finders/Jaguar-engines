Methodology

What this data is, what it isn't, and how we built it.

Source: Internal CRM quote-request data from EnginesMarket.co.uk and EngineFinders.co.uk, combined. Full calendar year 2025. 24,650 unique quote requests across all brands we cover; 4,625 of these relate specifically to Jaguar engines.

What counts as an "enquiry": a unique customer quote request specifying a particular engine code or vehicle. Repeat requests from the same customer for the same engine are not double-counted.

What this data cannot tell you: our dataset reflects people who contacted us about a replacement engine — it is not a random sample of every Jaguar on UK roads. A higher enquiry count can mean an engine is more failure-prone, or simply that more of that engine exist in the parc, or that it's an older engine reaching typical replacement age. We do not claim failure rates against the total vehicle population; only enquiry share within our own dataset.

Refresh cycle: annually, each February, covering the prior full calendar year. Figures are locked at time of publication and are not silently revised — any correction is logged on our public Corrections Log rather than edited away.

What This Means For Jaguar Owners

The two most-enquired engines, AJ200D (Ingenium diesel) and AJ126 3.0 V6 SC, are not necessarily the two "worst" engines Jaguar has built — they are simply the two most common ones reaching typical replacement mileage across a large vehicle parc [PATTERN DATA]. What's more informative is the R-to-V column: almost every engine on this list clears our 60% safety threshold, meaning replacement is financially sound on the typical host vehicle. The two flagged ⚠️ Watch — 276DT and 224DT — are engines most often fitted to older, lower-value hosts (X-Type, early XF), where the calculation is closer and worth checking individually using our Repair vs Replace Calculator.

It's also worth reading rank order alongside the underlying engine population rather than in isolation. The AJ126, for example, generates 560 enquiries in 2025 but is fitted to a wide range of models (XF, XJ, F-Pace, F-Type) — its absolute enquiry volume reflects both its popularity and its presence across the range. For the full breakdown behind any single figure on this page, see AJ126 Failure Rate, Average Mileage at Failure and Most Enquired Models.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which Jaguar engine is the most reliable?

Based on our 2025 enquiry data, the AJ200P / 204PT (Ingenium petrol) generates the third-lowest enquiry volume relative to its vehicle parc, and its known failure patterns (cooling system, water pump) are generally less catastrophic than the diesel Ingenium's timing chain issues. The AJ133 5.0 V8 post-2013 is also considered robust.

Does a high enquiry count mean an engine is unreliable?

Not on its own. Enquiry volume reflects how many vehicles are on the road with that engine as much as it reflects failure frequency. We pair enquiry data with our separate Known Failure Patterns research to distinguish popularity from reliability.

Is this data representative of all Jaguars on UK roads?

No, and we want to be upfront about that. Our dataset reflects people who contacted us about a replacement engine, which naturally over-represents engines that fail and under-represents ones that don't. Full methodology and limitations are set out in this report.

How often is this report updated?

Annually, each February, covering the prior full calendar year. This is our first published edition and establishes the baseline every future report will be measured against.
