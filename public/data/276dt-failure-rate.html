Mileage‑at‑Failure Distribution

At what mileage do 276DT owners typically report problems?

Based on our [PATTERN DATA] from specialist interviews and owner surveys, the 276DT shows a clear mileage‑related pattern. The majority of enquiries and reported failures fall into three bands:

  • 80k–100k miles (Early): Some owners report a cold‑start knock or rattle, which can be an early indicator of bearing wear. If caught here, a bottom‑end rebuild may be possible.
  • 100k–120k miles (Mid): This is the most common failure window. Oil pump failure becomes more frequent, and turbo issues also start to appear.
  • 120k–140k miles (Late): Catastrophic oil pump failure is most frequent in this band. The engine may seize without warning.

Importantly, the 276DT's oil pump failure is often sudden and without extensive prior warning - unlike timing chain issues that give a rattling noise for weeks. This makes regular oil pressure testing and early diagnosis critical. Many specialists recommend an oil pump inspection at 80k miles to catch any degradation.

The oil pump housing failure is the most critical and costly. It is caused by a weak point in the pump housing at the tensioner mount, which can fail and cause a loss of oil pressure. A cold‑start knock is the classic early warning - ignoring it can lead to a seized engine and a full replacement. Our full oil pump failure guide explains the diagnosis and repair process in detail.

Turbocharger failure and EGR issues are less catastrophic but still expensive. Regular servicing with the correct oil specification and avoiding excessive idling can help extend turbo life.

How Long They Actually Last

Real‑world lifespan of the 276DT - and what makes the difference.

With proper maintenance, the 276DT is capable of 150,000–200,000 miles of reliable service [EM-INTERVIEW]. Many examples on UK roads have exceeded this, particularly those in Jaguar saloons that are predominantly motorway‑driven.

The key to longevity is oil changes at 5,000–6,000 mile intervals (significantly shorter than the manufacturer's extended schedule), using a high‑quality oil that meets JLR spec. Frequent short trips and towing heavy loads (more common in Land Rover applications) increase oil pump stress, so Jaguar owners may have a slight advantage here.

Early detection of oil pump wear is crucial. Specialists recommend an oil pressure test at 80k miles to check for any degradation in the pump housing. A simple sump inspection can also reveal the condition of the oil pump and tensioner.

Our Verdict

A balanced assessment of the 276DT's reliability and value.

Reliability rating: 2.5 / 5 (provisional)

The 276DT is a torquey V6 diesel that delivers strong performance in the XF and XJ. However, its reputation is seriously damaged by the oil pump housing failure, which can be sudden and expensive. With proactive maintenance - particularly shortened oil change intervals and early oil pressure testing - it can still provide a long, trouble‑free life, but owners must be vigilant.

Our advice: If you're buying used, prioritise a car with a documented service history and evidence of regular oil changes. Consider having an oil pressure test performed as part of the pre‑purchase inspection. If you already own a 276DT, budget for an oil pump inspection at 80k miles and be alert to any cold‑start knocking.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the 276DT engine, and which Jaguar models use it?

The 276DT is a 2.7‑litre V6 diesel engine, also known as the TDV6. It appears in the Jaguar XF (X250), XJ (X350, X358), and previously in the X‑Type. It is also widely used in Land Rovers such as the Discovery 3 and Range Rover Sport.

Is the 276DT a reliable engine?

It has a reputation for oil pump housing failure, which is a well‑known weak point. With diligent maintenance - especially frequent oil changes and early oil pressure testing - many examples exceed 150,000 miles. However, the failure risk is real and can be sudden.

What are the signs of oil pump failure on a 276DT?

The most common early warning is a knocking or rattling noise from the engine on cold start, which may fade as the engine warms up. As wear progresses, the knock becomes louder and more persistent, and oil pressure may drop. Catastrophic failure can occur without much warning.

How much does it cost to replace a 276DT engine?

A reconditioned engine supply costs £5,000–£7,500, with fitting labour adding £910–£1,170 at independent rates. Main dealer costs are significantly higher. An oil pump replacement may be cheaper if caught early, but full replacement is often the only option once the pump fails catastrophically.

What is the typical lifespan of a 276DT engine?

With proper care, many 276DT engines exceed 150,000 miles. However, the oil pump failure risk increases after 80,000 miles, so proactive monitoring is essential. Some well‑maintained examples have reached 200,000 miles.
