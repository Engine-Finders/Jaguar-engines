P0016 Jaguar - Crankshaft / Camshaft Position Correlation

A code that signals the engine's crankshaft and camshaft are out of sync. On Jaguar petrol and diesel engines, this often points to a stretched timing chain, a faulty variable valve timing (VVT) solenoid, or a worn tensioner. Ignoring it risks catastrophic engine damage.

P0016 means the engine control unit (ECU) has detected that the crankshaft and camshaft positions are no longer synchronised within the acceptable tolerance. On most Jaguar engines - from the Ingenium AJ200D to the AJ133 V8 - this is typically caused by timing chain stretch, a failed tensioner, or a variable valve timing (VVT) solenoid malfunction. The fix can range from a £200 solenoid replacement to a £2,500+ timing chain overhaul.

  • Most common real cause: a worn timing chain tensioner or stretched chain - typically £1,200–£2,500 fitted [PATTERN DATA]
  • Cheapest fix: a faulty VVT solenoid - £200–£400 fitted [PATTERN DATA]
  • Commonly seen alongside cold-start rattle, engine management light, and limp mode
  • Often appears with P0011 or P0016 on the same bank - the combination helps identify which side is at fault

Do not ignore P0016. If the timing jumps, pistons and valves can collide, writing off the engine. Have it diagnosed immediately.

What P0016 Actually Means

In a modern Jaguar engine, the crankshaft position sensor and camshaft position sensor work together to tell the ECU exactly where each piston is in its stroke and which valves are open. The ECU uses this data to control fuel injection, ignition timing, and variable valve timing. P0016 is triggered when the ECU detects that the camshaft position (relative to the crankshaft) has drifted beyond the allowed range - typically more than a few degrees. This can happen because the timing chain has stretched, a tensioner has lost pressure, or a VVT actuator has stuck. The code is often engine‑specific: on V6 and V8 engines, you may see P0016 for bank 1 and P0017 for bank 2. On four‑cylinder Ingenium diesels and 204PT petrols, a single P0016 typically indicates the intake camshaft on the single bank. The underlying cause is almost always mechanical, not electrical - which makes this a code that demands immediate attention.

Symptoms You'll Notice Alongside P0016

  • Engine management light illuminated (usually amber or red)
  • Cold‑start rattle - a metallic clatter that lasts a few seconds on startup
  • Rough idle, especially when cold - see misfire / rough idle
  • Noticeable power loss or limp mode, particularly under acceleration
  • Increased fuel consumption and reduced engine response
  • In severe cases, the engine may stall or fail to start

If you hear a rattle on startup and see the engine light, stop driving and arrange a diagnostic check immediately.

Diagnostic Order - Cheapest‑First

A competent Jaguar specialist will follow this order to avoid misdiagnosing an expensive timing chain job.

1

Check Oil Level & Pressure

Low oil can cause VVT solenoids to malfunction. Top up and clear codes; if the code returns, proceed.

2

Inspect VVT Solenoid

Remove and test the solenoid for sticking. Clean or replace; this resolves a significant number of cases.

3

Live‑Data Cam / Crank Offset

Using diagnostic software, compare the camshaft position to the crankshaft. A deviation of more than 5° indicates mechanical wear.

4

Check Timing Chain Tensioner

If the offset is excessive, the tensioner may have lost pressure; often requires chain cover removal for inspection.

5

Inspect Chain & Guides

If wear is confirmed, the timing chain, guides, and tensioner should be replaced as a kit. This is the most involved repair.

Can You Still Drive Your Jaguar with P0016?

No - or only to get it directly to a garage. P0016 indicates a potential mechanical mismatch between the crankshaft and camshaft. If the timing chain has stretched or a tensioner has failed, continuing to drive risks the chain jumping teeth, which can cause pistons to strike valves - a catastrophic failure that often requires a full engine replacement. Even if the cause is a faulty VVT solenoid, driving with the code can cause the ECU to run in a reduced‑power mode, increasing fuel consumption and potentially damaging the catalytic converter. The only safe action is to have the car recovered to a specialist for diagnosis. If you absolutely must drive, keep it to a short, low‑speed journey of no more than a few miles, and listen for any unusual rattles.

Related Fault Codes

P0016 is often accompanied by other codes. The combination helps pinpoint the root cause.

P0011 - Intake Camshaft Timing Over‑Advanced

If both P0016 and P0011 appear, it strongly suggests a VVT solenoid or oil pressure issue on the intake side.

P0017 - Crankshaft / Camshaft Position Correlation (Bank 1 Sensor B)

This is the equivalent for the exhaust camshaft on V6/V8 engines; both can appear if the timing is severely off.

P0340 - Camshaft Position Sensor Circuit

An electrical fault with the sensor itself, which can cause intermittent P0016 codes.

Cold‑Start Rattle

Not a fault code, but a key symptom that often precedes P0016; see our symptom guide for more detail.

Frequently Asked Questions About P0016

What does P0016 mean on a Jaguar?

It means the ECU has detected that the crankshaft and camshaft are out of sync by more than a few degrees. This is often due to a stretched timing chain, a failed tensioner, or a faulty VVT solenoid.

Can I drive with P0016 on my Jaguar?

No - or only to get it directly to a garage. Driving with this code risks the timing chain jumping, which can cause pistons to strike valves and destroy the engine. Have the car recovered.

Is P0016 a serious fault?

Yes, it's considered a serious fault. While it may be caused by a simple VVT solenoid, it could also indicate imminent timing chain failure. Immediate diagnosis is essential.

How much does it cost to fix P0016 on a Jaguar?

The cost ranges from about £200 for a VVT solenoid replacement to £2,500–£3,500 for a full timing chain kit replacement. Diagnostic time is typically 1–2 hours.

Will clearing the code fix P0016?

Only temporarily. The code will return if the underlying mechanical issue persists, and clearing it destroys freeze‑frame data that helps diagnosis.
