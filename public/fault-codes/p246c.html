P246C Jaguar - DPF Restriction - Ash Accumulation

This code indicates the diesel particulate filter (DPF) has accumulated excessive ash that cannot be cleared by regeneration. On Jaguar diesel engines - particularly the 2.0 Ingenium AJ200D and 2.2 Duratorq - this means the DPF is nearing the end of its service life and will need professional cleaning or replacement.

P246C means the ECU has detected that the DPF is restricted due to ash accumulation beyond the level that regeneration can clear. Unlike soot, ash is a by‑product of regeneration itself and builds up over the life of the filter. On Jaguar diesels, this code typically appears after 100,000–150,000 miles and signals that the DPF requires professional cleaning or replacement. The fix can range from a £200–£400 chemical cleaning to a £1,200–£2,200 DPF replacement fitted [PATTERN DATA].

  • Most common cause: normal ash accumulation at high mileage - typically 100k+ miles
  • Worst case: DPF physically damaged or beyond cleaning - requires replacement: £1,200–£2,200 fitted [PATTERN DATA]
  • Often accompanied by DPF warning light, limp mode, and reduced power
  • Frequently appears alongside P2463 (soot accumulation) or P2002 (efficiency below threshold)

Ignoring P246C will eventually lead to a complete DPF blockage, requiring replacement. A forced regeneration will not fix this - the ash is not combustible.

What P246C Actually Means

The diesel particulate filter traps soot from the exhaust. During regeneration, the ECU raises exhaust temperatures to burn the soot to ash. While soot is combustible, ash is not - it is a mineral residue that accumulates permanently. Over time, ash fills the DPF, reducing its capacity and increasing exhaust backpressure. P246C is triggered when the ECU calculates that the ash load has exceeded a threshold, typically based on mileage, pressure sensor readings, or a model-based estimate. On Jaguar engines, this code is common on higher‑mileage vehicles, especially those used predominantly for short urban trips that never allow the DPF to reach regeneration temperatures, accelerating ash buildup. Unlike a soot‑related code like P2463, P246C indicates that the filter is physically worn out and regeneration will not restore it. The only effective solutions are professional cleaning (which can remove some ash) or a complete DPF replacement.

The code is often engine‑specific: on four‑cylinder Ingenium diesels, P246C typically points to the single DPF. On V6 engines (though rare in Jaguar), it may appear with bank‑specific variants. The underlying cause is almost always age or mileage‑related, making it essential to assess the DPF's physical condition before deciding on a repair.

Symptoms You'll Notice Alongside P246C

  • DPF warning light illuminated (amber, often accompanied by a message like "DPF Full")
  • Engine management light often appears as well
  • Limp mode - reduced power and restricted revs, especially under load
  • Noticeable loss of acceleration and increased fuel consumption
  • Excessive black smoke from the exhaust, particularly when accelerating
  • In severe cases, the engine may struggle to start or stall
  • Frequent failed regeneration attempts - you may notice a strong smell of diesel or a hot exhaust after unsuccessful regens

If you see the DPF light and the car is in limp mode, avoid driving any further and arrange a diagnostic check immediately.

Diagnostic Order - Cheapest‑First

A competent Jaguar specialist will follow this order to confirm whether the DPF is truly at end‑of‑life or if a simpler fix is possible.

1

Scan for Fault Codes

Read all codes to confirm P246C and check for others like P2463, P244B, or P2002 to understand the full picture.

2

Live‑Data: Soot & Ash Load

Using diagnostic software, read the DPF soot and ash load percentages. If ash load is above 80–90%, the DPF is likely at end‑of‑life.

3

Check Differential Pressure Sensor

Compare live pressure readings to expected values. A faulty sensor can give false ash load readings; test and replace if needed.

4

Attempt a Forced Regeneration

If soot load is high, a forced regen may clear soot, but if ash load is the culprit, the regen will fail or not reduce the ash percentage. This step confirms whether the problem is soot or ash.

5

Inspect DPF Internally (Bore Scope)

A physical inspection can confirm ash accumulation or damage. If ash is excessive or the filter is damaged, replacement or professional cleaning is required.

Can You Still Drive Your Jaguar with P246C?

It depends on severity - but generally, only to get it directly to a garage. If the DPF light is on but the car is not in limp mode, you may be able to drive it for a short distance, but you should avoid any extended journeys. Continued driving with a DPF restricted by ash will cause increasing backpressure, which can damage the turbocharger and lead to a full DPF replacement. If the car is in limp mode, do not drive it - have it recovered. Even if you attempt a forced regeneration, it will not remove ash, so the code will return. The only safe course is to have the car inspected by a specialist who can assess the ash load and recommend cleaning or replacement. If you must drive, limit it to a few miles at low speed, and watch for any warning lights or performance changes.

Remember: a forced regeneration will not fix P246C - it only addresses soot. Ash is permanent and requires mechanical removal or a new filter.

Related Fault Codes

P246C is often accompanied by other codes. The combination helps pinpoint the root cause.

P2463 - DPF Soot Accumulation

This code indicates the DPF is full of soot and needs a regeneration. If both P2463 and P246C appear, it suggests both soot and ash are an issue.

P244B - DPF Differential Pressure Too High

This code is triggered when pressure difference is excessive, often due to blockage. It can appear alongside P246C when the DPF is severely restricted.

P2002 - DPF Efficiency Below Threshold

Indicates the DPF is not filtering particles effectively, which can be caused by ash accumulation or physical damage.

Frequently Asked Questions About P246C

What does P246C mean on a Jaguar?

It means the DPF has accumulated excessive ash that cannot be removed by regeneration. The filter is nearing the end of its service life and requires professional cleaning or replacement.

Can I drive my Jaguar with P246C?

Only if the car is not in limp mode - and even then, only to get it directly to a garage. If it's in limp mode, stop driving immediately to avoid costly DPF or turbocharger damage.

Is P246C a serious fault?

Yes, it's serious. While it may be resolved with a DPF clean, if left ignored, it will lead to a full replacement costing over £1,200–£2,200.

How much does it cost to fix P246C on a Jaguar?

The cost ranges from about £200–£400 for professional cleaning to £1,200–£2,200 for a new DPF fitted. Diagnostic time is typically 1–2 hours.

Will a forced regeneration fix P246C?

No - forced regeneration only burns off soot, not ash. If the code is triggered by ash accumulation, a regen will not clear it.

What causes P246C on a Jaguar diesel?

The most common cause is normal ash accumulation at high mileage (typically over 100k miles). Short trips that prevent proper regeneration can accelerate this.
