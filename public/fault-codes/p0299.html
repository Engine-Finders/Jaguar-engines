P0299 Jaguar - Turbocharger Underboost

A code that signals the turbocharger is not delivering enough boost pressure. On Jaguar diesel engines, this often points to a boost leak, a stuck wastegate, or a failing turbo actuator. Ignoring it can lead to poor performance and eventual turbo failure.

P0299 means the engine control unit (ECU) has detected that the actual turbocharger boost pressure is significantly below the commanded target. On most Jaguar diesel engines - from the Ingenium AJ200D to the 3.0 TDV6 - this is typically caused by a boost leak, a faulty turbo actuator, or a stuck wastegate. The fix can range from a £50 hose replacement to a £1,500+ turbocharger overhaul.

  • Most common real cause: a split boost hose or loose intercooler pipe - typically £50–£200 fitted [PATTERN DATA]
  • Worst case: turbocharger failure requiring replacement - £1,200–£2,500 fitted [PATTERN DATA]
  • Commonly seen alongside limp mode, engine management light, and turbo whistle
  • Often appears with P0234 or P0299 on the same bank - the combination helps identify the root cause

Do not ignore P0299. Reduced boost can cause the engine to run rich, which can damage the catalytic converter and DPF.

What P0299 Actually Means

In a modern Jaguar diesel with a turbocharger, the ECU uses a boost pressure sensor (MAP sensor) to monitor the air pressure in the intake manifold. The ECU calculates a target boost pressure based on engine speed, load, and other factors. P0299 is triggered when the actual boost pressure is more than a certain percentage below the target for a set period of time. This code indicates an underboost condition - the turbo isn't producing enough pressure. This can happen because of a leak in the intake or intercooler system, a faulty boost control solenoid, a stuck wastegate, or internal turbo damage. The code is common on Ingenium diesel engines in the XE and XF, as well as the 3.0 TDV6 in the XF and F-Pace. The underlying cause is almost always related to the boost system, making it a code that demands systematic diagnosis.

Symptoms You'll Notice Alongside P0299

  • Engine management light illuminated (usually amber or red)
  • Noticeable power loss or limp mode, especially under acceleration
  • Poor throttle response - the car feels sluggish
  • Turbo whistle or hissing noise from the engine bay
  • Increased fuel consumption due to the engine running rich to compensate
  • In severe cases, excessive black smoke from the exhaust

If you experience a loss of power and hear a hissing sound, stop driving and arrange a diagnostic check immediately.

Diagnostic Order - Cheapest‑First

A competent Jaguar specialist will follow this order to avoid misdiagnosing an expensive turbocharger replacement.

1

Visual Inspection of Boost Hoses

Check for splits, cracks, or loose connections on the intake and intercooler pipes. A simple hose clamp or replacement is often the fix.

2

Check Turbo Actuator & Solenoid

Test the actuator with a vacuum pump or diagnostic tool. A sticking solenoid can be cleaned or replaced cheaply.

3

Test Boost Pressure Sensor

Compare MAP sensor readings to actual manifold pressure. A faulty sensor can cause the ECU to think boost is low when it's not.

4

Inspect Wastegate Operation

Check if the wastegate is stuck open or partially open. This can be caused by carbon buildup or a failed actuator.

5

Turbocharger Health Check

If all other causes are ruled out, the turbo itself may be damaged. A specialist will check for shaft play, damaged blades, or oil leaks.

Can You Still Drive Your Jaguar with P0299?

Conditionally, yes - but only to get it directly to a garage. P0299 is not an immediate engine-destroying fault like low oil pressure. However, driving with an underboost condition can cause the engine to run rich, which can clog the DPF and damage the catalytic converter over time. More critically, if the cause is a failing turbo, continued driving can lead to catastrophic turbo failure, sending debris into the engine. The safe approach is to avoid heavy acceleration and long journeys. If the car has entered limp mode, it's unsafe for motorway driving due to lack of power. Have it recovered or drive it gently to a specialist.

Related Fault Codes

P0299 is often accompanied by other codes. The combination helps pinpoint the root cause.

P0234 - Turbocharger Overboost

The mirror image. If both P0299 and P0234 appear, suspect a stuck wastegate or a faulty boost control solenoid.

P0235 - Turbocharger Boost Sensor Circuit

An electrical fault with the MAP or boost pressure sensor, which can cause intermittent P0299 codes.

P0401 - Exhaust Gas Recirculation (EGR) Flow Insufficient

A stuck EGR valve can reduce turbo response; see our EGR valve failure guide for more detail.

Turbo Whistle

Not a fault code, but a key symptom that often precedes P0299; see our symptom guide for more detail.

Frequently Asked Questions About P0299

What does P0299 mean on a Jaguar?

It means the ECU has detected that the turbocharger is not producing enough boost pressure. This is often due to a boost leak, a faulty turbo actuator, or a stuck wastegate.

Can I drive with P0299 on my Jaguar?

Conditionally, yes - but only to get it directly to a garage. Avoid heavy acceleration and long journeys, as driving with an underboost condition can damage the DPF and catalytic converter. If the car is in limp mode, have it recovered.

Is P0299 a serious fault?

It can be. While it may be caused by a simple split hose, it could also indicate imminent turbocharger failure. Immediate diagnosis is recommended.

How much does it cost to fix P0299 on a Jaguar?

The cost ranges from about £50 for a boost hose replacement to £1,200–£2,500 for a full turbocharger replacement. Diagnostic time is typically 1–2 hours.

Will clearing the code fix P0299?

Only temporarily. The code will return as soon as the ECU detects the boost pressure discrepancy again. Clearing it also destroys freeze‑frame data that helps diagnosis.
