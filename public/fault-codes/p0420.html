P0420 Jaguar — Catalyst System Efficiency Below Threshold

This code indicates the engine control unit (ECU) has detected that the catalytic converter is not reducing emissions efficiently. On Jaguar petrol engines — particularly the AJ126 3.0 V6 and AJ133 5.0 V8 — this can be caused by a failing catalyst, a faulty oxygen sensor, or an exhaust leak. Ignoring it may lead to failed MOT emissions tests and further damage.

P0420 means the ECU has detected that the catalytic converter's efficiency is below the required threshold — typically because the oxygen sensors after the catalyst are showing similar readings to the ones before it, indicating the catalyst isn't cleaning the exhaust properly. On Jaguar petrol engines, this is often due to a degraded catalytic converter (especially on higher‑mileage cars), a faulty downstream oxygen sensor, or an exhaust leak. The fix can range from a £200–£400 sensor replacement to a £1,200–£2,500 catalytic converter replacement [PATTERN DATA].

  • Most common cause: a failed or degraded catalytic converter — typically £1,200–£2,500 fitted (aftermarket) [PATTERN DATA]
  • Cheaper possibility: faulty downstream oxygen sensor — £200–£400 fitted [PATTERN DATA]
  • Often accompanied by engine management light, a smell of rotten eggs, and reduced fuel economy
  • Can appear alongside P0133 (O2 sensor slow response) or P0420 itself

Do not ignore P0420. A failing catalytic converter can lead to a failed MOT and, if left unchecked, may cause the catalyst to break apart and block the exhaust.

What P0420 Actually Means

The catalytic converter is part of the exhaust system that converts harmful gases (carbon monoxide, hydrocarbons, and nitrogen oxides) into less harmful substances. The ECU monitors the converter's efficiency using two oxygen sensors — one before the catalyst (upstream) and one after it (downstream). Under normal conditions, the downstream sensor should show a much steadier signal than the upstream one because the catalyst stores oxygen and smooths out fluctuations. P0420 is triggered when the downstream sensor's signal resembles the upstream sensor's — meaning the catalyst is not storing oxygen effectively and emissions are not being cleaned. On Jaguar petrol engines, this code is common on higher‑mileage vehicles, especially those with AJ126 V6 and AJ133 V8 engines. The root cause is often a deteriorated catalyst, but it can also be a faulty oxygen sensor, an exhaust leak, or even an engine misfire that has contaminated the catalyst. Diagnosing the cause correctly is crucial to avoid replacing the expensive catalytic converter unnecessarily.

Symptoms You'll Notice Alongside P0420

  • Engine management light illuminated (amber)
  • Rotten egg smell from the exhaust — a sign of sulfur compounds in the catalytic converter
  • Reduced fuel economy — the engine may run richer to compensate
  • Noticeable loss of power, especially under acceleration
  • Failed MOT emissions test — the car will not pass the exhaust emissions part
  • In severe cases, a rattling noise from the exhaust (if the catalyst substrate has broken apart)
  • Increased exhaust backpressure, which can affect turbocharger performance on supercharged V8s

If you smell rotten eggs or hear a rattle, have the exhaust system inspected immediately to avoid a blocked catalyst.

Diagnostic Order — Cheapest‑First

A competent Jaguar specialist will follow this order to avoid misdiagnosing a catalytic converter when a sensor or leak is the real issue.

1

Scan for All Codes

Read all codes to confirm P0420 and check for others like P0133 (O2 sensor slow) or misfire codes that could have damaged the catalyst.

2

Inspect for Exhaust Leaks

Check the exhaust system, especially around the oxygen sensor bungs and joints, for any leaks that could affect sensor readings.

3

Live‑Data Oxygen Sensor Readings

Using diagnostic software, compare the upstream and downstream O2 sensor signals. If the downstream signal mirrors the upstream, the catalyst is likely failing. If the downstream signal is slow or erratic, the sensor may be faulty.

4

Check Fuel Trims and Engine Operation

Verify the engine is running correctly (no misfires, correct fuel trims). A rich/lean condition can cause false P0420 codes or damage the catalyst.

5

Test the Catalytic Converter

If the sensors and engine are healthy, the catalyst is likely the issue. A backpressure test or temperature comparison (inlet vs outlet) can confirm internal blockage or efficiency loss.

Can You Still Drive Your Jaguar with P0420?

Yes, but with caution — and only for a limited time. P0420 does not typically cause immediate drivability issues, but it will cause a failed MOT emissions test. Driving with a failing catalytic converter can lead to increased fuel consumption and, if the catalyst breaks apart internally, it can block the exhaust and cause a significant loss of power. In severe cases, a blocked exhaust can lead to engine overheating or damage to the turbochargers (on supercharged models). It's safe to drive to a garage for diagnosis, but you should avoid extended journeys. If you notice a rattling noise from the exhaust or a strong smell of rotten eggs, stop driving and have the car recovered. The code will also illuminate the engine management light, which may affect your MOT readiness.

Related Fault Codes

P0420 is often accompanied by other codes. The combination helps pinpoint the root cause.

P0133 — O2 Sensor Circuit Slow Response (Bank 1 Sensor 1)

A slow upstream O2 sensor can cause incorrect fuel trims, which may damage the catalyst and trigger P0420.

P0135 — O2 Sensor Heater Circuit (Bank 1 Sensor 1)

A faulty heater circuit can cause the sensor to read incorrectly, potentially leading to a false P0420 code.

P0300 — Random/Multiple Cylinder Misfire

Misfires can dump unburnt fuel into the exhaust, overheating and destroying the catalytic converter.

AJ126 Cooling System Failure

While not directly related, cooling issues can affect engine performance and emissions, indirectly contributing to catalyst degradation.

Frequently Asked Questions About P0420

What does P0420 mean on a Jaguar?

It means the catalytic converter is not reducing emissions efficiently, either because the catalyst has degraded, an oxygen sensor is faulty, or there's an exhaust leak.

Can I drive my Jaguar with P0420?

Yes, for a limited time, but it will fail an MOT emissions test and may lead to further damage if the catalyst breaks apart. Have it diagnosed promptly.

Is P0420 a serious fault?

It can be — a failed catalyst will cost over £1,000 to replace. However, it's often caused by a cheaper sensor or leak, so proper diagnosis is essential.

How much does it cost to fix P0420 on a Jaguar?

The cost ranges from about £200 for a sensor replacement to £1,200–£2,500 for an aftermarket catalytic converter. Diagnostic time is typically 1–2 hours.

Will an O2 sensor spacer fix P0420?

No — spacers may temporarily suppress the code but are not a fix and will cause an MOT failure. They do not address the underlying issue and are not recommended.

Can a bad MAF sensor cause P0420?

Indirectly, yes — a faulty MAF can cause incorrect air/fuel mixture, which can damage the catalyst over time. However, P0420 is usually a catalyst or O2 sensor issue.
