P0562 Jaguar — System Voltage Low

A code that signals the Engine Control Module (ECM) has detected the system voltage is below the minimum operating threshold. On Jaguar models, this often points to a failing battery, faulty alternator, or a parasitic drain. Ignoring it can lead to hard starting, stalling, and damage to sensitive electronics.

P0562 means the vehicle's electrical system voltage has dropped below 10 volts for a prolonged period. On most Jaguar models — from the Ingenium AJ200D to the AJ133 V8 — this is typically caused by a failing battery, a faulty alternator, or a parasitic drain. The fix can range from a £100 battery replacement to a £400+ alternator and battery replacement.

What P0562 Actually Means

P0562 is a generic OBD-II code that stands for "System Voltage Low." It is set when the Engine Control Module (ECM) detects that the vehicle's system voltage (typically the battery voltage) has dropped below 10 volts for a certain period. On Jaguar vehicles, the electrical system relies on a stable voltage supply for all modules — from the ECM to the transmission control module, infotainment, and lighting. When voltage drops too low, the ECM may shut down non-essential systems to preserve power, leading to reduced performance, hard starting, or even stalling. The most common causes are an ageing battery, a failing alternator, or a parasitic drain that discharges the battery overnight. Unlike mechanical fault codes, P0562 is an electrical issue that can affect multiple systems, making it essential to address promptly before it leads to more expensive repairs.

Symptoms You'll Notice Alongside P0562

  • Battery warning light illuminated on the dashboard (red battery icon)
  • Engine management light illuminated (amber)
  • Slow cranking when starting the engine, especially on cold mornings
  • Engine may stall, particularly at idle or when electrical loads are high (lights, heated seats)
  • Dimming headlights or flickering interior lights
  • Electrical accessories (windows, locks, radio) may operate slowly or intermittently
  • In severe cases, the engine may fail to start at all

If you notice the battery light and the engine stalls, do not restart repeatedly — you may damage the starter motor. Have the car recovered.

Diagnostic Order — Cheapest‑First

A competent Jaguar specialist will follow this order to avoid misdiagnosing an expensive alternator when a simple battery or connection issue is at fault.

1

Check Battery Connections

Inspect the battery terminals for corrosion or looseness. Clean with a wire brush and tighten securely. A simple and free fix.

2

Test Battery Voltage

Measure the battery voltage with the engine off. A reading below 12.2V indicates a discharged or failing battery. Jump-start and retest.

3

Test Alternator Output

With the engine running, measure voltage across the battery terminals. Should be 13.8–14.5V. If below 13V, the alternator is likely faulty.

4

Perform Parasitic Draw Test

With the vehicle off, measure current draw. Anything above 50mA indicates a drain; isolate circuits by pulling fuses to find the culprit.

5

Replace Battery / Alternator as Needed

If the battery is over 4 years old, replace it. If the alternator fails, replace it along with the belt and tensioner if worn.

Can You Still Drive Your Jaguar with P0562?

Only to get directly to a garage — and avoid using electrical accessories. P0562 indicates low system voltage, which means the battery is not being charged or is severely depleted. If the alternator is failing, the vehicle is running entirely on battery power, and once that runs out, the engine will stall and leave you stranded. Driving with low voltage can also cause erratic behaviour from the engine, transmission, and ABS modules, potentially leading to unsafe conditions. If the battery is simply old and flat, you may be able to jump-start the car, but it will likely die again. The safest action is to have the car recovered or replaced with a known-good battery. If you must drive, switch off all non-essential electrical loads (radio, heated seats, lights if possible), and head directly to a garage. Do not drive if the battery light is flashing or the engine is stalling.

Related Fault Codes

P0562 is often accompanied by other voltage-related codes. The combination helps pinpoint the root cause.

P0563 — System Voltage High

If the voltage is too high, it often indicates a faulty voltage regulator or alternator overcharging the system.

P0622 — Generator Field Control Circuit Malfunction

This code points to a problem with the alternator's field control circuit, often causing undercharging or overcharging.

Battery Warning Light

Not a fault code, but a key warning that often accompanies P0562; see our warning light guide for more detail.

Frequently Asked Questions About P0562

What does P0562 mean on a Jaguar?

It means the Engine Control Module has detected the system voltage is below 10 volts, typically due to a failing battery, faulty alternator, or parasitic drain.

Can I drive with P0562 on my Jaguar?

Only to get directly to a garage, and avoid using electrical accessories. Driving with low voltage can cause stalling and leave you stranded. Have the car recovered if possible.

Is P0562 a serious fault?

Yes — it can leave you stranded and cause damage to the starter motor and other electrical components if ignored. Immediate diagnosis is recommended.

How much does it cost to fix P0562 on a Jaguar?

The cost ranges from about £100 for a battery replacement to £400–£800 for an alternator and battery replacement. Diagnostic time is typically 1 hour.

Will clearing the code fix P0562?

Only temporarily. The code will return if the underlying voltage issue persists, and clearing it removes freeze‑frame data that helps diagnosis.

Can a bad battery cause P0562?

Yes — an old or failing battery is the most common cause of P0562. If the battery is over 4 years old, it should be tested and likely replaced.
