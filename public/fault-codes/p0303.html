P0303 Jaguar - Cylinder 3 Misfire

This code indicates the engine control unit (ECU) has detected a misfire on Cylinder 3. On Jaguar engines - from the 2.0 Ingenium AJ200D to the AJ126 V6 and AJ133 V8 - this is a specific warning that often points to a faulty ignition coil, spark plug, fuel injector, or compression issue on that cylinder.

P0303 means the ECU has detected a misfire specifically on Cylinder 3. On most Jaguar petrol and diesel engines - including the Ingenium AJ200D, AJ126 V6, and AJ133 V8 - this is typically caused by a faulty ignition coil, a worn spark plug, a clogged fuel injector, or a compression issue on that cylinder. The fix can range from a £100–£300 coil or plug replacement to £400–£800 for injector or compression repairs [PATTERN DATA].

  • Most common cause: a faulty ignition coil or spark plug on Cylinder 3 - typically £150–£350 fitted [PATTERN DATA]
  • Worst case: low compression or fuel injector failure - £400–£1,500+ [PATTERN DATA]
  • Often accompanied by rough idle, loss of power, and the engine management light
  • May appear with P0300 (random misfire) or P0263 (contribution fault)

Do not ignore P0303. A misfire on Cylinder 3 can damage the catalytic converter and lead to expensive repairs if left unchecked.

What P0303 Actually Means

Misfire occurs when the air‑fuel mixture in a cylinder fails to ignite properly, or when combustion is incomplete. The ECU monitors the crankshaft speed to detect misfires - each cylinder firing causes a slight acceleration; if that acceleration is missing or reduced on Cylinder 3, the ECU logs a misfire for that cylinder. On Jaguar engines, P0303 is the specific code for Cylinder 3. On four‑cylinder Ingenium diesels, Cylinder 3 is one of the four; on V6 and V8 engines, Cylinder 3 is typically on Bank 1 (the same bank as Cylinder 1). The most common causes are a faulty ignition coil (on petrol engines), a worn spark plug, a clogged or faulty fuel injector, or a compression issue. Unlike a random misfire code (P0300), P0303 gives you a specific location to start diagnostics, which can save time and money.

Symptoms You'll Notice Alongside P0303

  • Engine management light illuminated (amber, often flashing during severe misfires)
  • Rough idle, especially when cold - see misfire / rough idle
  • Loss of power and hesitation under acceleration - see limp mode
  • Increased fuel consumption and a rough engine note
  • In severe cases, the engine may stall or have a noticeable miss at idle
  • Unburnt fuel smell from the exhaust, or black smoke on petrol engines - see black smoke

If the engine light is flashing, avoid driving and have the car recovered to prevent catalytic converter damage.

Diagnostic Order - Cheapest‑First

A competent Jaguar specialist will follow this order to avoid misdiagnosing an expensive repair when a simple coil or spark plug is the real issue.

1

Scan for All Codes

Read all codes to confirm P0303 and check for others like P0300, P0203 (injector circuit), or P0171 (lean) that could indicate the root cause.

2

Swap Ignition Coil or Spark Plug

For a quick test, swap the Cylinder 3 ignition coil with Cylinder 1 (or spark plug). If the misfire moves to the other cylinder, the coil or plug is faulty.

3

Check Fuel Injector (Cylinder 3)

If the coil and plug are good, the injector may be at fault. A balance test or swapping injectors can confirm.

4

Check for Vacuum Leaks

Using a smoke tester or propane, check the intake manifold and hoses for leaks that could affect Cylinder 3.

5

Perform Compression Test (Cylinder 3)

If the above checks are normal, a compression test can reveal mechanical issues like worn piston rings or valve problems.

Can You Still Drive Your Jaguar with P0303?

It depends - but generally, only for a short distance. P0303 indicates a misfire on Cylinder 3 that can cause unburnt fuel to enter the exhaust system, which can overheat and destroy the catalytic converter. If the engine light is flashing, the misfire is severe enough to cause damage - stop driving immediately and have the car recovered. If the light is solid and the engine runs reasonably smoothly, you may be able to drive to a garage, but you should avoid heavy acceleration and extended journeys. Continued driving with a misfire can also lead to further engine damage, especially if the cause is low compression or a timing issue. The safest course is to have the car diagnosed as soon as possible.

Related Fault Codes

P0303 is often accompanied by other codes. The combination helps pinpoint the root cause.

P0300 - Random / Multiple Cylinder Misfire

If both P0300 and P0303 appear, it suggests misfires are occurring on Cylinder 3 and possibly other cylinders.

P0203 - Injector Circuit Malfunction (Cylinder 3)

If an injector circuit code appears alongside P0303, it suggests a fuel delivery issue on Cylinder 3.

P0171 - System Too Lean (Bank 1)

A lean condition can cause misfires; if both appear, check for vacuum leaks or MAF issues.

Frequently Asked Questions About P0303

What does P0303 mean on a Jaguar?

It means the ECU has detected a misfire specifically on Cylinder 3, indicating a fuel, ignition, or mechanical issue on that cylinder.

Can I drive my Jaguar with P0303?

Only for a short distance if the light is solid; if flashing, stop driving immediately to avoid catalytic converter damage.

Is P0303 a serious fault?

It can be - a misfire can damage the catalytic converter and lead to expensive repairs. Prompt diagnosis is essential.

How much does it cost to fix P0303 on a Jaguar?

The cost ranges from about £100 for spark plugs to £400 for a coil, and up to £1,500+ for compression repairs. Diagnostic time is typically 1–2 hours.

Can a bad fuel injector cause P0303?

Yes, a clogged or faulty injector on Cylinder 3 can cause a misfire, especially on diesel engines.

Will clearing the code fix P0303?

Only temporarily - if the underlying issue is not fixed, the code will return. Clearing it without diagnosis is not a solution.
