U0100 Jaguar — Lost Communication With ECM/PCM

A code that signals the Engine Control Module (ECM) or Powertrain Control Module (PCM) has lost communication with another module on the vehicle’s CAN bus network. On Jaguar models, this often points to a wiring fault, a failing module, or a blown fuse. Ignoring it can lead to no-start conditions, erratic behaviour, or complete vehicle shutdown.

U0100 means the vehicle’s Engine Control Module (ECM) or Powertrain Control Module (PCM) has lost communication with another module on the CAN bus. On most Jaguar models — from the Ingenium AJ200D to the AJ133 V8 — this is typically caused by a wiring fault, corroded connector, blown fuse, or a failed module. The fix can range from a £50 fuse or relay replacement to a £500+ module replacement or wiring repair.

  • Most common real cause: a blown fuse or corroded connector — typically £50–£150 fitted [PATTERN DATA]
  • Worst case: failed ECM/PCM or damaged CAN bus wiring — £400–£1,200 fitted [PATTERN DATA]
  • Commonly seen alongside engine management light, no-start, and limp mode
  • Often appears with other U-codes (e.g., U0101, U0102) — the combination helps identify which module is offline

Do not ignore U0100. A loss of communication can leave you stranded or cause intermittent faults that are hard to trace. Immediate diagnosis is recommended.

What U0100 Actually Means

In a modern Jaguar, multiple electronic modules communicate over a Controller Area Network (CAN) bus. The Engine Control Module (ECM) — also called the Powertrain Control Module (PCM) — is the master controller for engine functions. U0100 is a generic OBD-II code that indicates the ECM/PCM has lost communication with another module on the CAN bus. This could be the Transmission Control Module (TCM), Anti-lock Brake System (ABS), Body Control Module (BCM), or any other module. The code is set when the ECM does not receive a message from a specific module for a certain period. The underlying cause is almost always electrical — a wiring break, poor ground, blown fuse, or a failed module. Unlike mechanical fault codes, U0100 is a network issue, and it can affect multiple systems, causing a cascade of warning lights and loss of functions.

Symptoms You'll Notice Alongside U0100

  • Engine management light illuminated (amber or red)
  • Engine may crank but not start, or may start and then stall
  • Loss of power, limp mode, or reduced engine performance
  • Transmission shifting issues (if TCM is affected)
  • Instrument cluster gauges (speedo, tacho) may freeze or drop to zero
  • Electrical accessories (windows, locks, lights) may behave erratically
  • Intermittent stalls or sudden loss of power while driving

If the engine dies while driving, the vehicle may be unsafe to continue. Pull over safely and arrange recovery.

Diagnostic Order — Cheapest‑First

A competent Jaguar specialist will follow this order to avoid misdiagnosing a costly module replacement when a simple fix will do.

1

Check Fuses & Relays

Inspect all fuses and relays related to the ECM and other modules. Replace any blown fuses; a blown fuse may indicate a short.

2

Inspect Wiring & Connectors

Visually check the ECM connectors and CAN bus wiring harness for corrosion, chafing, or loose pins. Clean with contact cleaner and reseat.

3

Check Battery Voltage & Grounds

Measure battery voltage and check main engine and chassis grounds. Low voltage or poor grounds can cause communication errors.

4

Perform CAN Bus Test

Using a diagnostic tool or oscilloscope, check the CAN bus for proper voltage and signal integrity. Look for shorts or opens.

5

Isolate the Faulty Module

If the bus is healthy, use a scan tool to identify which module is not responding. Then test that module's power, ground, and communication lines.

Can You Still Drive Your Jaguar with U0100?

Only to get directly to a garage — and only if the engine is running. U0100 indicates a loss of communication between the ECM and another module. If the ECM itself is not receiving power or is faulty, the engine may not start at all. If the engine does start, you may experience stalling, reduced power, or transmission issues. Driving with an intermittent communication fault can be dangerous — the engine could cut out without warning. The safest action is to have the car recovered to a specialist. If you must drive, keep it to a short, low-speed journey and be prepared for sudden loss of power. Do not drive if the engine management light is flashing or the vehicle is in limp mode with reduced power.

Related Fault Codes

U0100 is often accompanied by other U-codes. The combination helps pinpoint which module is offline.

U0101 — Lost Communication with TCM

If U0100 is accompanied by U0101, the Transmission Control Module is likely offline, causing shifting issues.

U0102 — Lost Communication with Transfer Case Control Module

This may appear on AWD Jaguar models if the transfer case module fails to communicate.

U0121 — Lost Communication with ABS Control Module

A common companion code; if the ABS module is offline, the vehicle may lose stability control and braking aids.

Won't Start / No Crank

Not a fault code, but a key symptom that often accompanies U0100; see our symptom guide for more detail.

Frequently Asked Questions About U0100

What does U0100 mean on a Jaguar?

It means the Engine Control Module (ECM) has lost communication with another module on the CAN bus network. This could be due to wiring issues, a faulty module, or a blown fuse.

Can I drive with U0100 on my Jaguar?

Only to get directly to a garage, and only if the engine runs. Driving with this code risks sudden stalling or loss of power. Have the car recovered if possible.

Is U0100 a serious fault?

Yes, it's a serious electrical fault. It can cause the engine to stop running or affect other critical systems like transmission and ABS. Immediate diagnosis is essential.

How much does it cost to fix U0100 on a Jaguar?

The cost ranges from about £50 for a fuse or connector repair to £400–£1,200 for a module replacement and programming. Diagnostic time is typically 1–2 hours.

Will clearing the code fix U0100?

Only temporarily. The code will return if the underlying communication issue persists, and clearing it removes freeze‑frame data that helps diagnosis.

Can a low battery cause U0100?

Yes — a low battery or poor ground can cause voltage drops that disrupt CAN bus communication, triggering U0100. Always check battery health first.
