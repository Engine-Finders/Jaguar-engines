P164B Jaguar - Reductant Quality Performance

A code that signals the reductant (AdBlue/DEF) quality has degraded below the acceptable threshold, affecting NOx conversion efficiency. On Jaguar diesel engines, this often points to contaminated AdBlue, a faulty NOx sensor, or a failing dosing module. Ignoring it can lead to reduced engine performance, increased emissions, and eventual limp mode.

P164B means the Engine Control Module (ECM) has detected that the quality of the reductant (AdBlue) is below the required standard for effective NOx reduction. On most Jaguar diesel models - particularly those with the Ingenium AJ200D - this is typically caused by poor-quality AdBlue, contamination, or a faulty NOx sensor. The fix can range from a £100 AdBlue flush and refill to a £600+ sensor or dosing module replacement.

Do not ignore P164B. A degraded reductant system can lead to failed MOTs, reduced fuel economy, and costly exhaust system damage.

What P164B Actually Means

P164B is a manufacturer-specific code for Jaguar (and some other brands) that indicates the reductant quality performance has fallen below the required threshold. The Selective Catalytic Reduction (SCR) system uses AdBlue (a urea solution) to reduce nitrogen oxide (NOx) emissions. The ECM continuously monitors the quality of the reductant via the NOx sensors and temperature sensors in the exhaust system. When the ECM detects that the reductant is no longer effective-either because it is contaminated, diluted, or of poor quality-it sets P164B. This code is particularly common on Ingenium diesel engines fitted to models like the XE, XF, and F-Pace. The underlying cause is almost always fluid-related, but it can also be triggered by a faulty NOx sensor that misreads the exhaust gas composition. Unlike mechanical faults, this is an emissions system issue that, if left unaddressed, can lead to restricted performance and a no-start condition after a countdown timer expires.

Symptoms You'll Notice Alongside P164B

  • Engine management light illuminated (amber or red)
  • AdBlue warning light or message on the instrument cluster
  • Limp mode - reduced power and restricted RPM
  • Increased fuel consumption and a noticeable drop in performance
  • Excessive white smoke from the exhaust, especially under acceleration
  • A strong ammonia smell from the exhaust (indicative of poor NOx conversion)
  • In severe cases, a countdown timer may appear, and if not addressed, the engine may fail to restart

If you see the AdBlue warning or a countdown message, do not ignore it - the vehicle may become undrivable once the timer expires.

Diagnostic Order - Cheapest‑First

A competent Jaguar specialist will follow this order to avoid misdiagnosing a costly sensor or module replacement when a simple fluid change will do.

1

Check AdBlue Level & Quality

Ensure the AdBlue tank is not empty. Test the fluid quality using a refractometer; contamination or dilution is a common cause.

2

Perform AdBlue System Reset & Relearn

After topping up with quality AdBlue, reset the SCR system using a diagnostic tool and perform a forced regeneration of the NOx sensors.

3

Inspect AdBlue Dosing Module & Lines

Check for leaks, blockages, or crystallization in the dosing injector and supply lines. Clean or replace as needed.

4

Test NOx Sensors

Using live data, monitor the NOx sensor readings before and after the SCR. If values are outside expected ranges, the sensor may be faulty.

5

Check SCR Catalyst

If all else fails, inspect the SCR catalyst for physical damage or blockage. This is the most invasive and expensive step.

Can You Still Drive Your Jaguar with P164B?

Yes, but with caution - and only if the countdown timer hasn't expired. P164B indicates a quality issue with the reductant, which affects emissions but does not immediately damage the engine. However, the vehicle may enter a derated mode (limp mode) to reduce NOx output, limiting speed and acceleration. More critically, if the system detects that the issue persists, a countdown timer (usually 500 miles) may appear, after which the engine will refuse to start. Driving with this code may also cause the SCR catalyst to become permanently damaged if the fluid is severely contaminated. The safest action is to have the AdBlue system inspected and flushed as soon as possible. If you see a countdown message, do not delay - have the vehicle recovered to a specialist to avoid being stranded.

Related Fault Codes

P164B is often accompanied by other SCR-related codes. The combination helps pinpoint the root cause.

P204F - Reductant System Performance

This code indicates a general reductant system performance issue; if combined with P164B, it suggests a more severe quality or delivery problem.

P20E8 - Reductant Pressure Too Low

Often appears when the dosing module fails to build adequate pressure, which can also affect fluid quality delivery.

P20E8 - Reductant Pressure Too Low

Related to the AdBlue pressure sensor; a low pressure can cause incomplete atomization and quality degradation.

Frequently Asked Questions About P164B

What does P164B mean on a Jaguar?

It means the Engine Control Module has detected that the reductant (AdBlue) quality is below the required standard for effective NOx reduction. This is usually due to contaminated or poor-quality AdBlue.

Can I drive with P164B on my Jaguar?

Yes, but only for a limited time. The vehicle may enter limp mode, and a countdown timer may appear. If ignored, the engine may eventually refuse to start. Have the system checked promptly.

Is P164B a serious fault?

It's a moderate-to-serious emissions fault. It can lead to failed MOTs, reduced performance, and potential SCR catalyst damage if left unaddressed.

How much does it cost to fix P164B on a Jaguar?

The cost ranges from about £100 for an AdBlue flush and refill to £400–£900 for a NOx sensor or dosing module replacement. Diagnostic time is typically 1 hour.

Will clearing the code fix P164B?

Only temporarily. The code will return if the underlying quality or system issue persists. Clearing it also removes freeze‑frame data that helps diagnosis.

Can I add water to AdBlue to fix it?

No - adding water or any other fluid will dilute the AdBlue and likely worsen the quality issue, triggering P164B more quickly. Always use ISO 22241-compliant AdBlue.
