P132B Jaguar - Turbocharger Boost Control Position Sensor

A code that signals the engine control unit (ECU) has detected a performance or range issue with the turbocharger boost control position sensor circuit. On Jaguar Ingenium diesel and petrol engines - and some V6/V8 applications - this typically points to a faulty boost actuator, wiring damage, carbon build-up on the turbo vanes, or a failing turbocharger. Ignoring it can lead to reduced power, poor fuel economy, and eventual turbocharger failure.

P132B means the ECU has detected that the turbocharger boost control position sensor is reporting a value outside the expected range. On most Jaguar engines - from the Ingenium AJ200D and 204PT petrol to the AJ126 V6 - this is typically caused by a stuck or slow-moving boost actuator, carbon deposits on the variable-geometry turbo vanes (VGT), or wiring faults in the sensor circuit. The fix can range from a £200 actuator cleaning or replacement to a £1,500+ turbocharger overhaul.

  • Most common real cause: a stuck or faulty boost control actuator / position sensor - typically £200–£500 fitted [PATTERN DATA]
  • Worst case: turbocharger vane seizure requiring a replacement turbo - £1,200–£2,800 fitted [PATTERN DATA]
  • Commonly seen alongside limp mode, engine management light, and turbo whistle
  • Often appears with P0299 (Turbocharger Underboost) or P0234 (Turbocharger Overboost) - the combination helps identify whether the issue is electrical or mechanical

Do not ignore P132B. A failing boost control system can cause overboost conditions that damage the engine, or underboost that leaves you without power when you need it. Get it diagnosed promptly.

What P132B Actually Means

In a modern Jaguar engine with a turbocharger, the ECU continuously monitors the position of the boost control actuator - the mechanism that adjusts the turbocharger's variable-geometry vanes (VGT) or wastegate to regulate boost pressure. The position sensor feeds back the actual position of the actuator, and the ECU compares it to the commanded position. P132B is triggered when the ECU detects that the feedback from the position sensor is outside the expected range - either because the actuator is stuck, the sensor is faulty, or the wiring between them has failed. This code is often engine‑specific: on four‑cylinder Ingenium diesels and 204PT petrols, it usually points to the VGT actuator on the single turbocharger. On V6 and V8 applications, it may be bank‑specific depending on the twin‑turbo layout. The underlying cause is almost always related to carbon build-up, electrical failure, or mechanical wear, making it essential to diagnose properly before assuming the turbocharger itself needs replacement.

Symptoms You'll Notice Alongside P132B

  • Engine management light illuminated (usually amber or red)
  • Limp mode - reduced engine power, often with a Restricted Performance message
  • Noticeable turbo whistle or whine that changes with engine load
  • Slow or delayed throttle response, especially when accelerating from low RPM
  • Increased fuel consumption and a lack of mid-range torque
  • In severe cases, the engine may hesitate or surge under acceleration
  • On diesel Ingenium engines, you may also notice excessive black smoke from the exhaust due to over-fuelling

If you notice a sudden loss of power accompanied by a new whine or whistle, stop driving and arrange a diagnostic check immediately - a seized turbo can shed debris into the engine.

Diagnostic Order - Cheapest‑First

A competent Jaguar specialist will follow this order to avoid misdiagnosing a turbocharger replacement when a simple repair will do.

1

Read Live Data

Using a diagnostic tool, monitor the boost control position sensor reading while actuating the turbo. A value that doesn't move when commanded indicates a stuck actuator or faulty sensor.

2

Inspect Wiring & Connector

Check the sensor harness for chafing, corrosion, or loose pins. This is a common failure point on higher‑mileage cars and costs little to inspect.

3

Test Actuator Operation

Apply vacuum or a 12V test signal to the actuator (depending on type) to see if it moves freely. If it's sticky, remove and clean the actuator rod and vane ring.

4

Carbon Inspection (VGT)

If the actuator moves but the vane position is still out of range, the turbocharger may need to be removed to inspect the vane ring for carbon seizure.

5

Turbocharger Health Check

Check shaft play and bearing condition. If there is excessive play, the turbo core is worn and replacement is the only durable solution.

Can You Still Drive Your Jaguar with P132B?

Only to get it directly to a garage - and avoid hard acceleration. P132B indicates the ECU is receiving an invalid or unexpected signal from the boost control position sensor. If the actuator is stuck, the turbocharger may be delivering either too much boost (overboost) or too little (underboost). Overboost can cause pre‑ignition and potential engine damage; underboost leaves you without power when you need it, which can be dangerous on motorways. If the code is intermittent, the ECU may still allow normal driving with reduced performance, but the underlying issue will not fix itself. The safest action is to have the car recovered to a specialist for diagnosis. If you absolutely must drive, keep RPM low and avoid full-throttle acceleration. Listen for any new whistling or grinding noises - if you hear either, stop immediately and call for recovery.

Related Fault Codes

P132B is often accompanied by other codes. The combination helps pinpoint the root cause.

P0299 - Turbocharger Underboost

If both P132B and P0299 appear, it strongly suggests the actuator is stuck in the closed position or the vanes are seized - the ECU is commanding boost but not seeing it.

P0234 - Turbocharger Overboost

This occurs when the actuator is stuck open or the sensor is reporting a false position; the ECU sees more boost than commanded and cuts power to protect the engine.

P00AF - Turbocharger Boost Control A Performance

A related code that indicates the actuator is slow to respond, often a precursor to P132B as the sensor becomes increasingly erratic.

Turbo Whistle / Whine

Not a fault code, but a key symptom that often precedes P132B; see our symptom guide for more detail on what different sounds mean.

P164B - Turbocharger Boost Control Position Sensor Circuit Range/Performance

This is a very close relative of P132B; both point to the same circuit. P164B often appears on the same diagnostic scan and can indicate the sensor itself has failed.

Frequently Asked Questions About P132B

What does P132B mean on a Jaguar?

It means the ECU has detected that the turbocharger boost control position sensor is reporting a value outside the expected range - typically because the actuator is stuck, the sensor has failed, or the wiring is damaged.

Can I drive with P132B on my Jaguar?

Only to get directly to a garage. Driving with this code risks overboost or underboost, which can damage the engine or leave you without power. Have the car recovered if you notice unusual noises.

Is P132B a serious fault?

Yes, it's considered a serious fault. While it may be caused by a simple sticking actuator, it can also indicate a failing turbocharger. Immediate diagnosis is essential to avoid engine damage.

How much does it cost to fix P132B on a Jaguar?

The cost ranges from about £200 for an actuator cleaning or replacement to £1,200–£2,800 for a full turbocharger replacement. Diagnostic time is typically 1–2 hours.

Will clearing the code fix P132B?

Only temporarily. The code will return if the underlying mechanical or electrical issue persists, and clearing it destroys freeze‑frame data that helps diagnosis.

Does P132B affect fuel economy?

Yes - a faulty boost control system can cause over-fuelling (if the ECU thinks boost is low) or a lack of power, both of which increase fuel consumption.
