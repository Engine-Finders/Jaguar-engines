P0403 Jaguar - EGR Control Circuit

A code that signals an electrical or control fault in the exhaust gas recirculation (EGR) system. On Jaguar diesel engines, this often points to a faulty EGR solenoid, a wiring issue, or a failed EGR position sensor. Ignoring it can lead to reduced performance and eventual engine damage.

P0403 means the engine control unit (ECU) has detected an electrical or control circuit fault in the EGR system. On most Jaguar diesel engines - from the Ingenium AJ200D to the 3.0 TDV6 - this is typically caused by a faulty EGR solenoid, damaged wiring, or a failed EGR position sensor. The fix can range from a £100 wiring repair to a £400–£700 EGR valve replacement.

  • Most common real cause: a faulty EGR solenoid or position sensor - typically £180–£400 fitted [PATTERN DATA]
  • Cheapest fix: a wiring repair or loose connection - £100–£250 fitted [PATTERN DATA]

  • Often appears with P0401 or P0402 - the combination helps identify the root cause

Do not ignore P0403. A control circuit fault can prevent the EGR system from functioning correctly, leading to poor combustion and increased emissions.

What P0403 Actually Means

In a modern Jaguar diesel, the exhaust gas recirculation (EGR) system is controlled by the ECU via an electrical circuit that operates the EGR solenoid (or motor) and monitors the position sensor. P0403 is triggered when the ECU detects an electrical fault in this control circuit - such as an open circuit, short circuit, or an out-of-range voltage. This is distinct from P0401 (insufficient flow) and P0402 (excessive flow), which are mechanical faults. The code indicates a problem with the electrical side of the EGR system, which can prevent the valve from operating correctly. The code is common on Ingenium diesel engines in the XE and XF, as well as the 3.0 TDV6 in the XF and F-Pace. The underlying cause is almost always electrical, making it a code that demands systematic diagnosis.

Symptoms You'll Notice Alongside P0403

  • Engine management light illuminated (usually amber)
  • Noticeable power loss or limp mode, especially under acceleration
  • Rough idle or hesitation on acceleration - see misfire / rough idle
  • Excessive black smoke from the exhaust
  • Increased fuel consumption
  • In severe cases, a lack of engine braking or surging at low speeds

If you experience a loss of power and see black smoke, arrange a diagnostic check immediately.

Diagnostic Order - Cheapest‑First

A competent Jaguar specialist will follow this order to avoid misdiagnosing an expensive EGR valve replacement.

1

Visual Inspection of Wiring & Connectors

Check for loose, corroded, or damaged wires at the EGR valve and solenoid. A simple connection repair is often the fix.

2

Check Fuse & Relay

Inspect the EGR circuit fuse and relay. A blown fuse or faulty relay can be replaced cheaply.

3

Test EGR Solenoid Resistance

Using a multimeter, check the solenoid's resistance. Out-of-spec readings indicate a faulty solenoid.

4

Check EGR Position Sensor Signal

Using diagnostic software, read the sensor's voltage. A faulty sensor can be identified by erratic readings.

5

Consider EGR Valve Replacement

If wiring and solenoid are fine, the valve assembly may need replacement. This is the most expensive repair.

Can You Still Drive Your Jaguar with P0403?

Conditionally, yes - but only to get it directly to a garage. P0403 is an electrical fault, which means the EGR valve may not be responding to ECU commands. This can cause the valve to stay in an open or closed position, leading to poor combustion and increased emissions. If the car has entered limp mode, it's unsafe for motorway driving due to lack of power. The safe approach is to avoid heavy acceleration and long journeys. Have it recovered or drive it gently to a specialist.

Related Fault Codes

P0403 is often accompanied by other codes. The combination helps pinpoint the root cause.

P0401 - EGR Flow Insufficient

If both P0401 and P0403 appear, the valve may be stuck closed due to an electrical fault.

P0402 - EGR Excessive Flow

If both P0402 and P0403 appear, the valve may be stuck open due to an electrical fault.

P0405 - EGR Position Sensor Circuit Low

An electrical fault with the sensor itself, which can cause intermittent P0403 codes.

Black Smoke

Not a fault code, but a key symptom that often accompanies P0403; see our symptom guide for more detail.

Frequently Asked Questions About P0403

What does P0403 mean on a Jaguar?

It means the ECU has detected an electrical or control circuit fault in the EGR system. This is often due to a faulty solenoid, damaged wiring, or a failed position sensor.

Can I drive with P0403 on my Jaguar?

Conditionally, yes - but only to get it directly to a garage. Avoid heavy acceleration and long journeys, as an electrical fault can cause the EGR valve to malfunction, leading to poor combustion and increased emissions. If the car is in limp mode, have it recovered.

Is P0403 a serious fault?

It can be. While it may be resolved with a simple wiring repair, it could also indicate a failing EGR valve that needs replacement. Immediate diagnosis is recommended.

How much does it cost to fix P0403 on a Jaguar?

The cost ranges from about £100 for a wiring repair to £400–£700 for a full EGR valve replacement. Diagnostic time is typically 1–2 hours.

Will clearing the code fix P0403?

Only temporarily. The code will return as soon as the ECU detects the circuit fault again. Clearing it also destroys freeze‑frame data that helps diagnosis.
