P0402 Jaguar - EGR Excessive Flow

A code that signals the exhaust gas recirculation (EGR) system is flowing too much exhaust gas back into the intake. On Jaguar diesel engines, this often points to a stuck-open EGR valve, a faulty EGR position sensor, or a blocked EGR cooler. Ignoring it can lead to reduced performance and eventual engine damage.

P0402 means the engine control unit (ECU) has detected that the exhaust gas recirculation (EGR) flow rate is higher than commanded. On most Jaguar diesel engines - from the Ingenium AJ200D to the 3.0 TDV6 - this is typically caused by a stuck-open EGR valve, a faulty EGR position sensor, or carbon buildup in the EGR system. The fix can range from a £150 cleaning to a £600+ EGR valve or cooler replacement.

  • Most common real cause: carbon buildup causing the EGR valve to stick open - typically £150–£350 for cleaning [PATTERN DATA]
  • Worst case: EGR valve or cooler replacement - £400–£900 fitted [PATTERN DATA]
  • Commonly seen alongside engine management light, limp mode, and black smoke
  • Often appears with P0401 or P0402 on the same bank - the combination helps identify the root cause

Do not ignore P0402. Excessive EGR can cause poor combustion, leading to increased soot and potential DPF blockage.

What P0402 Actually Means

In a modern Jaguar diesel, the exhaust gas recirculation (EGR) system reduces nitrogen oxide (NOx) emissions by recirculating a portion of exhaust gas back into the intake manifold. The ECU controls the EGR valve to regulate the flow rate based on engine load, speed, and temperature. P0402 is triggered when the ECU detects that the actual EGR flow is higher than the commanded flow - meaning the valve is allowing too much exhaust gas into the intake. This can happen because the EGR valve is stuck open due to carbon buildup, the EGR position sensor is faulty, or the EGR cooler is blocked. The code is common on Ingenium diesel engines in the XE and XF, as well as the 3.0 TDV6 in the XF and F-Pace. The underlying cause is almost always related to the EGR system, making it a code that demands systematic diagnosis.

Symptoms You'll Notice Alongside P0402

  • Engine management light illuminated (usually amber)
  • Noticeable power loss or limp mode, especially under acceleration
  • Rough idle or hesitation on acceleration - see misfire / rough idle
  • Excessive black smoke from the exhaust
  • Increased fuel consumption
  • In severe cases, a lack of engine braking or surging at low speeds

If you experience a loss of power and see black smoke, arrange a diagnostic check immediately.

Diagnostic Order - Cheapest‑First

A competent Jaguar specialist will follow this order to avoid misdiagnosing an expensive EGR valve replacement.

1

Read EGR Position Data

Using diagnostic software, read the commanded vs actual EGR valve position. A significant discrepancy often indicates a stuck valve or sensor issue.

2

Inspect for Carbon Buildup

A visual inspection of the EGR valve can reveal carbon deposits. Cleaning the valve and the intake ports is often the fix.

3

Check EGR Cooler

A blocked cooler can restrict flow; a pressure test or visual check can confirm this.

4

Test EGR Solenoid & Vacuum System

Check for vacuum leaks and test the solenoid operation. A cheap repair if found.

5

Consider EGR Valve Replacement

If cleaning fails and the sensor is confirmed working, replacement is the final step.

Can You Still Drive Your Jaguar with P0402?

Conditionally, yes - but only to get it directly to a garage. P0402 is not an immediate engine-destroying fault like low oil pressure. However, driving with excessive EGR flow can cause poor combustion, leading to increased soot production, which can clog the DPF and EGR cooler over time. More critically, if the code is caused by a stuck-open EGR valve, continued driving can lead to a significant loss of power and increased fuel consumption. If the car has entered limp mode, it's unsafe for motorway driving due to lack of power. Have it recovered or drive it gently to a specialist.

Related Fault Codes

P0402 is often accompanied by other codes. The combination helps pinpoint the root cause.

P0401 - EGR Flow Insufficient

The mirror image. If both P0401 and P0402 appear, suspect a faulty EGR position sensor or wiring issue.

P0403 - EGR Solenoid Circuit

An electrical fault with the EGR solenoid, which can cause intermittent flow issues.

Frequently Asked Questions About P0402

What does P0402 mean on a Jaguar?

It means the ECU has detected that the EGR valve is allowing too much exhaust gas into the intake. This is often due to carbon buildup, a stuck valve, or a faulty position sensor.

Can I drive with P0402 on my Jaguar?

Conditionally, yes - but only to get it directly to a garage. Avoid heavy acceleration and long journeys, as excessive EGR flow can lead to poor combustion and increased soot, which can damage the DPF. If the car is in limp mode, have it recovered.

Is P0402 a serious fault?

It can be. While it may be resolved with a simple cleaning, it could also indicate a failing EGR valve or cooler that needs replacement. Immediate diagnosis is recommended.

How much does it cost to fix P0402 on a Jaguar?

The cost ranges from about £150 for a cleaning to £400–£900 for a full EGR valve or cooler replacement. Diagnostic time is typically 1–2 hours.

Will clearing the code fix P0402?

Only temporarily. The code will return as soon as the ECU detects the flow discrepancy again. Clearing it also destroys freeze‑frame data that helps diagnosis.
