P0670 Jaguar - Glow Plug Control Module Circuit

A code that signals a fault in the glow plug control module circuit. On Jaguar diesel engines - from the Ingenium AJ200D to the older AJ-V6 and 306DT - this often points to a failed control module, faulty glow plugs, or wiring issues. Ignoring it can lead to cold-start problems, increased emissions, and failed MOTs.

P0670 means the engine control unit (ECU) has detected a circuit malfunction in the glow plug control module. On most Jaguar diesel engines - from the Ingenium AJ200D to the 306DT V6 - this is typically caused by a failed glow plug control module, worn glow plugs, or corroded wiring. The fix can range from a £150–£300 module replacement to a £400+ glow plug set and module overhaul.

  • Most common real cause: a failed glow plug control module - typically £150–£300 fitted [PATTERN DATA]
  • Worst case: multiple faulty glow plugs and module replacement - £400–£800 fitted [PATTERN DATA]
  • Commonly seen alongside glow plug warning light, cold-start rattle, and hard starting
  • Often appears with P0671 to P0674 (individual glow plug circuit codes) - the combination helps pinpoint which plug or the module itself is at fault

Do not ignore P0670. A failed glow plug system can cause poor cold starting, excessive white smoke, and unburnt fuel washing the cylinders, leading to long-term engine wear.

What P0670 Actually Means

In a modern Jaguar diesel engine, the glow plug system pre-heats the combustion chambers to aid cold starting and reduce emissions. The glow plug control module (also known as the glow plug relay or timer) receives a signal from the ECU and supplies current to each glow plug for a set duration. P0670 is triggered when the ECU detects an electrical fault in the control module's circuit - meaning the module is not communicating properly, the supply voltage is incorrect, or the module has failed internally. This code is engine‑specific: on four‑cylinder Ingenium diesels, the module is often integrated into the engine wiring harness; on older V6 engines like the 306DT, it's a separate relay box. The underlying cause is usually electrical (corrosion, blown fuse, failed module) rather than mechanical, but a faulty module can also be caused by a shorted glow plug drawing excessive current.

Symptoms You'll Notice Alongside P0670

  • Engine management light illuminated (usually amber)
  • Glow plug warning light flashing or staying on longer than usual
  • Hard starting when the engine is cold - may take several cranks or require multiple attempts
  • Excessive white smoke from the exhaust on cold start, which clears as the engine warms
  • Rough idle for the first 10–30 seconds after a cold start
  • Increased fuel consumption during warm-up phase
  • In severe cases, the engine may fail to start at all in very cold weather

If you notice the glow plug light flashing and the engine struggles to start, have the system checked promptly to avoid starter motor strain and battery drain.

Diagnostic Order - Cheapest‑First

A competent Jaguar specialist will follow this order to avoid misdiagnosing a module failure when a simple fuse or wire is at fault.

1

Check Fuse & Relay

Locate the glow plug fuse and relay in the engine bay fuse box. Replace any blown fuse and test the relay; a cheap fix if this is the issue.

2

Inspect Wiring & Connector

Visually check the module connector for corrosion, bent pins, or chafed wires. Clean with contact cleaner and repair any damage.

3

Test Glow Plug Resistance

Using a multimeter, measure the resistance of each glow plug. A reading outside 0.5–2.0 ohms (depending on spec) indicates a faulty plug that may have damaged the module.

4

Perform Module Output Test

With a diagnostic tool, command the module to activate and measure voltage at the glow plug harness. No output means the module is dead; replace it.

5

Replace Module & Glow Plugs

If the module is confirmed faulty, replace it along with all glow plugs (as a set) to avoid repeat failure. This is the most comprehensive fix.

Can You Still Drive Your Jaguar with P0670?

Yes, but with caution - and avoid cold starts. P0670 does not directly affect engine running once it's warm, but it does impact cold starting and emissions. You can drive the car, but you may find it hard to start on cold mornings, and you may notice white smoke and rough running until the engine warms up. Prolonged driving with a faulty glow plug system can cause unburnt fuel to wash the cylinder walls, leading to bore wear and oil dilution. It can also overload the starter motor and battery. The safest approach is to get the system diagnosed and repaired as soon as possible - especially before winter. If the engine fails to start, do not keep cranking; you risk draining the battery and damaging the starter. Call for assistance or have the car recovered to a specialist.

Related Fault Codes

P0670 is often accompanied by individual glow plug circuit codes. The combination helps pinpoint the root cause.

P0671 - Glow Plug Circuit Cylinder 1

If P0670 appears with P0671, it suggests a specific plug or its wiring is shorted, which may have damaged the module.

P0672 - Glow Plug Circuit Cylinder 2

Similar to P0671, but for cylinder 2; often seen together if multiple plugs are failing.

P0673 - Glow Plug Circuit Cylinder 3

Indicates a fault on cylinder 3; common on V6 engines where access is tighter.

Hard starting / won't start

Not a fault code, but a key symptom that often precedes P0670; see our symptom guide for more detail on cold-start issues.

Frequently Asked Questions About P0670

What does P0670 mean on a Jaguar?

It means the ECU has detected a fault in the glow plug control module circuit - either the module itself has failed, there's a wiring issue, or a glow plug is shorted and drawing excessive current.

Can I drive with P0670 on my Jaguar?

Yes, but cold starts will be difficult and you may see white smoke. It's not immediately dangerous, but should be fixed promptly to avoid starter wear and cylinder bore damage.

Is P0670 a serious fault?

It's a moderate priority fault. It won't leave you stranded once the engine is warm, but it can lead to starting problems in cold weather and increased emissions, which could fail an MOT.

How much does it cost to fix P0670 on a Jaguar?

The cost ranges from about £150 for a replacement control module to £400–£800 if the module and all glow plugs need replacing. Diagnostic time is typically 1 hour.

Will clearing the code fix P0670?

Only temporarily. The code will return if the underlying electrical or module issue persists. Clearing it also removes freeze‑frame data that helps diagnosis.

Can a faulty glow plug cause P0670?

Yes - a shorted glow plug can draw excessive current and blow the internal driver in the control module, triggering P0670 even before individual plug codes appear.
