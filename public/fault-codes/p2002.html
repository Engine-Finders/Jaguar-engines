P2002 Jaguar — DPF Efficiency Below Threshold

A code that signals the diesel particulate filter (DPF) is not filtering exhaust gases efficiently. On Jaguar diesel engines, this often points to a damaged DPF, a faulty differential pressure sensor, or a failed regeneration. Ignoring it can lead to expensive DPF replacement and engine damage.

P2002 means the engine control unit (ECU) has detected that the diesel particulate filter (DPF) is not removing soot particles from the exhaust as efficiently as expected. On most Jaguar diesel engines — from the Ingenium AJ200D to the 3.0 TDV6 — this is typically caused by a cracked or melted DPF, a faulty differential pressure sensor, or a failed regeneration. The fix can range from a £150 sensor replacement to a £2,500+ DPF replacement.

  • Most common real cause: a faulty DPF differential pressure sensor — typically £150–£300 fitted [PATTERN DATA]
  • Worst case: DPF replacement — £1,800–£2,800 fitted [PATTERN DATA]

Do not ignore P2002. A failing DPF can cause exhaust backpressure to damage the turbocharger and engine.

What P2002 Actually Means

In a modern Jaguar diesel, the diesel particulate filter (DPF) is monitored by a differential pressure sensor that measures the pressure drop across the filter. The ECU uses this reading to calculate the soot load and the filter's efficiency. P2002 is triggered when the ECU detects that the DPF is not trapping soot particles effectively — meaning the filter's efficiency has dropped below a calibrated threshold. This can happen because the DPF is physically damaged (cracked or melted), the differential pressure sensor is faulty, or the filter has become so clogged that it cannot regenerate properly. The code is common on Ingenium diesel engines in the XE and XF, particularly on vehicles used predominantly for short urban trips. The underlying cause is almost always related to the DPF or its sensor, making it a code that demands systematic diagnosis.

Symptoms You'll Notice Alongside P2002

  • DPF warning light illuminated (usually amber or red)
  • Engine management light illuminated (usually amber)
  • Noticeable power loss or limp mode, especially under acceleration
  • Reduced fuel economy
  • Excessive black smoke from the exhaust
  • In severe cases, a burning smell or increased oil level due to diesel dilution

If you see the DPF warning light, try a motorway run at steady speed. If the light persists, arrange a diagnostic check immediately.

Diagnostic Order — Cheapest‑First

A competent Jaguar specialist will follow this order to avoid misdiagnosing an expensive DPF replacement.

1

Read DPF Pressure Sensor Data

Using diagnostic software, read the differential pressure and compare to expected values. A faulty sensor can be replaced cheaply.

2

Check DPF Soot Load

Read the calculated soot load. If it's within limits but efficiency is low, suspect a sensor or physical damage.

3

Perform Forced Regeneration

If the soot load is high, a forced regeneration may clear the code. This costs about £150–£350.

4

Inspect DPF Physically

A visual inspection with an endoscope can reveal cracks or melting. This is often required before deciding on replacement.

5

Consider DPF Replacement

If the DPF is damaged, replacement is the only option. This is the most expensive repair.

Can You Still Drive Your Jaguar with P2002?

Conditionally, yes — but only to get it directly to a garage. P2002 is similar to P2463 but specifically indicates efficiency below threshold, which often means the DPF is not filtering properly. Driving with a damaged DPF can cause exhaust backpressure to build up, which can damage the turbocharger and lead to engine oil dilution. If the car has entered limp mode, it's unsafe for motorway driving due to lack of power. The safe approach is to avoid heavy acceleration and long journeys. If the DPF warning light is flashing or red, stop driving immediately and have the car recovered.

Related Fault Codes

P2002 is often accompanied by other codes. The combination helps pinpoint the root cause.

P2463 — DPF Soot Accumulation

This code indicates excessive soot load, which often precedes P2002 when the DPF becomes damaged.

P2459 — DPF Regeneration Frequency

This code is triggered when the DPF requires regeneration too often, indicating a root cause like a faulty sensor or injector issue.

P0401 — Exhaust Gas Recirculation (EGR) Flow Insufficient

Frequently Asked Questions About P2002

What does P2002 mean on a Jaguar?

It means the ECU has detected that the DPF is not filtering exhaust gases efficiently, often due to a faulty pressure sensor or physical damage to the filter itself.

Can I drive with P2002 on my Jaguar?

Conditionally, yes — but only to get it directly to a garage. Avoid heavy acceleration and long journeys, as a damaged DPF can cause exhaust backpressure that damages the turbocharger. If the warning light is red or flashing, stop driving immediately.

Is P2002 a serious fault?

Yes, it can be serious. While it may be caused by a faulty sensor, it often indicates a damaged DPF that needs replacement. Immediate diagnosis is recommended.

How much does it cost to fix P2002 on a Jaguar?

The cost ranges from about £150 for a sensor replacement to £1,800–£2,800 for a full DPF replacement. Diagnostic time is typically 1–2 hours.

Will clearing the code fix P2002?

Only temporarily. The code will return as soon as the ECU recalculates the efficiency, which is usually within a drive cycle or two. Clearing it also destroys freeze‑frame data that helps diagnosis.
