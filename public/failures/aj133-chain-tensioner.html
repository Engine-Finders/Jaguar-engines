AJ133 Pre-2013 Timing Chain Tensioner Wear — Symptoms, Cost & Diagnosis

Symptom Progression — What Owners Notice, In Order

The timing chain tensioner failure on pre‑2013 AJ133 engines is rarely a sudden event. It gives audible and drivability warnings over weeks or months, and the earlier you act, the cheaper the fix.

  1. Stage 1 – Cold‑start rattle. A brief metallic rattle on startup from cold, lasting 1–2 seconds, then quietening. This is the earliest and most critical warning sign — often dismissed as normal until it worsens.
  2. Stage 2 – Rattling on warm start. The rattle becomes noticeable even when the engine is warm, especially after sitting for 30–60 minutes. The tensioner is no longer holding oil pressure effectively.
  3. Stage 3 – Continuous rattling at idle. A persistent rattle or clatter at idle, which may disappear with revs. At this point the tensioner is significantly worn and the chain may be slapping the guides.
  4. Stage 4 – Timing fault codes and poor running. The ECU may flag camshaft/crank correlation codes (P0016, P0017) and the engine may run roughly, hesitate or lose power.
  5. Stage 5 – Catastrophic failure. The tensioner fails completely, the chain skips or breaks, and valves meet pistons. The engine is typically scrap at this point.

If you hear any rattle on start‑up, do not ignore it. A simple garage inspection can confirm tensioner health before it becomes a £9,000+ engine replacement. For a broader view of Jaguar timing chain issues, see our timing chain diagnosis guide.

Root Cause

The AJ133 5.0‑litre V8 uses chain‑driven camshafts with hydraulic tensioners that rely on oil pressure to maintain correct chain tension. On engines built before mid‑2013, the tensioner design was prone to rapid wear — specifically, the internal ratchet mechanism and sealing ring would degrade, allowing oil to drain back when the engine was off. This resulted in a loss of tension on startup, causing the chain to slap against the guides and creating the characteristic rattle. Over time, this repeated slapping accelerates guide wear, stretches the chain, and eventually leads to timing errors or catastrophic failure if the chain jumps or breaks. Jaguar revised the tensioner design in 2013 (introducing an improved ratchet and sealing ring), and later engines are far more robust [EM-INTERVIEW]. The failure is therefore a known design‑level issue on early cars, not a maintenance failure per se — but it can be prevented by early intervention.

What Makes Failure More or Less Likely

📅

Build Date

Pre‑2013 units are the highest risk. If your car was built after mid‑2013, the tensioner design was revised and failure is rare. Always verify build date using the VIN or door sticker.

🛢️

Oil Change History

Regular oil changes using the correct specification (5W‑30 or 0W‑20) help maintain tensioner hydraulic pressure. Extended intervals accelerate wear.

🔊

Early Warning Ignored

Owners who ignore the cold‑start rattle and continue driving are far more likely to reach catastrophic failure. Prompt investigation is the single biggest factor in keeping the repair affordable.

How a Specialist Confirms It

1

Cold‑Start Listening Test

A specialist starts the engine from stone cold and listens for the rattle. If it lasts more than 1–2 seconds, the tensioners are suspect.

2

Oil Pressure & Tensioner Check

Measuring oil pressure at idle and inspecting the tensioners (via a borescope through the oil filler cap or cam cover) can confirm wear without major disassembly.

3

Timing Chain Inspection

In advanced cases, the cam covers may need to be removed to inspect chain stretch and guide wear. This is the definitive check but requires more labour.

Prevention & Early Detection

You cannot eliminate the risk on a pre‑2013 AJ133, but you can significantly reduce the chance of catastrophic failure.

  • Don't ignore the rattle. If you hear any rattle on startup, get it checked immediately. A simple oil pressure test and tensioner inspection can save thousands.
  • Use the correct oil. Spec 5W‑30 or 0W‑20 (Jaguar spec) and change it at or before 10,000 miles. Clean oil helps maintain tensioner hydraulic pressure.
  • Consider preventative tensioner replacement. For pre‑2013 cars approaching 60,000 miles, many specialists recommend changing the tensioners and guides as a preventative measure — it's far cheaper than a full engine rebuild.

Frequently Asked Questions

What are the first signs of AJ133 timing chain tensioner failure?

The earliest sign is a brief rattle on cold start (1–2 seconds). As the tensioner wears, the rattle becomes longer and may also occur on warm starts. Ignoring this can lead to chain skip and catastrophic engine damage.

Can I replace just the tensioners, or does the whole timing chain need to be replaced?

In early stages, replacing the tensioners and guides is often sufficient. However, if the chain has stretched or the guides are severely worn, a full timing chain kit (tensioners, guides, chain, and sprockets) is recommended. A specialist inspection will determine the scope.

How much does AJ133 chain tensioner replacement cost in the UK in 2025?

For a supply‑and‑fit of a reconditioned engine with new tensioners, expect £5,500–£9,000 at an independent specialist. If caught early and only tensioners are needed, the cost may be lower (around £2,500–£4,500 for parts and labour). Main dealer costs are significantly higher.

Is it worth fixing an AJ133 tensioner failure on an older Jaguar?

On most XF, XJ and F‑Type models, yes — vehicle values typically range from £8,000 to £35,000, making the repair worthwhile. However, on a high‑mileage XK or XF worth less than £8,000, the repair may exceed 60% of the car's value. Use our repair‑vs‑replace calculator to help decide.

Is there a recall for AJ133 timing chain tensioners?

Jaguar did not issue a formal recall for this issue, but they did revise the tensioner design in 2013. Some extended warranties have covered tensioner failures, but most owners pay out of pocket.
