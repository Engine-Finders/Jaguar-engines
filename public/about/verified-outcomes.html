Where Our Outcome Data Comes From

We collect outcome data through two primary channels:

  • Post‑sale surveys — sent to owners 3‑6 months after their engine replacement or major repair. We ask about satisfaction, whether the work met expectations, whether any issues arose, and whether they would choose the same specialist again. These are conducted via email and are voluntary; response rates are typically around 30-40% [EM-SURVEY].
  • Specialist outcome reports — we also track completion rates, response times, and warranty claim patterns through our CRM. These are aggregated at the network level and are not published per specialist.

We do not publish individual customer testimonials, nor do we allow specialists to collect reviews through us. Our purpose is quality assurance, not marketing. The data we collect feeds directly into our specialist vetting and editorial standards, and is also used in our case studies as aggregate analysis.

Survey Methodology

Every survey follows a consistent structure to ensure comparability:

  • Timing: 3‑6 months after the job is completed — long enough to uncover any latent issues, but close enough for accurate recall.
  • Questions: 5‑7 questions covering overall satisfaction (1‑5 scale), whether the fault was resolved, whether any warranty claims were made, whether the cost matched the estimate, and a free‑text field for additional comments.
  • Anonymity: Responses are anonymised before any aggregation. We never link a response to a specific owner or vehicle in published material.
  • Voluntary participation: Owners are invited but not required. We do not incentivise responses, to avoid skewing the data.

The full question set and our handling of free‑text responses are documented in our data methodology. Any corrections to survey data are logged in our corrections log.

Aggregate Patterns (What We Publish)

We publish aggregate findings in our case studies and data reports. These are always anonymised and presented as patterns, not individual experiences. For example:

  • Decision splits — what proportion of owners chose repair vs replacement for a given failure [EM-SURVEY]
  • Satisfaction rates by engine type or repair type
  • Common post‑repair issues and how they were resolved
  • Cost‑to‑value comparisons based on real outcomes

We never publish quotes or paraphrased comments from individual respondents. Our editorial standards explicitly prohibit fabricated testimonials, and we hold ourselves to the same standard for real ones — we simply don't use them.

How We Use This Data for Vetting

Outcome data is a key input to our ongoing specialist review. We look for patterns at the specialist level:

  • Satisfaction trends — are owners consistently satisfied or are there clusters of dissatisfaction?
  • Warranty claim rates — a high rate of warranty claims may indicate quality issues with supplied units or fitting work
  • Response and completion times — tracked through our CRM, not surveys

Where a specialist's outcome metrics fall below our thresholds, we reduce their routing or remove them from the network entirely, following the process described on our specialist vetting page. We do not publish individual specialist ratings, because our goal is to maintain quality, not to run a public review board. However, the aggregate data we do publish is transparent and open to scrutiny — our data verification page explains how we tag and verify every claim.

Limitations of Our Data

We are transparent about what our outcome data can and cannot tell you:

  • Sample size — our survey pool is limited to owners who used our network and chose to respond. It is not a representative sample of all Jaguar owners in the UK, and we do not claim it is.
  • Self‑selection bias — owners who respond may have more extreme experiences (very good or very bad) than the average. We account for this in our analysis by looking at the full distribution, not just averages.
  • No long‑term tracking — we survey at 3‑6 months, not years. Some issues may emerge later, and we do not track that.
  • Specialist outcomes are not published individually — we use the data for internal decision‑making, not for public comparison. If you want to know about a specific specialist, we recommend you speak to them directly.

We regularly review our methodology to address these limitations, and we publish any changes in our corrections log.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do you publish individual customer reviews?

No. We never publish named or anonymised individual testimonials. All outcome data is aggregated and used internally for quality assurance, not for public marketing.

How do you collect outcome data?

We send voluntary surveys to owners 3‑6 months after their engine replacement or major repair. We also track response times and warranty claims through our CRM. Full details are in our data methodology.

Can I see the survey results for a specific specialist?

No. We do not publish individual specialist ratings. Our purpose is to maintain network quality, not to run a public review board. If you have questions about a specific specialist, we recommend contacting them directly.

How do you ensure the data is reliable?

We use consistent survey questions, anonymise responses, and cross‑reference with CRM data. Any corrections to our methodology or published findings are logged in our corrections log.
