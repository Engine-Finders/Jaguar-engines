AJ133 Cold Start Rattle

A cold start rattle on this 5.0‑litre V8 supercharged engine is the single most important early warning sign you should not ignore - especially on pre‑2013 models. Here's exactly what it means, which mileage band to worry about, and what it costs to fix before it becomes catastrophic.

On the AJ133 specifically, a cold start rattle that lasts 1–3 seconds and gradually gets longer over weeks is the leading indicator of timing chain tensioner wear - a well‑documented weakness on pre‑2013 engines. It can also be a worn supercharger coupler, though that produces a different sound.

What This Engine Specifically Does

Where our general cold start rattle guide covers multiple possible causes across the Jaguar range, on the AJ133 the picture narrows to two dominant signatures. The first is a brief, metallic rattle on the very first cold start of the day, lasting 1–3 seconds and disappearing as oil pressure builds. This is the classic timing chain tensioner wear signature - well‑documented on pre‑2013 AJ133 V8s. The second is a higher‑pitched, "loose change" rattle that appears under light acceleration, not on start‑up - that's the supercharger coupler. The two sounds are quite different to an experienced ear: the tensioner rattle is a deeper, more "diesel‑like" clatter on start‑up; the coupler rattle is a constant chatter that rises with engine speed. Both can sound similar to an untrained ear over a phone recording, which is why in‑person diagnosis matters.

Why This Engine In Particular

The AJ133's timing chain tensioner is hydraulic and relies on oil pressure to maintain proper chain tension. On pre‑2013 engines, the tensioner seals weaken over time - a known design issue - allowing oil to drain back overnight, leaving the chain slack on the next cold start until pressure builds. What makes this engine‑specific is the progressive nature: the rattle initially lasts just a second, but as wear advances, it can last 3–5 seconds, and eventually the chain can jump a tooth, bending valves and turning a £2,500 repair into a £9,000+ engine replacement. This is a design characteristic of this particular tensioner arrangement, which is why the same symptom means something quite different on, say, a AJ126 or an Ingenium engine.

How to Confirm It's This, Not Something Else

Before assuming the worst, rule out the cheaper alternatives specific to this engine. Low oil level can produce a similar transient rattle on cold start and is free to check yourself - top up to the correct level with the right specification oil and listen again the next cold start. A failing water pump bearing or alternator pulley can produce a similar metallic noise, though these are usually more constant rather than start‑up‑specific. If the rattle is only on the very first cold start of the day, lasts 1–3 seconds, and gets longer over weeks, that's the timing chain tensioner. If the noise is a constant "loose change" rattle under acceleration, that's the supercharger coupler. If the rattle is accompanied by a check engine light with codes P0011 or P0016 (camshaft timing), that's strong confirmation of timing chain wear - stop driving and book a specialist immediately.

Verdict

If you have a pre‑2013 AJ133 and hear any cold‑start rattle, do not delay - book a specialist this week, not next month. The timing chain tensioner issue on pre‑2013 engines is the single most critical failure mode on this V8, and catching it early is the difference between a £3,000 repair and a £10,000+ engine replacement. The supercharger coupler rattle is less urgent but should be addressed before the play damages the supercharger snout. Frequently Asked Questions

Is an AJ133 cold start rattle always the timing chain tensioner?

It's the most critical cause, but not the only one. Supercharger coupler wear can produce a rattle under load, not on cold start. The key is when the noise occurs: cold start = chain tensioner; under acceleration = supercharger coupler. Low oil level can also cause a transient rattle.

At what mileage does an AJ133 typically start rattling on cold start?

Most cases in our data fall between 60,000 and 120,000 miles, with pre‑2013 examples most affected. The rattle often starts subtly around 70k–80k and worsens gradually. Many AJ133s pass through this range without ever developing the fault, particularly with strong service history and regular oil changes.

Can I drive with an AJ133 cold start rattle?

If it's a brief (1–2 second) rattle that disappears immediately, you can drive it to a specialist - but book it in within the next few days. If the rattle lasts more than 3 seconds, or if it's accompanied by a check engine light (especially timing‑related codes), do not drive it. The risk of the chain jumping and causing catastrophic valve damage is too high.

How do I tell the difference between the supercharger coupler and the timing chain?

Timing chain tensioner noise is a brief rattle on cold start that fades as oil pressure builds. Supercharger coupler noise is a constant "loose change" rattle that appears under light acceleration, especially between 1,500–2,500 rpm, and is present regardless of engine temperature. They sound different: coupler is a higher‑pitched metallic chatter; chain tensioner is a deeper, more "diesel‑like" clatter on start‑up.
