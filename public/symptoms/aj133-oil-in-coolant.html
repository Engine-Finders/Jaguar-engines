AJ133 Oil in Coolant / Mayonnaise Under Oil Cap

Oil mixing with coolant, often presenting as a creamy residue under the oil cap or an oily film in the expansion tank, is a different conversation on the AJ133 than on any other Jaguar engine. Here's exactly what it means on the 5.0 V8, the components most likely to be responsible, and real cost data if it turns out to be the fault this engine is known for.

On the AJ133 specifically, oil mixing with coolant-often visible as a creamy "mayonnaise" under the oil filler cap or as an oily film in the expansion tank-is a critical warning sign. It almost always points to a failed oil cooler (a known weakness on the 5.0 V8) or a head gasket failure, the latter being more common on pre-2013 models. Ignoring it risks overheating, engine seizure, and a bill of £5,500–£9,000 for a replacement engine [EM-QUOTE]. What This Engine Specifically Does

Where our general oil contamination guide covers several possible causes, on the AJ133 the picture narrows considerably. The dominant failure signatures are either a leaking oil cooler-the oil-to-water heat exchanger mounted in the V of the engine-or a head gasket failure that allows oil and coolant to mix. The most obvious tell-tale is a thick, creamy "mayonnaise" residue on the underside of the oil filler cap, often accompanied by a drop in coolant level and an oily film on the surface of the expansion tank. This is distinct from the occasional condensation that can appear on short journeys (which is normal) and from the milky oil caused by a faulty PCV system. On the AJ133, the presence of this mayo, especially when accompanied by coolant loss, is a red flag that requires immediate investigation.

Why This Engine In Particular

The AJ133 V8 has two known pathways for oil and coolant to mix. The first is the oil cooler, which uses engine coolant to regulate oil temperature. The seals within the oil cooler can fail over time, especially on higher-mileage engines, allowing oil to leak into the coolant passage and vice versa. The second, more serious, is a head gasket failure. Pre-2013 AJ133s are particularly susceptible due to a combination of cooling system weaknesses (plastic crossover pipes that become brittle) and the original head gasket design. When the head gasket fails, oil can enter the coolant jackets, and coolant can enter the oil galleries-leading to the classic milky residue. This is a design characteristic of this particular V8 layout rather than a universal issue, which is why the same symptom means something quite different on, say, a previous-generation AJ-V8 with a simpler cooling system.

How to Confirm It's This, Not Something Else

Before jumping to the worst conclusions, it's worth ruling out the less serious possibilities. Condensation from short journeys can create a small amount of mayo on the oil cap, but it should disappear after a long drive and the coolant level will remain stable. If the mayo is thick, persistent, and accompanied by coolant loss or overheating, it's time for professional diagnosis. A specialist will perform a cooling system pressure test to check for leaks, and a combustion leak test (sniff test) to detect exhaust gases in the coolant-a sure sign of a head gasket failure. An oil cooler leak can be diagnosed by isolating the oil cooler and pressure testing both the oil and coolant circuits. Do not continue driving with confirmed oil-coolant mixing; it can lead to bearing damage and engine seizure.

Verdict

Get it diagnosed by a specialist immediately. Oil and coolant mixing is a serious issue that can escalate quickly. If it's an oil cooler seal, the repair is relatively straightforward and cost-effective. If it's a head gasket, early intervention can save the engine; ignoring it will lead to overheating, warped heads, and a full engine replacement. Frequently Asked Questions

Is mayo under the oil cap always a head gasket failure on an AJ133?

Not always. On the AJ133, a failed oil cooler is actually the more common cause of oil and coolant mixing, especially on later models. However, head gasket failure is also common, particularly on pre-2013 engines. A specialist can differentiate with a combustion leak test and pressure testing of both systems.

Can I drive an AJ133 with oil in the coolant?

No. Continued driving with oil in the coolant will compromise the cooling system's efficiency, leading to overheating, and the contaminated oil can damage bearings and other engine components. Stop driving and have it towed to a specialist.

How much does it cost to fix oil in coolant on an AJ133?

If it's a simple oil cooler seal replacement, expect £1,500–£3,000 at an independent specialist. A head gasket replacement can cost £3,500–£5,500, and if the engine has overheated and suffered damage, you're looking at £5,500–£9,000 for a reconditioned engine supply and fit.

How can I prevent oil and coolant mixing on my AJ133?

Regular coolant changes (every 3 years or 60,000 miles) using G13 spec, and monitoring the oil cap and coolant level monthly can catch issues early. On pre-2013 models, consider proactive replacement of the oil cooler seals and cooling system components (crossover pipes, water pump) as a preventative measure.
