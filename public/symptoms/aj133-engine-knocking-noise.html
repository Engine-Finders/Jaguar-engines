AJ133 Engine Knocking Noise

A knock on this 5.0‑litre V8 supercharged engine is a different conversation to a knock on any other Jaguar petrol. The AJ133 has two distinct failure signatures - one that's a warning, and one that's an emergency. Here's exactly what each sounds like, which mileage band matters, and what it costs to put right.

On the AJ133 specifically, a knocking or rattling noise is almost always one of two things: a timing chain tensioner failure (pre‑2013 engines, catastrophic if ignored) or a worn supercharger coupler (fixable, less urgent).

What This Engine Specifically Does

Where our general engine knocking noise guide covers multiple possible causes across the Jaguar range, on the AJ133 the picture narrows to two dominant signatures. The first is a cold‑start rattle that lasts 1–3 seconds and gets progressively longer over weeks - this is the classic timing chain tensioner wear signature, well‑documented on pre‑2013 AJ133 V8s. The second is a metallic, "loose change" rattle that appears under light acceleration, especially between 1,500 and 2,500 rpm - this is the supercharger coupler, a wear item on the 5.0‑litre supercharger. The two sounds are quite different to an experienced ear: the tensioner rattle is a deeper, more "diesel‑like" clatter on start‑up; the coupler rattle is a higher‑pitched chatter that rises with engine speed. Both can sound similar to an untrained ear over a phone recording, which is why in‑person diagnosis matters.

Why This Engine In Particular

The AJ133 5.0 V8 has two well‑known mechanical weaknesses. The timing chain tensioner on pre‑2013 engines is hydraulic and relies on oil pressure - its seals weaken over time, allowing oil to drain back and causing chain slap on cold start until pressure builds. If ignored, the chain can jump a tooth, bending valves and turning a £3,000 repair into a £10,000+ engine replacement. The supercharger coupler is a plastic/elastomer component that drives the supercharger rotors - it wears over time, developing play that manifests as the "loose change" rattle under load. What makes this engine‑specific is the access difficulty: the supercharger sits atop the V8, meaning even a relatively inexpensive coupler replacement requires significant labour, and the timing chain is a substantial job requiring engine removal on some XJ and XF models. Differentiating between the two is critical because the repair costs differ by a factor of five.

How to Confirm It's This, Not Something Else

Before assuming the worst, rule out the cheaper alternatives. Low oil level can cause a rattling timing chain tensioner on cold start - check the dipstick and top up if needed. A failing water pump bearing or alternator pulley can produce a similar metallic noise, though these are usually more constant rather than start‑up‑specific or load‑specific. If the noise is a brief rattle on the very first cold start of the day that lasts 1–3 seconds and gradually gets longer, that's almost certainly the timing chain tensioner. If the noise is a constant "loose change" rattle under light acceleration, that's the supercharger coupler. If the noise is a deep, rhythmic knock that gets louder with load, that could be bottom‑end bearing wear - rare on this engine, but not unheard of on high‑mileage, poorly maintained examples - and requires immediate specialist attention.

Verdict

If you have a pre‑2013 AJ133 and hear any cold‑start rattle, do not delay - book a specialist this week, not next month. The timing chain tensioner issue on pre‑2013 engines is the single most critical failure mode on this V8, and catching it early is the difference between a £3,000 repair and a £10,000+ engine replacement. The supercharger coupler rattle is less urgent but should be addressed before the play damages the supercharger snout. Frequently Asked Questions

Is an AJ133 knocking noise always the timing chain tensioner?

No - it's the most critical cause, but not the only one. Supercharger coupler wear is equally common, particularly on higher‑mileage examples, and produces a different sound (rattle under load, not on cold start). The key is when the noise occurs: cold start = chain tensioner; under acceleration = supercharger coupler.

At what mileage does an AJ133 typically start knocking?

Timing chain tensioner issues often appear between 60,000 and 120,000 miles on pre‑2013 engines. Supercharger coupler wear is more common in the 70,000–100,000 mile range. Many AJ133s pass through these ranges without ever developing the fault, particularly with strong service history and regular oil changes.

Can I drive with an AJ133 cold‑start rattle?

If it's a brief (1–2 second) rattle that disappears immediately, you can drive it to a specialist - but book it in within the next few days. If the rattle lasts more than 3 seconds, or if it's accompanied by a check engine light (especially timing‑related codes), do not drive it. The risk of the chain jumping and causing catastrophic valve damage is too high.

How do I tell the difference between the supercharger coupler and the timing chain?

Timing chain tensioner noise is a brief rattle on cold start that fades as oil pressure builds. Supercharger coupler noise is a constant "loose change" rattle that appears under light acceleration, especially between 1,500–2,500 rpm, and is present regardless of engine temperature. They sound different: coupler is a higher‑pitched metallic chatter; chain tensioner is a deeper, more "diesel‑like" clatter on start‑up.
