276DT White Smoke From Exhaust

White smoke on this specific Jaguar V6 diesel is a different conversation to the same symptom on any other Jaguar engine. Here's exactly what it means on the 276DT, the components most likely to be responsible, and real cost data if it turns out to be the fault this engine is known for.

On the 276DT specifically, white smoke from the exhaust is a clear sign of either unburnt diesel fuel or coolant entering the combustion chamber — most commonly caused by a faulty injector, a leaking EGR cooler, or a failing head gasket. It's a serious issue that, if ignored, can lead to engine damage, contamination of the oil, or a blocked DPF.

What This Engine Specifically Does

Where our general white smoke guide covers several possible causes across the whole Jaguar range, on the 276DT the picture narrows considerably. This 2.7-litre V6 diesel's most common causes of white smoke are either unburnt fuel (from a faulty injector or a timing issue) or coolant vapour (from a leaking head gasket or a failed EGR cooler). White smoke is distinct from the thick black cloud of over-fuelling and the blue haze of oil burning. On the 276DT, white smoke that appears on startup and then clears may be condensation, but persistent white smoke – especially if it has a sweet smell (coolant) or a strong diesel odour – is a serious warning. The 276DT's EGR cooler is known to develop leaks, allowing coolant to be drawn into the intake, and injector failure is not uncommon on higher-mileage units. All require immediate attention to prevent further engine damage.

Why This Engine In Particular

The 276DT's design, while robust in many areas, has specific vulnerabilities that lead to white smoke. The EGR cooler, which recirculates exhaust gases to reduce emissions, is prone to internal leaks on this engine – coolant can seep into the exhaust stream and produce a thick white plume. The injectors, operating at very high pressure, can develop wear or electrical faults, causing incomplete combustion and white smoke. Finally, the head gasket can fail, allowing coolant to enter the cylinders – this is often accompanied by a loss of coolant and overheating. This is a design characteristic of this particular engine family, and the same symptom on, say, a previous-generation AJ-V8 would point to a very different set of causes – often a failing thermostat or water pump, rather than the more EGR- and injector-focused failures on the 276DT.

The EGR cooler and injector – two common sources of white smoke on the 276DT.

How to Confirm It's This, Not Something Else

Before assuming the worst, rule out cheaper alternatives. A small amount of white smoke on a cold start that disappears quickly can be a normal characteristic of some diesel engines, but on the 276DT it is not. If the white smoke is persistent, especially if it's accompanied by a noticeable drop in coolant level, a sweet smell from the exhaust, a loss of power, or an engine overheating warning, it's a serious concern. A specialist will perform a cooling system pressure test to check for leaks, a cylinder leak-down test to check for head gasket failure, and a diagnostic scan to check for injector faults. They will also inspect the EGR cooler for signs of leakage. This is not a DIY job; ignoring 276DT white smoke can lead to a complete engine failure, a blocked DPF, and a much more expensive repair bill.

Verdict

If you see persistent white smoke from your 276DT, get it inspected by a specialist without delay. This is a serious issue on this engine, and every mile you drive risks turning a £300–£800 injector repair into a £5,000–£7,500 engine replacement. If you catch it early — before the smoke becomes heavy or other symptoms appear — you can replace the faulty component and save the engine. If the head gasket has failed or the engine has suffered internal damage, a full rebuild or replacement is on the cards. Frequently Asked Questions

Is 276DT white smoke always the head gasket?

No, it's a common cause but not the only one. A faulty injector, a leaking EGR cooler, or even condensation on a cold start can cause white smoke. Persistent white smoke with a sweet smell or coolant loss is more likely a head gasket or EGR cooler leak.

At what mileage does a 276DT typically start producing white smoke?

Most cases in our data occur between 80,000 and 140,000 miles, though this is heavily dependent on service history and cooling system maintenance. Regular coolant changes and using the correct antifreeze can reduce the risk.

Can I drive a 276DT with white smoke?

If you see white smoke, you should drive it only to a specialist for inspection. Continuing to drive with this symptom can lead to catastrophic engine failure, DPF blockage, or oil contamination. Have it checked as soon as possible.

How much does it cost to fix white smoke on a 276DT?

If caught early, an injector replacement typically costs £300–£800 at an independent specialist. An EGR cooler replacement can cost £400–£1,200. A head gasket replacement can be £1,500–£2,500. If the engine has suffered severe damage, you're looking at £5,000–£7,500 for a reconditioned engine supply and fit.
