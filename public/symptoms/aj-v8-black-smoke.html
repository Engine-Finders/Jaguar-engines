AJ-V8 Black Smoke From Exhaust

Black smoke from your AJ-V8 exhaust is a clear sign of an overly rich fuel mixture. Here's what causes it on this V8 family, how to diagnose the source, and what it costs to fix.

On the Jaguar AJ-V8, black smoke from the exhaust is a classic sign of an over-rich fuel mixture. The most common causes are a faulty MAF sensor, a clogged air filter, leaking fuel injectors, or a turbocharger that's over-boosting (on supercharged models). Addressing it early can save the engine from further damage.

What This Engine Specifically Does

Where our general black smoke guide covers a broad range of causes across all Jaguar models, on the AJ-V8 the picture is more focused. This family of Jaguar V8s (4.0, 4.2, and 5.0 supercharged) is known for a few specific failure modes that manifest as black smoke. The most common are a faulty MAF sensor (which misreads air intake and causes the ECU to over-fuel), a clogged air filter (restricting airflow), leaking fuel injectors (dripping excess fuel), or in supercharged models, a turbocharger that is over-boosting. Black smoke is distinct from the blue smoke of oil burning and the white smoke of coolant ingress. It is a sign that the engine is running too rich, which can lead to fouled spark plugs and catalytic converter damage if left unchecked.

Why This Engine In Particular

The AJ-V8's engine management system relies on accurate data from the MAF sensor to calculate the correct fuel mixture. Over time, the MAF sensor can become contaminated with oil or dirt, causing it to under-read airflow. The ECU then injects less fuel than needed — wait, that would cause a lean condition. Actually, a contaminated MAF sensor typically under-reads airflow, causing the ECU to inject less fuel, which would cause a lean condition, not black smoke. A failing MAF sensor can also fail in a way that over-reads airflow, causing over-fuelling and black smoke. Alternatively, the sensor can fail completely, and the ECU uses a default value that often results in a rich mixture. Leaking fuel injectors are another common issue on higher-mileage AJ-V8s, allowing fuel to drip into the cylinders even when not commanded. On supercharged models, a stuck wastegate or faulty boost control solenoid can cause the turbocharger to over-boost, forcing more air into the engine than the ECU expects, leading to a rich mixture and black smoke. The same symptom on a diesel 276DT would point to a different set of causes (often injector or turbo issues), but on the AJ-V8, the MAF sensor and fuel system are the prime suspects.

How to Confirm It's This, Not Something Else

Before assuming the worst, rule out simpler possibilities. Check the air filter — a severely clogged filter restricts airflow, causing a rich mixture and black smoke. Replacing it is a simple, low-cost fix. Also check for any vacuum leaks or split boost hoses, which can confuse the MAF sensor and lead to over-fuelling. If the black smoke persists, especially if it's accompanied by a loss of power, hesitation, or a check engine light, it's time for a professional diagnosis. A specialist will perform a MAF sensor test, an injector balance test, and a boost pressure test (on supercharged models) to check the turbocharger. Do not ignore black smoke; it will only get worse and can lead to a blocked catalytic converter.

Verdict

Get it diagnosed by a specialist as soon as you notice black smoke. The AJ-V8's black smoke is a clear sign of an over-rich mixture, and early intervention can save you from a much larger bill. If it's a simple air filter or MAF sensor, the repair is cheap and straightforward. If it's an injector or turbocharger issue, catching it early can prevent secondary damage to the catalytic converters. Frequently Asked Questions

Is black smoke from an AJ-V8 always a fuel injector issue?

Not always. It can also be a faulty MAF sensor, a clogged air filter, or on supercharged models, a turbocharger over-boosting. A specialist can diagnose the exact cause with a diagnostic scan and component testing.

At what mileage does an AJ-V8 typically start producing black smoke?

Most cases occur between 60,000 and 120,000 miles, though this is heavily dependent on service history and driving conditions. Regular air filter changes and using good quality fuel can significantly reduce the risk.

Can I drive an AJ-V8 with black smoke?

It's not recommended. Continued driving with black smoke can quickly damage the catalytic converters, leading to much more expensive repairs. Have it inspected as soon as possible.

How much does it cost to fix black smoke on an AJ-V8?

A MAF sensor replacement is typically £150–£400. Injector replacement is £300–£800. Turbocharger repair (supercharged models) is £800–£2,000. If the engine has suffered severe damage, you're looking at £5,500–£9,000 for a reconditioned engine supply and fit.

What causes black smoke on a supercharged AJ-V8?

On supercharged models, a stuck wastegate or faulty boost control solenoid can cause the turbocharger to over-boost, forcing too much air into the engine and resulting in a rich mixture and black smoke. This is a known issue on the 5.0 SC engine.
