Coolant disappearing on the AJ126 3.0 V6 supercharged with no visible puddle underneath is a classic warning sign. On this engine, it almost always means a cracked coolant crossover pipe - the plastic Y‑pipe hiding in the supercharger valley - or a slowly leaking water pump. What This Engine Specifically Does

Where our general coolant loss guide covers a wide range of causes across the Jaguar range, on the AJ126 the picture narrows significantly. This 3.0 V6 supercharged loses coolant invisibly for one of two dominant reasons: a cracked coolant crossover pipe - the plastic Y‑pipe that sits in the valley between the supercharger and the engine block - or a slowly leaking water pump. The tell‑tale difference is the location of coolant loss: the crossover pipe leaks into the supercharger valley, where the coolant pools and evaporates without ever reaching the ground; the water pump can leak from its seal or weep hole, sometimes only when the engine is hot and under pressure, leaving little to no trace.

Why This Engine In Particular

The AJ126's cooling system has two well‑documented weaknesses. The coolant crossover pipe - a plastic Y‑shaped pipe that routes coolant between the two cylinder heads - is buried under the supercharger and becomes brittle with age, heat cycling, and exposure to hot coolant. It cracks without warning, allowing coolant to leak into the valley where it evaporates, explaining why owners often see no visible leak. The water pump, driven by the supercharger belt, is prone to bearing failure and seal leaks over time. What makes this engine‑specific is the access difficulty: the crossover pipe is completely hidden under the supercharger, so a pressure test is the only reliable way to confirm the fault. Many owners top up the coolant for weeks before the issue becomes severe enough to overheat the engine, which is why understanding the symptom is so important.

How to Confirm It's This, Not Something Else

Before assuming crossover pipe or water pump, rule out the simple stuff. A loose expansion tank cap can allow coolant to escape as vapour - check the cap and replace if the seal looks perished. A tiny leak in a radiator hose can cause slow loss without leaving a puddle, especially if it only leaks when hot. If the coolant level is dropping but there's no visible leak, the next steps are for a specialist: a coolant pressure test (pumping the system to 1.5 bar and watching for pressure drop) and a boroscope inspection of the supercharger valley. If the coolant loss is accompanied by white smoke from the exhaust or a sweet smell in the cabin, that's a more serious issue - likely a head gasket - and requires immediate attention.

Verdict

Don't ignore coolant loss on an AJ126 - book a specialist this week, not next month. Coolant disappearing with no visible leak is almost never a minor issue on this engine. The crossover pipe and water pump are both known weak points, and driving with a leaking coolant system can lead to overheating, head gasket failure, and catastrophic engine damage. Catching the fault early means a £600–£1,200 repair; ignoring it could mean £9,000+ for a new engine. Frequently Asked Questions

Is coolant loss on an AJ126 always the crossover pipe?

It's the most common cause on this engine, but not the only one. A slowly leaking water pump or a faulty expansion tank cap can also cause coolant loss without visible drips. The key is to perform a pressure test to locate the leak - a specialist can usually identify the source within an hour.

At what mileage does an AJ126 typically start losing coolant?

Crossover pipe issues often appear between 70,000 and 100,000 miles. Water pump leaks are more common in the 60,000–90,000 mile range, particularly on cars that have not had regular coolant changes.

Can I drive with an AJ126 that's losing coolant?

If the coolant loss is slow and you're topping up regularly, you can drive it to a specialist - but avoid long journeys and check the coolant level every time you stop. If the loss is rapid or the temperature gauge starts to rise, stop immediately and call a specialist. Driving with low coolant can cause overheating and catastrophic engine damage.

How do I tell the difference between a crossover pipe and a water pump leak?

A crossover pipe leak usually results in coolant loss with no visible puddle, as the coolant pools in the supercharger valley and evaporates. A water pump leak often leaves a small puddle under the front of the engine, especially after the car has been parked. A coolant pressure test is the definitive way to find the leak.
