AJ-V8 Oil in Coolant / Mayonnaise Under Oil Cap

Finding oil in your coolant or a milky mayonnaise-like residue under the oil cap on your AJ-V8 is a serious red flag. This usually means coolant and oil are mixing internally — a problem that can quickly lead to engine failure if ignored.

Oil in coolant or a milky residue on the oil cap of your AJ-V8 is a clear sign of internal fluid contamination — most commonly caused by a failed oil cooler, a head gasket failure, or (rarely) a cracked cylinder head. This is an urgent issue that requires immediate diagnosis.

What This Engine Specifically Does

Where our general oil in coolant guide covers a broad range of causes across all Jaguar models, on the AJ-V8 the picture is more focused. This family of Jaguar V8s (4.0, 4.2, and 5.0 supercharged) is prone to two specific internal failures that allow oil and coolant to mix: the oil cooler, mounted in the valley of the V, which can rupture internally and allow oil to enter the coolant system (and vice versa), and the head gasket, which can fail between an oil gallery and a water jacket. The first symptom you'll notice is often a creamy, mayonnaise-like sludge under the oil filler cap or on the dipstick, or a film of oil floating in the coolant reservoir. This is not a case of condensation from short trips — it's a mechanical failure that needs immediate attention. Driving with contaminated oil will rapidly destroy the bearings and turbocharger (on supercharged models).

Why This Engine In Particular

The AJ-V8's design places the oil cooler in a vulnerable position — right in the V of the engine, where it's exposed to high heat cycling. The cooler's internal seals and core can degrade over time, especially if the cooling system has been neglected or incorrect coolant has been used. When the oil cooler fails, oil (at high pressure) can leak into the coolant circuit, or coolant can leak into the oil, creating that tell-tale milky emulsion. Additionally, the head gasket on this engine is known to fail in certain conditions, particularly if the engine has been overheated — which is a common consequence of coolant loss (another frequent AJ-V8 issue). The same symptom on a diesel 276DT would typically point to a different set of causes, but on the AJ-V8, the oil cooler is the prime suspect.

How to Confirm It's This, Not Something Else

Before assuming the worst, carry out these safe checks. First, check the oil dipstick — if the oil appears milky or has a chocolate-mousse consistency, that's a clear sign of coolant in the oil. Second, check the underside of the oil filler cap — a thick, creamy residue that doesn't wipe off easily is a strong indicator. Third, check the coolant reservoir — look for an oily film on top of the coolant. If you see any of these, do not drive the vehicle. A specialist will perform a cooling system pressure test, a combustion leak test (sniff test), and an oil analysis to confirm the source. In some cases, an internal inspection with a borescope may be needed. Ignoring this symptom can lead to complete engine seizure due to bearing damage from contaminated oil.

Verdict

Get it diagnosed immediately — do not drive. Oil in coolant or mayonnaise under the oil cap on an AJ-V8 indicates a serious internal failure. Driving with contaminated oil will destroy the engine bearings and turbocharger (on supercharged models) in a matter of miles. Early detection of an oil cooler failure can limit the repair to under £1,500; ignoring it can lead to a full engine replacement costing over £5,500. Frequently Asked Questions

Is oil in coolant on an AJ-V8 always a head gasket failure?

No. The most common cause is a failed oil cooler, which sits in the V of the engine. Head gasket failure is also possible, but the oil cooler is the prime suspect on this engine.

At what mileage does an AJ-V8 typically develop oil in coolant?

Most cases occur between 60,000 and 120,000 miles, particularly on vehicles that have had poor cooling system maintenance or have experienced overheating.

Can I drive an AJ-V8 with oil in coolant or mayonnaise under the oil cap?

No. Stop driving immediately. Contaminated oil will not lubricate properly and can destroy the engine within a few hundred miles.

How much does it cost to fix oil in coolant on an AJ-V8?

An oil cooler replacement is typically £800–£1,500. A head gasket repair is £1,500–£3,000. If the engine is damaged, expect £5,500–£9,000 for a reconditioned engine supply and fit.

What causes the milky mayonnaise under the oil cap on an AJ-V8?

That's an emulsion of oil and water (coolant). It's caused by internal mixing, most commonly from a failed oil cooler or head gasket. Short trips can also cause minor condensation, but a thick, persistent sludge is a sign of a serious failure.
