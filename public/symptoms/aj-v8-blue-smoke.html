AJ-V8 Blue Smoke From Exhaust

Blue smoke from your AJ-V8 exhaust is a clear sign of oil burning in the combustion chamber. Here's what causes it on this V8 family, how to diagnose the source, and what it costs to fix.

On the Jaguar AJ-V8, blue smoke from the exhaust is a classic sign of oil burning. The most common causes are worn valve stem seals (especially on higher-mileage engines) and worn piston rings. Addressing it early can save the engine from further damage.

What This Engine Specifically Does

Where our general blue smoke guide covers a broad range of causes across all Jaguar models, on the AJ-V8 the picture is more focused. This family of Jaguar V8s (4.0, 4.2, and 5.0 supercharged) is known for two specific failure modes that manifest as blue smoke: worn valve stem seals and worn piston rings. Valve stem seals can harden and crack with age and heat, allowing oil to seep down the valve stems and into the combustion chamber, especially on cold starts or after idling. Worn piston rings, on the other hand, allow oil from the sump to enter the combustion chamber under load, causing a continuous blue haze. Both produce a distinct blue-tinged smoke — different from the black smoke of over-fuelling or the white smoke of coolant ingress.

Why This Engine In Particular

The AJ-V8's design, while robust, places the valve stem seals in a high-heat environment that can cause them to harden over time, especially on pre-2013 engines. This is a well-documented issue on higher-mileage units. Worn piston rings are less common but can occur if the engine has been subjected to poor oil change intervals or has been overheated. The blue smoke typically appears in different scenarios: a puff of blue smoke on cold start that quickly clears is usually valve stem seals; a constant blue haze under acceleration is more likely piston rings. The same symptom on a diesel engine like the 276DT would point to a very different set of causes (often turbocharger oil seals), but on the AJ-V8, the valvetrain and bottom-end are the prime suspects.

How to Confirm It's This, Not Something Else

Before assuming the worst, rule out simpler possibilities. Check the oil level — overfilling can cause blue smoke. If the oil level is correct, pay attention to when the smoke appears. If it's a puff on cold start that disappears, valve stem seals are likely. If it's a continuous haze under acceleration or at idle, piston rings or cylinder bore wear may be the issue. Also check for oil leaks that could be dripping onto the exhaust — that creates white smoke, not blue. A specialist will perform a compression test and a leak-down test to check ring sealing, and may use a borescope to inspect the cylinder bores. They may also perform a wet compression test to confirm ring wear. Do not ignore blue smoke — it will only get worse and can lead to fouled spark plugs and catalytic converter damage.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is blue smoke from an AJ-V8 always a valve stem seal issue?

Not always. It can also be worn piston rings, a blocked PCV system, or in rare cases, a damaged turbocharger oil seal (on supercharged models). A specialist can identify the cause through a series of tests.

At what mileage does an AJ-V8 typically start producing blue smoke?

Valve stem seal failure often appears between 60,000 and 120,000 miles, particularly on pre-2013 engines. Piston ring wear is more common at higher mileages or on engines with poor maintenance history.

Can I drive an AJ-V8 with blue smoke?

Yes, but you should have it diagnosed quickly. Blue smoke indicates oil is being burned, which can foul spark plugs, damage catalytic converters, and lead to more serious engine damage if left unchecked.

How much does it cost to fix blue smoke on an AJ-V8?

Valve stem seal replacement is typically £1,500–£3,000. Piston ring repair is £3,000–£6,000. If the engine is severely damaged, expect £5,500–£9,000 for a reconditioned engine supply and fit.

What happens if I ignore blue smoke on an AJ-V8?

Continued oil burning will foul the spark plugs, damage the catalytic converters, and eventually lead to excessive oil consumption. If piston rings are the cause, the engine will lose compression and power, leading to a full rebuild or replacement.
