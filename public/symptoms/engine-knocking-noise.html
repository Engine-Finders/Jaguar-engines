A knocking noise that appears on cold start and fades as the engine warms is the classic early sign of bottom-end bearing wear — and on a Land Rover diesel, it should be diagnosed within days, not weeks.

  • On 306DT (TDV6/SDV6) engines, cold-start knock is the leading indicator of crankshaft bearing failure — the most-enquired fault in our data
  • A knock that gets louder under load is more urgent than one that only appears at idle
  • Not every knock is catastrophic — timing chain tensioner wear and injector noise both present similarly and cost far less
  • Typical cost range across all causes: £450 to £9,500 depending on which of the four causes below it turns out to be [EM-QUOTE]
  • Timing matters more than most owners assume — the same fault caught at week one and week twelve can carry a very different bill

What It Actually Sounds Like

Owners describe engine knock in a handful of consistent ways. Which one matches yours narrows the field considerably.

  • A deep, rhythmic knock from low down, in time with engine speed — bottom-end bearing wear
  • A light metallic rattle from the front or top, worst in the first few seconds — timing chain or tensioner
  • A sharp tapping that rises with revs — injector or valvetrain noise
  • A knock only under acceleration — see our separate guide to rattle under acceleration
  • A knock accompanied by blue exhaust smoke — check blue smoke causes alongside this page

Why This Ranking, and What the Data Shows

This isn't a generic list assembled from forum consensus. The ranking reflects the actual proportion of "engine knocking" enquiries in our 2025 dataset that resolved to each of these four causes once properly diagnosed. Crankshaft bearing wear sits at the top not because it's inherently more common across every Land Rover engine, but because the 306DT and 276DT — the two engines most associated with it — are also two of the most numerous diesels in the parc reaching typical failure mileage right now. That's an important distinction covered fully on our data methodology page: a high rank reflects volume and vulnerability together, not a claim that bearing failure is somehow "normal." Timing chain tensioner wear ranks second largely because the pre-2019 Ingenium diesel is now the single most-enquired engine on this entire site, and its own weak point happens to present as a very similar noise.

How to Narrow It Down Yourself

Safe checks only. None of these require the vehicle to be raised or panels removed — if a check needs a lift, we've said so rather than telling you to attempt it.

1

When does it happen?

Note whether it's only on cold start, only when warm, or constant. Cold-start-only that fades points strongly toward bearing wear.

2

Does it change with revs?

From the driver's seat, with the vehicle stationary and in park. A knock rising directly with engine speed is bottom-end; one that stays constant is likely ancillary.

3

Check your oil level and condition

Low or contaminated oil accelerates every cause on this page. See the correct spec on our 306DT oil capacity guide.

Anything beyond this needs a specialist. Locating a knock properly requires a stethoscope on specific points of the block and, in most cases, the vehicle on a lift — not something to attempt on a driveway.

When It's Serious vs When It Can Wait

🔴 Stop driving now

Knock accompanied by an oil pressure warning light, or a sudden loud knock with power loss. Continuing risks turning a repairable fault into a scrap engine.

🟠 Diagnose within days

Cold-start knock that's been getting gradually longer or louder over weeks. This is the window where the repair-vs-replace decision is still genuinely open.

🟢 Book it in, but don't panic

A light tap that hasn't changed in months, no warning lights, no power loss, oil level correct. Still worth diagnosing, but not an emergency.

What It Costs to Put Right

The spread here is wide because the causes are genuinely different jobs. Injector noise on a Discovery TD5 is a few hundred pounds; crankshaft bearing failure on a Range Rover Sport SDV6 is a full engine replacement. Frequently Asked Questions

Can I drive a Land Rover with a knocking engine?

Briefly and gently, if there is no oil pressure warning and no power loss — but you should be driving it to a specialist, not continuing normal use. If the knock is accompanied by an oil pressure light, stop immediately; continuing at that point is what turns a repairable engine into a scrap one.

Why does my Land Rover only knock when cold?

Cold-start knock that fades as the engine warms is the classic signature of bearing wear. Cold oil is thicker and takes a moment to reach the bearings; once warm, oil pressure masks the noise. It fading does not mean it is improving — it usually means it is early.

How much does it cost to diagnose an engine knock?

Most independent JLR specialists charge for an hour or two of diagnostic time, typically £90–£220 depending on region. Many will offset this against the job if you proceed with them.

Is a knocking noise always the crankshaft?

No. Timing chain tensioner wear, V8 chain rattle and injector noise all produce knocking or tapping sounds and cost substantially less to resolve than a crankshaft bearing issue.

Will an oil top-up fix a knocking noise?

If the knock is caused by genuinely low oil level, topping up can reduce the noise temporarily, but it does not undo any bearing wear that has already occurred and the underlying cause still needs diagnosing.
