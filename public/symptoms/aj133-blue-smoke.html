AJ133 Blue Smoke From Exhaust

Blue smoke on the 5.0‑litre V8 supercharged engine is a clear signal that oil is entering the combustion chamber — and on the AJ133, the most likely cause is worn valve stem seals, a known weakness on this engine. Here's exactly what it means, which mileage band to worry about, and what it costs to put right.

On the AJ133 specifically, blue smoke from the exhaust — especially on cold start or after idling — is the classic signature of worn valve stem seals, a well‑documented weakness on this 5.0 V8. A failing supercharger oil seal is the other common cause, though less frequent.

What This Engine Specifically Does

Where our general blue smoke guide covers a wide range of causes across the Jaguar range, on the AJ133 the picture narrows significantly. This 5.0 V8 supercharged produces blue smoke for one of two dominant reasons: worn valve stem seals (oil seeping past the valve guides into the combustion chamber) or a failing supercharger oil seal (oil being drawn through the supercharger into the intake). The tell‑tale difference is timing: valve seal smoke is usually a puff on cold start or after idling for several minutes; supercharger seal smoke is more likely under hard acceleration or immediately after lifting off from boost.

Why This Engine In Particular

The AJ133's valve stem seals are a known weak point, particularly on pre‑2013 engines that have seen higher mileage or less‑than‑ideal oil change intervals. The seals harden over time due to heat cycling, allowing oil to trickle down the valve stems overnight and burn off on the next cold start. The supercharger oil seal issue is less common but well‑documented, especially on higher‑mileage examples where oil coking or bearing wear compromises the seal. What makes this engine‑specific is the access cost: valve seal replacement on the AJ133 requires removing the supercharger and both cylinder heads — a substantial labour job — while supercharger work requires removing the supercharger unit itself. Both are significant interventions, which is why differentiating between them matters.

How to Confirm It's This, Not Something Else

Before assuming valve seals or supercharger seal, rule out the simpler possibilities. Overfilled engine oil can cause blue smoke, particularly on high‑revving supercharged engines — check the dipstick and drain if necessary. A blocked PCV system can also cause oil to be drawn into the intake, so inspect the PCV valve and breather hoses. If the smoke appears on cold start and clears within 30–60 seconds, that's classic valve stem seals. If the smoke appears under acceleration or after a period of boost, that's more likely the supercharger seal. If the smoke is constant and accompanied by a loss of power, that could be worn piston rings — though this is much rarer on the AJ133 than on older, higher‑mileage engines.

Verdict

Don't ignore blue smoke on an AJ133 — book a specialist this week, not next month. The distinction between valve seals and supercharger seals is critical because the repair routes are entirely different, and both are significant enough that you want an accurate diagnosis before committing. A specialist can usually tell the difference within minutes with a cold‑start test and a boost check. Frequently Asked Questions

Is blue smoke on an AJ133 always the valve stem seals?

It's the most common cause on this engine, but not the only one. Supercharger oil seal failure can produce blue smoke under acceleration, and a blocked PCV system can cause oil to be drawn into the intake. The key is when the smoke appears: valve seals = cold start; supercharger = under load; PCV = constant.

At what mileage does an AJ133 typically start smoking blue?

Valve stem seals typically start causing blue smoke between 60,000 and 90,000 miles, particularly on pre‑2013 examples. Supercharger oil seal issues often appear later, in the 80,000–120,000 mile band, though this is highly dependent on service history.

Can I drive with blue smoke from the exhaust?

Yes — but you shouldn't ignore it. Driving with failing valve stem seals or a leaking supercharger seal can lead to increased oil consumption, fouled spark plugs, and potentially catalytic converter damage. Book a diagnostic within the next week or two; it's not an immediate emergency unless the smoke is very heavy and accompanied by a loss of power.

How do I tell the difference between valve seals and supercharger seals?

Valve seal smoke appears on cold start and clears quickly; supercharger seal smoke appears under acceleration or immediately after lifting off from boost. A specialist can also perform a leak‑down test or a boost check to confirm the cause — it's usually a straightforward diagnosis once the car is on a ramp.
