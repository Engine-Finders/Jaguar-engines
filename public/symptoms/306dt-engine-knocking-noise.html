What This Engine Specifically Does

Where our general engine knocking noise guide covers four possible causes across the whole Land Rover range, on the 306DT the picture narrows considerably. This V6's dominant failure signature is a deep, rhythmic knock present only in the first few seconds after a genuinely cold start, fading as oil pressure builds and the bearing surfaces are properly lubricated. It is distinct from the lighter, higher-pitched rattle typical of Ingenium timing chain wear, and distinct from the sharper tap of injector noise - though all three can sound similar to an untrained ear over a phone recording, which is why in-person diagnosis matters.

Why This Engine In Particular

The 306DT's crankshaft main bearings use a tin-aluminium coating that is prone to fatigue over extended mileage - full mechanism detail is on the dedicated 306DT Crankshaft Bearing Failure page. What makes the cold-start pattern so reliable as a diagnostic signal on this specific engine is the bearing clearance increasing measurably once wear begins, which oil pressure at operating temperature is enough to mask but cold, thick oil is not. This is a design characteristic of this particular bearing arrangement rather than a universal diesel trait, which is why the same symptom means something quite different on, say, a 204DTD.

How to Confirm It's This, Not Something Else

Before assuming the worst, it's worth ruling out the cheaper alternatives specific to this engine. Genuinely low oil level can produce a similar transient knock on cold start and is free to check yourself - top up to the correct level with the right specification oil and listen again the next cold start. A failing glow plug relay can occasionally cause a knock-like rattle during the first few seconds of cranking on diesel engines generally, though this is less common on the 306DT specifically than on smaller four-cylinder units. If the noise persists after ruling out oil level, and particularly if it's present on multiple consecutive cold starts rather than a one-off, that's the point to book a proper diagnostic listening test rather than continuing to monitor it yourself.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is a knocking 306DT always the crankshaft bearing?

It's the most likely cause given how commonly this specific engine presents this way, but not the only one. Injector noise and, less commonly, timing chain wear can produce a similar sound on this V6, though the cold-start pattern of crankshaft bearing wear is fairly distinctive once you know what to listen for.

At what mileage does a 306DT typically start knocking?

Most cases in our data fall between 80,000 and 140,000 miles, though this is a risk band rather than a certainty - many 306DTs pass through this range without ever developing the fault, particularly with strong service history.
