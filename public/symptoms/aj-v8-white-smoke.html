AJ-V8 White Smoke From Exhaust

White smoke from your AJ-V8 exhaust is a serious warning sign — it almost always means coolant is entering the combustion chamber. Here's what causes it, how to confirm the source, and what it costs to fix.

On the Jaguar AJ-V8, white smoke from the exhaust is a clear indicator of coolant being burned in the engine. The most common causes are a failed head gasket, a cracked cylinder head, or a leaking EGR cooler (on supercharged models). It's a serious issue that requires immediate diagnosis.

What This Engine Specifically Does

Where our general white smoke guide covers a broad range of causes, on the AJ-V8 the picture narrows to coolant-related issues. This family of Jaguar V8s (4.0, 4.2, and 5.0 supercharged) is known for head gasket failures, particularly on engines that have been overheated or have high mileage. White smoke from the exhaust is a tell-tale sign that coolant is leaking into the combustion chamber, where it is turned to steam. This is distinct from the blue smoke of oil burning and the black smoke of over-fuelling. The white smoke is often accompanied by a sweet smell (antifreeze) and a loss of coolant with no visible external leak. If you see white smoke, stop driving immediately — continued operation can cause severe engine damage.

Why This Engine In Particular

The AJ-V8's design, while generally robust, has a known vulnerability in the head gasket area, especially on pre-2013 engines. The aluminium block and heads have different expansion rates, and over time, the head gasket can fail, allowing coolant to seep into the cylinders. This is often exacerbated by overheating, which can warp the cylinder head or block. Additionally, the supercharged variants (5.0 SC) have an EGR cooler that can develop internal leaks, allowing coolant to enter the intake tract and produce white smoke. The same symptom on a diesel 276DT would often point to a different issue (like a failed EGR cooler or injector), but on the AJ-V8, the head gasket and cooling system are the prime suspects.

How to Confirm It's This, Not Something Else

Before assuming the worst, rule out simpler possibilities. First, check the coolant level — if it's low and you have white smoke, that's a strong indicator of internal coolant loss. Second, smell the exhaust — a sweet antifreeze smell points to coolant burning. Third, check the oil dipstick for a milky emulsion (coolant in oil) — this indicates a head gasket failure that is mixing coolant with oil. Fourth, perform a combustion leak test (sniff test) on the coolant reservoir to detect exhaust gases — positive means a head gasket breach. A specialist will also perform a cylinder leak-down test and may use a borescope to inspect the cylinders. Do not drive an AJ-V8 with white smoke; it will only worsen the damage.

Verdict

Get it diagnosed immediately — do not drive. White smoke on an AJ-V8 is a critical warning of coolant entering the combustion chamber. Continuing to drive can cause severe engine damage, including a hydrolocked engine. Early detection of a head gasket failure can limit the repair to under £3,000; ignoring it can lead to a full engine replacement costing over £5,500. Frequently Asked Questions

Is white smoke from an AJ-V8 always a head gasket issue?

Not always, but it's the most common cause. A cracked cylinder head, a leaking EGR cooler (on supercharged models), or a failed intake manifold gasket can also cause white smoke. A specialist can identify the exact source.

At what mileage does an AJ-V8 typically develop white smoke?

Head gasket failures often occur between 60,000 and 120,000 miles, particularly on engines that have been overheated or have had coolant system neglect.

Can I drive an AJ-V8 with white smoke?

No. Stop driving immediately. White smoke indicates coolant is entering the combustion chamber, which can lead to hydro-lock and catastrophic engine failure.

How much does it cost to fix white smoke on an AJ-V8?

Head gasket replacement is typically £1,500–£3,000. If the cylinder head is cracked or warped, expect £3,000–£6,000. If the engine is severely damaged, you're looking at £5,500–£9,000 for a reconditioned engine supply and fit.

What causes white smoke on a supercharged AJ-V8?

In addition to head gasket failure, the EGR cooler on supercharged models can develop internal leaks, allowing coolant to enter the intake system and produce white smoke. This is a known issue on the 5.0 SC engine.
